# Conflicts of interest: what the law says, what the facts show

A conflict of interest is not, in itself, an offence or proof of wrongdoing: it is a situation the law tries to prevent. This page explains the rule and its limits, then gives two documented cases: the State's use of consulting firms and the Epstein case. We do not ourselves label a situation a "conflict of interest": only the HATVP or the courts do.

!!! warning "Presumption of innocence"

    The investigations and complaints cited are not convictions. We do not name anyone who has not been convicted. A mistake, something missing? [Report it]({{ repositories.docs.url }}/issues/new/choose).

## What the law says

- **The definition.** According to the Directorate-General for Local Authorities, the Act of 22 December 2025 creating a status for local elected officials redefined conflict of interest: Article 2 of the Act of 11 October 2013 now covers a situation of interference between a **public** interest and a **private** interest that may influence, or appear to influence, the independent, impartial and objective exercise of a function. The "public-public conflict" is removed.[^dgcl-prevention-conflits-interets-2025]
- **The offence.** Unlawful taking of interest (Article 432-12 of the Criminal Code) is punishable by five years in prison and a €500,000 fine. In the version in force since 24 December 2025, the text specifies that a public interest, or any interest whose consideration the law excludes, is not an "interest" within the meaning of the article, and that the offence is not made out when the person could not act otherwise in order to meet a compelling general-interest reason.[^legifrance-code-penal-432-12]
- **Oversight.** The {{ res("hatvp", "HATVP") }}, created in 2013, oversees the ethics and probity of public officials, lobbying and, more recently, foreign influence.[^lcp-hatvp-rapport-2025]

## What the HATVP checks, in figures (2025)

Figures from the HATVP's 2025 activity report:

- **Declarations.** 10,362 asset and interest declarations received, of which 5,795 checked; 936 reminders, 7 injunctions to file a declaration and 57 cases sent to the courts for failure to file.[^hatvp-rapport-activite-2025]
- **Risk of conflict of interest.** Of 3,089 interest declarations checked, the HATVP recommended preventive measures, such as recusal, to 38.6% of the public officials checked. Fewer than half of the interest declarations checked fully meet the requirements of completeness, accuracy and sincerity.[^hatvp-rapport-activite-2025]
- **Parliamentarians.** Under the separation of powers, the HATVP cannot directly recommend preventive measures to them: when it identifies a manifest risk, it informs the bureau of the assembly concerned.[^hatvp-rapport-activite-2025]
- **Moves to the private sector ("revolving doors").** 641 opinions issued, 3.0% of them finding incompatibility; of the 391 opinions on planned moves to the private sector, 4.9% found incompatibility and 78.6% compatibility with reservations.[^hatvp-rapport-activite-2025]

## The limits, according to its president and the Court of Audit

In his 28 May 2026 review, the HATVP's president proposes in particular a power of injunction with periodic penalties and then a financial sanction in case of failure to file: according to him, the High Authority can today only report to the courts, beyond the reminders and injunctions to file that it already sends. He also notes that parliamentarians' asset declarations, which can be consulted only at the prefecture, are consulted about twenty times a year on average.[^lcp-hatvp-rapport-2025]

On revolving doors, a May 2025 Court of Audit report cited by LCP finds that follow-up of the reservations is insufficient and that breaches of them are partly left unpunished.[^lcp-hatvp-rapport-2025] We have not read that report at source.

## Case 1: consulting firms and McKinsey

{{ h.timeline('en', only=['aucun'], also=['2022-senat-rapport-sur-les-cabinets-de-conseil', '2022-pnf-informations-judiciaires-sur-les-cabinets-de-conseil-et-les-campagnes', '2024-senat-cabinets-de-conseil-proposition-de-loi-deuxieme-lecture', '2024-cash-investigation-mckinsey-une-firme-au-coeur-du-pouvoir', '2026-pnf-mckinsey-saisie-de-65-millions-en-belgique']) }}

The link with conflicts of interest rests on two questions: whether help given during a campaign was exchanged for public contracts afterwards, the subject of one of the two judicial investigations, and the ethics of consultants, the subject of the bill. The tax strand, which is separate, targets McKinsey as a company: it has not led to a ruling either, but a seizure of about €65 million was announced in September 2026. Independent investigations (the Senate report, "Cash Investigation") shed light on these facts without ruling on them: only the courts can say whether an offence was committed. No court decision has settled these questions in the sources read.

## Case 2: the Epstein case

The Paris prosecutor's office opened a preliminary investigation on 23 August 2019 into Jeffrey Epstein's activities.[^euronews-epstein-enquete-2019] On 14 February 2026, after millions of documents were released in the United States, it assigned lead magistrates to examine possible offences linked to French people, and the national financial prosecutor opened a preliminary investigation for laundering of aggravated tax fraud targeting a former minister, who has not been convicted.[^france24-epstein-parquet-2026] A proposal for a parliamentary inquiry committee was tabled on 5 February 2026.[^an-dossier-epstein-2026]

!!! note "A relationship is not, in itself, a conflict of interest"

    Under the law, there must be interference between the exercise of a function and a private interest. Having known or associated with a convicted person does not meet that definition on its own: it is for the investigations to say whether offences, financial or otherwise, occurred, and for the transparency authorities to assess any situations of interest. We therefore repeat no name and no unsettled accusation.
