# Filing a complaint, voluntary interview and trial

!!! abstract "Key points"

    - Violence: **medical certificate with ITT**, photos, witnesses, videos.
    - Complaint: any police station must take it, or send it by **registered letter to the prosecutor**.
    - The Défenseur des droits and the IGPN or IGGN **complement** a complaint, they don't replace it.
    - **Voluntary interview**: never go without first consulting a lawyer.
    - **Fast-track trial**: you can refuse to be tried the same day; relatives gather the supporting documents quickly.

!!! warning "Disclaimer"

    General information about French law, not legal advice. Contact a lawyer or a legal team before any police interview or court hearing.

!!! tip "Where to start?"

    - After police violence: [the flowchart](#flowchart-after-police-violence), then [document injuries and file a complaint](#after-violence-document-injuries-and-file-a-complaint).
    - You are summoned: [voluntary interview](#summoned-for-a-voluntary-interview-audition-libre).
    - Trial or fast-track court: [documents to gather](#fast-track-trial-and-court-documents-to-gather-urgently).

## Flowchart: after police violence

<div class="flow" markdown>

1. **Care** → see a doctor as soon as possible and ask for a **descriptive certificate with ITT**.[^ldh-violences]
2. **Evidence** → photos of the injuries with a ruler, unwashed clothes, witnesses' contact details, copies of videos off the phone.[^witness-preuve]
3. **Filing a complaint: where?**
    - **Police or gendarmerie station** → they are **obliged** to take your complaint, wherever the events happened.[^sp-plainte]
    - **Afraid of a refusal or pressure** → registered letter with acknowledgement of receipt to the **public prosecutor**.[^sp-plainte]
4. **On top of the complaint** (not instead of it) → Défenseur des droits[^ddd] and IGPN or IGGN.[^iggn]
5. **Get support** → legal team, lawyer, and the LDH guide.

</div>

## After violence: document injuries and file a complaint

1. **Detailed medical certificate**: see a doctor (GP, hospital A&E or forensic medical unit) as soon as possible. Ask for a detailed description of every injury (dimensions, typical pattern), its psychological impact and the number of days of **ITT** (total incapacity for work, even if you are not employed).[^ldh-violences] According to the LDH, certificates from GPs or A&E carry little weight in the procedure: to have your injuries recorded in a **forensic medical unit (UMJ)**, you need a **requisition**, which you get by filing a complaint.[^ldh-fiche-manif-2026]
2. **Physical evidence**: photograph your injuries with a ruler for scale, photograph and keep **cooled munition debris** (LBD rubber bullets, GENL pellets, red GM2L or black GL-304 fragments, batch markings — [see "Police weapons: effects, injuries and debris"](armes-police.md)), keep stained or torn clothes (unwashed), and gather witness contact details and videos.[^ldh-violences][^witness-preuve][^desarmons-armements-2020]
3. **File a criminal complaint (plainte)**:
    - Every police or gendarmerie station is **legally required** to take your complaint, regardless of where the events took place (refusing to take a complaint is unlawful).[^sp-plainte]
    - **To avoid being turned away or pressured at the desk**: send your complaint directly by **registered letter with acknowledgement of receipt (LRAR)** to the **public prosecutor (procureur de la République)** at the court of the place where the events occurred (dated and signed account, copy of the medical certificate and evidence).[^sp-plainte][^ldh-violences]
    - The LDH advises filing the complaint with the **IGPN** (in Paris) or the **IGGN** (if a gendarme is involved), or at a police station outside Paris, to obtain the UMJ requisition and the seizure of CCTV recordings: mind the **retention period of the images**. State the circumstances, your injuries and the number of days of ITT, attach the forensic medical certificate and, with a lawyer's help, the legal basis.[^ldh-fiche-manif-2026]
    - **Investigating judge**: for a crime, you can refer the matter directly; for a lesser offence (délit), a complaint with civil-party application is admissible only if the prosecutor has said they will not prosecute, or **3 months** after your complaint filed against a receipt or by registered letter. Go through a lawyer.[^cpp-85][^ldh-fiche-manif-2026]
4. **Additional reports** (do **not** replace a criminal complaint):
    - Refer the case to the {{ res("defenseur-des-droits") }} (free, independent investigation into law-enforcement ethics).[^ddd]
    - Report the facts to the IGPN (police) or {{ res("iggn-signalement", "IGGN") }} (gendarmerie).[^iggn] According to the LDH, a report on the IGPN platform does not count as a complaint.[^ldh-fiche-manif-2026]
5. Guides and support: {{ res("ldh-que-faire-violences", "LDH — What to do about police violence? (in French)") }} and {{ res("flagrant-deni") }} (legal guide and victim support).

## Summoned for a "voluntary interview" (audition libre)

If the police or gendarmerie summon you after a protest to be questioned "freely" (outside custody):[^sp-audition-libre]

- The written summons must state the alleged offence, your **right to be assisted by a lawyer** (free under legal aid depending on your income) and your **right to leave the premises at any time**.[^sp-audition-libre]
- **Never attend a voluntary interview without consulting a lawyer or legal team beforehand**: you can ask to reschedule the appointment so you have time to prepare your defence.[^sp-audition-libre][^sm-guide]
- During the interview, you have the **right to remain silent** and to re-read the record before deciding whether to sign it.[^sp-audition-libre]

## Fast-track trial and court: documents to gather urgently

If someone close to you is brought before the court at the end of custody for a {{ res("sp-comparution-immediate", "fast-track trial (comparution immédiate)") }}, the court rules the same day (or decides on pre-trial detention if the trial is adjourned).[^sp-ci]

Relatives must gather **immediately** and hand to the duty lawyer the **guarantees of representation (garanties de représentation)** (material proof that the person is not a flight risk and will attend hearings). They weigh in **avoiding pre-trial detention** or obtaining release: the court can order pre-trial detention, notably to keep the person available to the justice system.[^cpp-397-3][^cpp-144] The list below comes from lawyers' practice, not from a legal text:

- [ ] **Identity**: copy of ID card, passport or residence permit.
- [ ] **Address**: lease, latest rent receipt or utility bill (electricity, phone); if hosted by someone else: **signed and dated hosting certificate (attestation d'hébergement)** + copy of the host's ID + host's proof of address.
- [ ] **Work or studies**: employment contract, last 3 payslips, job offer, school enrolment certificate or student card.
- [ ] **Family and health**: family record book (livret de famille), current medical prescriptions, proof of medical or psychological care.
- [ ] **If needed**: CAF, RSA or France Travail (formerly Pôle emploi) certificate, and proof of **taking part in associations or cultural activities**.

You can also **hand them in advance to a trusted contact**, who will pass them to the lawyer ([Before you leave](avant-de-partir.md)).

Before the court, the defendant always has the **right to refuse to be tried that same day** and to request an adjournment: the case is then sent to a hearing held **4 to 10 weeks** later (the defendant may waive the 4-week minimum), to prepare their defence with their lawyer.[^sp-ci][^cpp-397-1]

## After the case: getting files erased

If the case is **dropped**, you are **acquitted** or get a **dismissal (non-lieu)**, your data may remain in the **TAJ** (criminal records processing file) and your DNA profile in the **FNAEG**. You can ask the prosecutor to **erase** it or to add a **mention** to it.[^cpp-230-8][^cpp-706-54-1] The steps and appeals are in [DNA, fingerprints and photos](adn-signalisation.md#asking-for-erasure).
