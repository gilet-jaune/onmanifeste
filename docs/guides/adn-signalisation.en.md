# DNA, fingerprints and photos in police custody

!!! abstract "Key points"

    - In custody or questioning, police can take your **photos** and **fingerprints** (called **signalisation**) and a **DNA sample** (a mouth swab) if they have **plausible reasons to suspect you** of an offence.[^cpp-55-1]
    - The decision must be **written and reasoned**.[^cpp-55-1][^cpp-fnaeg]
    - **Refusing is an offence**: 1 year in prison and €15,000.[^cpp-55-1][^cpp-706-56] **Talk to your lawyer before deciding.**
    - Your DNA is only **kept** in the national file (FNAEG) in certain cases, and **you can ask the prosecutor to erase it**.[^cpp-fnaeg][^cpp-706-54-1]
    - For a protest, the list of offences covered includes **violence**, **damage** and **taking part in a demonstration while armed**, but **not** insulting or resisting arrest.[^cpp-706-55]

This guide complements [Police custody](garde-a-vue.md). It explains **what the law says** so you do not have to improvise. It does not tell you to refuse or accept: that is a decision to take with a lawyer.

!!! warning "Not legal advice"

    These articles will be recodified by order no. 2025-1091 of 19 November 2025 (from 2029) and were amended in 2026. Check the version in force and, if in doubt, ask a lawyer.

## Signalisation and sampling: what the law allows

- **Flagrante delicto** (article 55-1) and **preliminary investigation** (article 76-2, with the prosecutor's authorisation): the judicial police officer can take **external samples** (mouth swab) and **identification records** (fingerprints or palm prints, photographs) from anyone against whom there are **one or more plausible reasons to suspect** that they committed or attempted the offence.[^cpp-55-1][^cpp-76-2]
- The decision is **written and reasoned**.[^cpp-55-1]
- **Photo and fingerprints** can feed police files: the photo, the **TAJ** (criminal records processing file), the fingerprints, the fingerprint file (FAED).[^sp-gav]
- **Without your consent**, force is only possible for **identity signalisation**: if it is the only way to establish who you are, you refuse to prove your identity or give a manifestly inaccurate one, the offence carries at least 3 years, and the prosecutor authorises it in writing. Your lawyer is notified.[^cpp-55-1]

## The DNA file (FNAEG)

| Situation | What happens to the DNA |
| --- | --- |
| **Plausible reasons to suspect** | The DNA can be **compared** with the file, without being kept.[^cpp-fnaeg] |
| **Serious or consistent evidence** that you committed an offence on the list | Your profile can be **kept** in the file, on the decision of the judicial police officer.[^cpp-fnaeg] |
| **Conviction** for an offence on the list | The profile is kept.[^cpp-fnaeg] |

The list in article 706-55 includes in particular: **intentional violence** (222-1 to 222-18, including violence against a person holding public authority), **destruction and damage** (322-1 to 322-14) and **taking part in a demonstration while carrying a weapon** (431-10). It **does not include** insulting or resisting arrest (433-6 and 433-7), unless the facts also amount to an offence on the list.[^cpp-706-55] ([What the law forbids at a protest](risques-penaux.md).)

## Refusing: an offence in its own right

- Refusing the sample or signalisation is punished by **1 year in prison and a €15,000 fine**.[^cpp-55-1][^cpp-706-56]
- Refusing the fingerprints or photos authorised by the prosecutor during an **identity verification** is punished by 3 months and €3,750.[^cpp-78-5]
- The Constitutional Council held that this penalty is "not manifestly disproportionate" and recalled that sampling implies **neither a declaration nor a presumption of guilt**.[^cc-2010-25]

That is why the question "accept or refuse?" is not settled alone in a police station: a refusal can add a prosecution to the one that led to custody.

## The TAJ: refusing the photo does not prevent registration

The **TAJ** (_traitement d'antécédents judiciaires_, criminal records processing file) is a file shared by the police and the gendarmerie, which replaces the former STIC and JUDEX. It holds the data of people under investigation **and of victims**. It is separate from the criminal record: you can be in it without any conviction.[^cnil-taj] It is also used for administrative inquiries, for example for access to certain jobs.[^cnil-taj-droits]

- The TAJ is fed by data collected **during investigations** (preliminary, flagrante delicto, letters rogatory) for **any crime or offence**, and for some fifth-class contraventions.[^cpp-230-6] The article does not tie registration to the photo: the procedure data is enough, so refusing to be photographed does not guarantee you will not be registered.
- The prosecutor oversees the file. At your request, they can order **erasure**, **rectification** or a **note**, and must answer within **2 months**.[^cpp-230-8] After a **final acquittal**, the data is **erased**, unless the prosecutor orders it kept; after a **dismissal or a case closed without action**, it carries a **note** (so it can no longer be consulted for administrative inquiries), unless erasure is ordered.[^cpp-230-8] A refusal can be challenged before the president of the investigating chamber.[^cpp-230-8]

- **What the Constitutional Council said (27 October 2017).** The file lists **everyone investigated** for a crime, an offence or certain fifth-class contraventions. It can hold a **photograph suitable for facial recognition**, and its data could then be kept **from five to forty years**, and also be consulted for administrative inquiries. The Council struck down the rule that denied any erasure to people investigated other than those acquitted, or benefiting from a dismissal or a case closed without action, as a **disproportionate infringement of privacy**.[^cc-2017-670] Current retention periods are in the section below.

- **What the European Court of Human Rights said (18 September 2014).** In _Brunet v. France_, about the STIC file (the TAJ's predecessor), it unanimously held that France violated the right to respect for private life (Article 8). The applicant, whose case had been closed without action after a criminal mediation, had **no real possibility of asking for erasure** of his data. A twenty-year retention period was, in that case, "assimilable" to indefinite retention.[^cedh-brunet]

### Refusing on principle

Some activists refuse signalisation and DNA **so as not to contribute to registration**, even at the price of a prosecution. It is a deliberate choice, to be made knowingly:

- **The cost is certain**: refusal is an offence (1 year and €15,000), added to the alleged facts.[^cpp-55-1][^cpp-706-56]
- **The gain is limited**: the TAJ is fed by the procedure, not by the photo alone (see above). You only refuse the photo and fingerprints.
- **Your situation matters**: a record, being a minor, a foreign or precarious person do not carry the same risks.

Decide **with your lawyer**, not in the cell.

## Checking what the TAJ holds after police custody

After police custody you may appear in the TAJ as a person investigated, even without prosecution. The CNIL describes the steps; ideally take them with a lawyer.[^cnil-taj-droits]

1. **Ask for access (do this first).** Write to the Ministry of the Interior (place Beauvau, 75008 Paris) stating that the request concerns the TAJ file, with a double-sided copy of your ID. Registered post is not mandatory but preferable. You will learn whether the cases in which you are investigated appear, for how long and for what purpose. The ministry has **2 months** to answer; access may be restricted so as not to hinder a procedure, and no answer after 2 months counts as a restriction. In that case, refer the matter to the CNIL (indirect access) with your ID, your request and any reply.[^cnil-taj-droits]
2. **Ask for rectification if data is inaccurate.** If the facts have been **reclassified in law**, rectification cannot be refused. Send the request by registered post to the **public prosecutor** where the events took place (or, if several departments are involved, to the **TAJ lead magistrate** at the Paris prosecutor general's office), or to the Ministry of the Interior; this magistrate may act on their own initiative or on request, has the same powers as the prosecutor, and rules within 2 months.[^cpp-230-9] Attach favourable decisions (acquittal, dismissal, case closed).[^cnil-taj-droits]
3. **Ask for erasure** if you benefited from an acquittal, a dismissal, a case closed without action, or a conviction with exemption from penalty or from entry in the criminal record. After an acquittal, the prosecutor may refuse and keep the data with a **mention** of the decision.[^cnil-taj-droits]
4. **No answer from the prosecutor, or a refusal**: you have **1 month** to appeal to the president of the investigating chamber (the Paris court of appeal's if you wrote to the lead magistrate).[^cnil-taj-droits]

**Retention periods** (general rule): 20 years for adults, with exceptions from 5 years (certain offences and minor offences) to 40 years (certain crimes and offences); 5 years for minors, up to 10 or 20 years for certain offences; 15 years for victims. If you are registered for old offences and commit a new one, the new offence's period applies to all.[^cnil-taj-droits]

**Why check**: the TAJ is consulted for administrative inquiries, notably for private-security professional cards, certain public jobs or jobs linked to security and defence, SNCF, RATP, customs, or naturalisation. Favourable outcomes (case closed, acquittal, exemption from entry) are then not viewable.[^cnil-taj-droits] A TAJ entry is not a criminal record.

## Asking for erasure

- If your profile was kept in the file although you were **not convicted** (case dropped, acquittal, dismissal), ask the **public prosecutor** to **erase** it. They do so on their own initiative or at your request if keeping it "no longer appears necessary".[^cpp-706-54-1]
- The prosecutor informs you of the outcome. If it is not erased, you can go to the **president of the investigating chamber**.[^cpp-706-54-1]
- Retention periods are set by decree: they are not detailed here. Ask your lawyer.

## What to do in practice

1. **Ask for your lawyer** before any sampling ([Police custody](garde-a-vue.md)).
2. **Ask for the written, reasoned decision** ordering it.
3. **Note** what is taken (photos, fingerprints, DNA) and the time.
4. **After the case**, if it is dropped or you are acquitted, send an **erasure request** by registered letter to the prosecutor.
