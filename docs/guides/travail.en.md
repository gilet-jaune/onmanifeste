# Strikes, protests and work

!!! abstract "Key points"

    - Private sector: a strike is a **collective stoppage**, no notice needed; no sanction except for serious misconduct.
    - Civil service: **union notice of 5 full days**; primary school teachers: declaration **48 hours beforehand**.
    - Public employees: the **duty of discretion** is about how you express yourself, not about the right to protest.
    - Pay is docked **in proportion** to the stoppage (for example 1/30 per day in the civil service).
    - Protesting **outside working hours** is your private life; otherwise, take leave or join a strike.
    - A job that blocks nothing: **numbers** count, a few hours can be enough, and you can **support** those whose stoppage weighs more.

Striking is a right; so is protesting. But time off work is handled differently depending on the case and your sector.

## Private sector

- A strike is a **collective, concerted stoppage** of work in support of **work-related demands** (pay, pensions, working conditions…).[^sp-greve-prive]
- **No notice** is required, neither to your employer nor by a union (except public services run by private companies).[^sp-greve-prive]
- You can be **neither sanctioned nor dismissed** for striking, except for gross misconduct (for example active participation in illegal acts).[^sp-greve-prive] A dismissal pronounced without gross misconduct is **void by operation of law**.[^ct-l2511-1]
- The employer deducts pay **in proportion** to the time stopped.[^sp-greve-prive]

## Public service

### Going on strike

- One or more unions must file a **notice** at least **5 full days** before the strike, stating its reasons, place, start time and length.[^sp-greve-public] It must come from a **representative** union, and the parties must **negotiate** during the notice period.[^ct-l2512-2]
- **Who is covered**: staff of the State, regions, départements and municipalities of **more than 10,000 inhabitants**, and staff of bodies running a public service, public or private.[^ct-l2512-1]
- **Rotating stoppages are banned**: the start and end times cannot differ between categories or staff members, and staggered or concertedly rotating stoppages across sectors or services are prohibited.[^ct-l2512-3]
- **Nursery and primary school teachers**: declare your intention to strike **at least 48 hours beforehand**, including one working day. Breaking these rules exposes you to disciplinary sanctions.[^sp-greve-public]
- Employees alone hold the right to strike: in public services, they **are not required to stop work for the whole period** stated in the notice.[^cass-2015-preavis-duree]
- **Pay deduction**: in the State civil service, 1/30 of pay per strike day, even for part of a day, bonuses included; in the local and hospital civil services, it is proportional (1/30 per day, 1/60 per half-day, 1/151.67 per hour).[^sp-greve-public]
- **No right to strike**: active police officers (including CRS), prison staff, judges, the military and Interior Ministry communications staff. In the local and hospital civil services, all employees can strike.[^sp-greve-public]

### Duty of discretion and neutrality

- Like everyone, you have the right to protest.[^cc-94-352] The **duty of discretion** (devoir de réserve) is not about your opinions but about **how** you express them, on duty and off duty.[^sp-reserve]
- It is assessed **case by case**: your rank, the circumstances, the visibility (social media or private conversation) and the words used (insulting or not).[^sp-reserve]
- **Union representatives** have more freedom within their mandate.[^sp-reserve]
- **On duty**, you must remain **neutral**: no sign of your political convictions in front of the public or colleagues.[^sp-reserve]
- [ ] In practice: no uniform, work badge or mention of your job at protests or in your posts; measured words; a separate account if your position is exposed ([social media](reseaux-sociaux.md)).

### If you are prosecuted

- A conviction recorded in **bulletin no. 2 of your criminal record** can, if deemed incompatible with your duties, lead to a disciplinary sanction, up to dismissal. It is **not automatic**: the administration must go through disciplinary proceedings, under a judge's review.[^acteurspublics-b2-revocation]
- From the moment you are taken into custody, inform your **lawyer** and your **union** ([police custody](garde-a-vue.md)).

## Protesting without striking

- Protesting **outside working hours** is your private life.
- Leaving during working hours **without being on strike** (or on leave) is an unauthorised absence: take leave or join a strike.

## If your absence blocks nothing

An office or administrative job rarely blocks a production chain on its own. That is no reason to stay on the sidelines. The pointers below are organising common sense, not established rules:

- [ ] **Numbers count**: a strike is also measured by its turnout, which is counted and seen. You count even if your job is not critical.
- [ ] **A few hours can be enough**: the pay deduction is proportional to the length of the stoppage.[^sp-greve-prive] Half a day costs less than a full day.
- [ ] **Protest outside working hours, or on leave**: see above.
- [ ] **Support those whose stoppage really blocks**: see [financially supporting strikers](travail.md#financially-supporting-strikers).
- [ ] **Join a union or a workplace group**: decide the form of action with your colleagues instead of guessing it alone.

### Slowdowns and work-to-rule

- To be protected from any sanction for striking, the movement must be a **total stoppage of work, collective, with work-related demands known to the employer**. Otherwise it can be ruled unlawful: no more protection, and sanction or dismissal become possible.[^sp-greve-prive]
- Slowing your work down (a "grève perlée") or following the rules to the letter without stopping (a "grève du zèle") is not a total stoppage: **these forms are risky**. Talk to a union first.
- In a transport dispute (a public service), the Versailles court of appeal held that one notice can cover short work stoppages spread over several days, and that a strike only becomes abusive in case of serious disorganisation, which the employer must prove. The Cour de cassation rejected the appeal, without publishing the ruling in its bulletin.[^cass-2017-greve-desorganisation] This concerns public services: do not assume it applies to your company.

## Financially supporting strikers

If you earn more than most strikers, you can help them hold out. From the most pooled to the most direct:

1. **A solidarity fund**: {{ res("caisse-solidarite") }} is a French non-profit (loi 1901) that helps workers in struggle. Donations come with no counterpart, the accounts are public and a map shows the local funds.
2. **Your union's strike fund**: ask the local or departmental union of the sector concerned what exists.
3. **Union dues**: if you are a member, they give a 66% tax credit, capped at 1% of your gross taxable income (salaries, pensions…). It cannot be combined with actual expenses.[^impots-cotisation-syndicale] The credit covers **dues**: ask the union whether a payment to its strike fund is treated as such, with a receipt.
4. **Giving to a person**: gifts of money are subject to gift tax whatever the amount, with some exceptions. For a gift from a friend there is no allowance, and the recipient must declare it within a month.[^sp-don-argent] That is a reason to prefer a fund over a personal transfer.
5. **An online fundraiser**: check who runs it (a union or an identifiable non-profit), who the money is paid out to and whether the accounts are public.

A payment by card, transfer or cheque leaves a trace in your name. If that matters to you, look at each fund's payment methods before giving.
