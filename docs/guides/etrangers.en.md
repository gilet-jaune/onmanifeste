# Protesting without French nationality

!!! abstract "Key points"

    - Peaceful protest is a **right for everyone**.
    - An arrest does **not automatically** lead to losing your permit or to an OQTF (removal order).
    - Carry a **copy** of your permit and the number of **La Cimade** or an immigration lawyer.
    - In custody: same rights, **interpreter**, consulate; appeals against an OQTF have **very short deadlines**.

Peaceful protest is a right for everyone, whatever their nationality.[^amnesty-droits] But an arrest can have extra consequences for your residence status: know them before you go.

## Risks to know

- Peaceful participation alone does not cost you a residence permit. However, facts considered a **threat to public order** can be taken into account by the prefecture at renewal or naturalisation.[^avocat-etrangers]
- The notion of **threat to public order** is vague and undefined; according to La Cimade, the alleged facts need not have led to a criminal conviction. It cites the case of a man arrested at a climate march with a scarf over his face against the gas, whom police considered a threat to public order and who was released at the end of custody; he was protected from expulsion as the spouse of a French citizen.[^cimade-menace-ordre-public]
- **Online too**: the Conseil d'État ruled on 2 October 2026 that withdrawing a residence card was justified for a person who had posted a message calling for a police officer's death and posted a photo again revealing the officer's identity and town, even though the message was removed very quickly and the behaviour was isolated.[^ce-2026-508967-message-en-ligne] These are extreme facts, but quickly deleting a message does not remove the threat: do not post calls for violence or officers' personal details (see [Filming law enforcement](filmer.md)). We read this decision only on an unofficial site.
- A removal order (**OQTF**) requires a separate administrative decision: an arrest does not trigger it automatically.[^avocat-etrangers]
- Without a valid permit, you can be **held to check your right of residence**. Only a **judicial police officer** can decide it, the prosecutor is informed from the start, it lasts **24 hours at most** (time spent on any 4-hour identity check is deducted) and takes place in a police or gendarmerie station. Your belongings can be searched and, if you provide nothing, your fingerprints and photo can be taken.[^sp-retenue]
- **Your rights during this hold**: to be told the reasons and the duration in a language you understand, an **interpreter**, a **lawyer** (chosen or court-appointed, present within the hour), a **doctor**, to tell your family and, if you care for minors, arrange for their care, to alert your **consulate** and to have the prosecutor informed. The record must state the start and end times; you may refuse to sign it.[^sp-retenue]
- **At the end of the hold**, the officer may release you (lawful residence or application under review), decide on detention or house arrest if a removal measure is taken, or place you in police custody, notably for remaining irregularly despite a final removal decision.[^sp-retenue]

## Before the protest

- [ ] Carry a **copy** of your residence permit or receipt (keep the original safe if you fear losing it).
- [ ] Write down the number of a **legal team** and of {{ res("cimade") }} or an immigration lawyer.
- [ ] If your situation is fragile (no permit, ongoing procedure), choose a **less exposed** part of the march and leave before dispersal ([dispersal and warnings](dispersion.md)).

## If you are arrested

- You have the **same rights** as everyone in custody: lawyer, doctor, interpreter, silence.[^sp-gav]
- You can ask for your country's **consular authorities** to be notified.[^sp-gav]
- Don't sign any document you don't understand; ask for the **interpreter**.
- If you receive a prefecture decision (refusal of residence, OQTF), **appeal deadlines are very short**: contact a lawyer, La Cimade or GISTI immediately.[^avocat-etrangers][^gisti]
