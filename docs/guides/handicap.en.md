# Protesting with a disability or chronic condition

!!! abstract "Key points"

    - Emergency for deaf people or those who cannot speak: **114** by text, chat or video.
    - Medication for **48 hours**, prescription and a **medical sheet** on paper.
    - A **dedicated buddy** and a scouted route (accessible section of the march, side streets).
    - In custody: ask for **a doctor** immediately; free **sign-language** interpreter if needed.

!!! warning "Accessible emergency number (114)"

    Deaf, hard-of-hearing, aphasic or non-speaking people: contact **114** by **SMS, chat or video** (free, 24/7 in France).[^urgence-114]

## Before you go: preparing your autonomy and care

- [ ] **Medication and prescriptions**: bring **48 hours' worth** of your medication (asthma, diabetes, epilepsy, heart or pain treatment) in its original packaging with a **paper copy of your prescription**.[^sm-guide][^eff-protest]
- [ ] **Paper medical note** in your pocket: medication, schedule, allergies, emergency contact and your regular doctor's details.[^eff-protest]
- [ ] **Disability card (Carte mobilité inclusion — CMI)** (copy or original) if you hold one.
- [ ] **Dedicated buddy**: never go alone; agree with one or two people to stay at your pace and accompany you all the way home.[^reporterre-handicap]
- [ ] **Route check**: check with organising unions or groups whether there is an **accessible contingent** (support vehicle, adapted pace, step-free route) and spot accessible stations and step-free side streets in advance.[^reporterre-handicap]

## During the protest: tear gas, noise and crowd movements

- **Asthma, respiratory or heart conditions**: tear gas (CS) can trigger severe bronchospasm. Keep your inhaler (bronchodilator) within immediate reach, wear a filtering mask and stay at the back or on well-ventilated sides of the march.[^allodocteurs-proteger][^medic-gestes]
- **Sensory hypersensitivity, autism, tinnitus**: stun grenade blasts and sirens often exceed pain thresholds; bring **earplugs or noise-cancelling headphones** and tinted glasses, and agree on a simple hand signal with your buddy to leave the crowd without needing to speak.[^reporterre-handicap]
- **Reduced mobility or wheelchair**: stay near clear perpendicular streets (without barriers or high kerbs) so you can leave the march at the first sign of tension or kettling.[^reporterre-handicap]

## If arrested or taken into custody

French law provides specific safeguards for your health and accessibility in police custody (garde à vue):[^sp-gav][^sm-guide]

1. **Medical examination and continuity of treatment**: ask **immediately for a doctor** as soon as your rights are notified. Hand them your prescription: the doctor rules on **whether your health is compatible with remaining in custody** and prescribes the administration of your essential medication throughout detention.[^sp-gav][^sm-guide]
2. **Deafness or speech difficulties**: you are entitled free of charge to a **sign-language interpreter (LSF)** or any technical aid allowing you to communicate with investigators and your lawyer from the start of custody.[^sp-gav]
3. **Adults under guardianship or curatorship (tutelle / curatelle)**: the senior police officer (OPJ) is legally required to **notify your guardian or curator**, who can appoint a lawyer or request a medical examination on your behalf.[^sp-gav]
