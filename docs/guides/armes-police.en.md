# Police weapons: effects, injuries and debris

!!! abstract "Key points"

    - Identifying the weapon by its **effect**, **wound pattern** and **debris** helps **adapt medical care** and **build evidence** (medical certificate with ITT, complaint).
    - **Explosive grenades (GM2L, GL-304 "Condor", 40 mm EF-SON / ASSD stun cartridges)**: risk of **blast effect** (145 to 175 dB: hearing loss, lungs, internal organs) and **shrapnel wounds (polycriblage)** from red/orange hard plastic or black rubber fragments **invisible on standard X-rays** (ask for an ultrasound or CT scan).
    - **Kinetic impacts**: **40 mm LBD** (40 mm ring-shaped bruise, fractures, severe eye injuries), **GENL** (18 rubber pellets leaving 2–3 cm geometric bruises), **baton / tonfa / telescopic baton** ("tramline" bruise with pale shocked skin in the middle, linear split wound or small round tip wound).
    - **Debris**: never pick up a burning tear-gas sub-munition or an unexploded grenade with bare hands; photograph markings with a scale and store cooled fragments in a clean bag.

!!! info "Where to start?"

    - **Someone has just been hit** → [identify the weapon and act in order](armes-police.md#flowchart-identify-the-weapon-and-act-in-order).
    - **Which weapon caused this injury?** → [recognising the weapon from the wound and debris](armes-police.md#recognising-the-weapon-from-the-wound-and-debris).
    - **At A&E or the doctor's** → [what to tell them](armes-police.md#what-to-tell-emergency-staff-and-the-doctor).
    - **Debris on the ground** → [collecting and photographing it](armes-police.md#collecting-and-photographing-debris-safely).

Recognising the weapon that caused an injury allows you both to **alert hospital staff to specific risks** (non-metallic fragments invisible on standard X-rays, internal blast injuries) and to **support the medical certificate and investigation** through characteristic wound patterns and munition markings.[^desarmons-armements-2020][^ldh-violences]

!!! warning "Life-threatening emergency, eyes and blast effect"

    Tearing wound, heavy bleeding, blow or projectile to the head or eye, shortness of breath, chest pain or confusion after a detonation (*blast effect*): call **112** (or **15**, SAMU) immediately. Never rinse or press on an eye hit by a projectile.[^cr-hemorragie][^medic-gestes]

## Flowchart: identify the weapon and act in order

<div class="flow" markdown>

1. **What effect at the moment of impact?**
    - **Close-quarters strike** → **baton** (`tonfa` or `telescopic baton`).[^desarmons-matraques-2024]
    - **Single high-speed projectile, no explosion** → **40 mm LBD** (black rubber bullet) or tear-gas cartridge fired in a flat trajectory.[^wiki-lbd][^desarmons-armements-2020]
    - **Irritant smoke, no loud detonation** → **tear-gas puck** (circular thermal burn + CS gas).[^desarmons-armements-2020]
    - **Explosion projecting black rubber pellets** → **GENL** sting-ball grenade (18 pellets).[^wiki-gmd][^desarmons-grenades-2024]
    - **Loud detonation with shockwave (_blast_) and shrapnel** → **GM2L** grenade (gas + red/orange plastic fragments), **GL-304 "Condor"** (black "tyre-like" rubber fragments) or **EF-SON / ASSD** cartridge (40 mm stun munition).[^wiki-gm2l][^desarmons-grenades-2024]
2. **What care and tests should you ask for?**
    - **Shrapnel peppering (GM2L, GL-304, EF-SON)** → tell emergency staff that plastic or rubber fragments are **radiolucent** (ask for an **ultrasound or CT scan** before stitching).[^desarmons-grenades-2024]
    - **Close detonation (_blast_)** → monitor lungs, balance and hearing; see an **ENT specialist or A&E within 24 hours** in case of tinnitus or hearing loss.[^desarmons-grenades-2024][^acouphenes]
3. **What evidence should you preserve?**
    - **On the body** → photograph every lesion with a **ruler** for scale and obtain a **descriptive medical certificate with ITT**.[^ldh-violences]
    - **On the ground** → photograph **cooled debris** (wide shot + close-up of markings with scale) and keep them in a clean bag.[^desarmons-armements-2020][^ldh-violences]

</div>

## Recognising the weapon from the wound and debris

French law enforcement uses so-called "intermediate force" weapons and pyrotechnic munitions regulated by the Internal Security Code, which regularly cause maiming and severe injuries.[^csi-r211-18][^desarmons-victimes]

| Weapon / munition | Firing mode and effect | Direct injuries and typical pattern | Secondary injuries | Debris to recognise on the ground |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Batons** (`Tonfa` / BDPL, `telescopic baton` / BTD)[^desarmons-matraques-2024] | Close-quarters strike (rigid side-handle baton or extendable steel shaft with round tip) | **Bruise with pale "shocked skin" in the middle** (parallel purple tramlines around a pale central band), **linear open wound**, or **small round open wound** (telescopic baton tip); small fractures, head trauma | Displaced fractures (forearm, hand raised in self-defence), broken ribs, internal bleeding | Rarely leaves debris (occasionally unscrewed metal tip or handle cap) |
| **Tear-gas grenades and cartridges (CS)**[^desarmons-armements-2020][^desarmons-marches-2026] | Hand-thrown (MP7, CM6) or fired from **56 mm Cougar**, **40 mm LMC** or **40 mm Cobra** launchers; splits into burning **tear-gas pucks** | **Thermal burns** (usually **circular**, from contact with a burning puck on skin or through melted clothing)[^cr-brulure] | Breathing distress, eye and skin irritation; **severe blunt trauma** (fractures, face, skull) if the cartridge hits in a **direct flat shot** or fails to separate[^medic-gestes][^wiki-zineb-redouane] | Cylindrical aluminium or plastic casings (56 mm or 40 mm, orange/red/green band), black or metal **cylindrical pucks** with vent holes, end caps |
| **40 mm LBD** ("Flash-Ball" by misnomer)[^wiki-lbd][^ddd-suspension-lbd][^publicsenat-lbd] | Black foam-tipped rubber projectile (40×46 mm) fired at **high speed in a direct shot** (up to 350 km/h)[^mdo-lbd] | **40 mm ring-shaped imprint** (4 cm red/purple ring with central **abrasion / friction burn**; asymmetry shows the **projectile's direction**), small fractures, soft-tissue damage, **loss of an eye** | Wind knocked out, broken ribs, head or facial trauma, **internal bleeding** (chest, abdomen) | **40 mm ogival black rubber bullet** (foam nose) and grey/black aluminium or plastic cylindrical casing (`MDCP` Alsetex / CTS)[^desarmons-marches-2026] |
| **GENL** ("Sting-ball" / _Désencerclante_, ex-GMD / DMP)[^wiki-gmd][^desarmons-grenades-2024][^desarmons-marches-2026] | Hand-thrown grenade: explosion projecting **18 black rubber pellets** at over 400 km/h (effective radius 15 m, up to 30 m; ~144 dB at 10 m) plus the central core; must be **rolled along the ground** and not thrown in the air[^mdo-genl] | **2 to 3 cm geometric bruises** (sharp-edged square, rectangular or round marks), small fractures, soft-tissue wounds, severe eye injuries | Mild hearing loss or tinnitus, loss of balance, psychological shock | **2 to 3 cm black rubber pellets**, perforated black plastic/metal cylindrical cage, fuse head and spoon |
| **GM2L** ("Combined effects": tear gas + stun)[^wiki-gm2l][^desarmons-grenades-2024][^publicsenat-gli-f4-retrait] | Fired from a **56 mm Cougar** launcher (replaced the GLI-F4): **loud explosion** (~160 dB at 10 m), **CS tear-gas** cloud (15 g of powder)[^mdo-gm2l] and projection of **red/orange debris** | **Shrapnel peppering (polycriblage)**, **tearing wounds**, bleeding, **mutilations** (hand, foot, face), with **high probability of visible or hidden foreign bodies** (radiolucent plastic)[^franceinfo-lens-igpn-2026] | Tear-gas effects, loss of balance, **severe hearing loss**, **blast effect** (internal damage to lungs, head and viscera from the shockwave), psychological shock | **Irregular fragments of bright red or orange hard plastic** (bright orange ejectable canister bearing a "do not pick up" pictogram), grey plastic outer body, brown lid, red adhesive tape, small metal spring, spoon[^mdo-gm2l] |
| **GL-304** ("Condor", "the Brazilian") and **ASSD**[^desarmons-grenades-2024][^desarmons-marches-2026] | Hand-thrown blast grenade: first fuse-ejection pop then **very loud explosion** (up to 175 dB at 2 m according to the manufacturer), **black "tyre-like" debris**[^mdo-gl304] | **Shrapnel peppering**, **tearing wounds**, bleeding, **mutilations**, with **high probability of visible or hidden foreign bodies** (radiolucent rubber) | Loss of balance, **severe hearing loss**, **blast effect** (lungs, head, viscera from the explosion), psychological shock | **Shredded thick black rubber fragments ("tyre")** (soft black elastomer body, cloud of inert white powder), metal washers, round base and black fuse head marked `GL-304` / `Condor` or `ASSD` |
| **EF-SON** (40 mm "Stun" cartridge)[^desarmons-grenades-2024][^desarmons-marches-2026] | Sound cartridge-grenade fired from a **40 mm Cobra** or 40 mm LMC/Cougar launcher (single block with its propellant): **loud explosion** (~161 dB at 10 m, range ~80 m), issued to the CRS since 2025; its use was **provisionally suspended** after a 15-year-old's hand was blown off in Lens (5 October 2026)[^mdo-efson][^franceinfo-lens-igpn-2026] | **Tearing wounds**, bleeding, **shrapnel peppering** (recent munition with limited consolidated field data so far) | Loss of balance, **severe hearing loss**, **blast effect** (lungs, head, viscera from the explosion), psychological shock | Black 40 mm cylinder forming a single block with its propellant, plastic and metal fragments |

## What to tell emergency staff and the doctor

When treated by **street medics**, at hospital A&E or by the doctor writing your medical certificate:[^medic-gestes][^ldh-violences]

- [ ] **Grenade shrapnel peppering (GM2L, GL-304, EF-SON): warn about X-ray-invisible fragments.** Immediately tell medical staff that the shrapnel is made of **hard plastic (red/orange) or black rubber ("tyre")**.[^desarmons-grenades-2024] Because these materials are often **radiolucent** (invisible on a standard X-ray), ask for a **soft-tissue ultrasound or CT scan** before any wound closure so no foreign body is left behind to cause a deep infection.
- [ ] **Blast effect after a loud explosion.** Above 140 to 160 dB (GM2L, GL-304, ASSD, EF-SON), the shockwave can damage not only eardrums but also hollow organs (lungs, digestive tract) and the brain.[^desarmons-grenades-2024] Report any shortness of breath, chest pain, coughing, nausea, dizziness or loss of balance.
- [ ] **Ringing ears (tinnitus), muffled hearing or hearing loss.** See an **ENT specialist or A&E within 24 hours** of the detonation: early treatment greatly increases the chances of recovering your hearing.[^acouphenes]
- [ ] **Descriptive medical certificate with ITT.** Ask the doctor to record each lesion's **exact dimensions and shape** (40 mm ring with central abrasion, 2 to 3 cm geometric bruises, parallel tramlines with pale centre, circular burn, shrapnel peppering and colour of extracted fragments), hearing or psychological symptoms, and the number of days of **ITT** (total incapacity for work, including for unemployed people, students or minors).[^ldh-violences]

## Collecting and photographing debris safely

Munition remnants (GENL rubber pellets, LBD bullets, red GM2L shards, GL-304 rubber fragments, casings bearing a batch number) are key physical evidence to identify the weapon and firing unit.[^desarmons-armements-2020][^ldh-violences]

1. **Never touch an unexploded or burning munition.** If an intact grenade is on the ground, move away immediately and warn everyone nearby. Do not pick it up or push it away: the LDH reminds that it may be an offensive grenade, able to cause a mutilation.[^ldh-fiche-manif-2026] Wait until a tear-gas puck has completely cooled before handling it (risk of severe burns).[^medic-gestes][^cr-brulure]
2. **Photograph the debris in place before moving it.** First take a **wide shot** showing the exact location (street corner, shopfront, street furniture), then a **close-up** with a ruler, coin or key next to it for **scale**.[^ldh-violences][^witness-preuve]
3. **Frame the markings and batch numbers.** Take sharp photos of all printed or stamped markings on the cylinder body, base or spoon (`SAE`, `Alsetex`, `Nobel Sport`, `Condor`, `GL-304`, `GM2L`, `GENL`, `EF-SON`, `40 mm` or `56 mm` calibre, batch number and date).[^desarmons-armements-2020][^desarmons-marches-2026]
4. **Keep cooled debris and clothing.** Place cooled shards, pellets or casings in a clean bag noting the exact time and place they were found. Also keep torn, melted or blood-stained clothes **unwashed** in a paper bag, and hand them to your lawyer, a legal team or the collective {{ res("desarmons-les") }}.[^ldh-violences][^desarmons-victimes]
