# Talking to the press at a protest

!!! abstract "The essentials"

    - **You don't have to answer.** Saying no is a normal answer.
    - **One message, one sentence**, about the substance, not about people.
    - **Speak for yourself or for the group**, never on behalf of someone else.
    - **Don't give identifying details** you don't want published: name, employer, neighbourhood.

A microphone held out at a protest is a chance to get a message across, and a risk of being misquoted or identified. This guide helps you decide before you are facing the mic. For filming, see [Filming law enforcement](filmer.md); for not relaying false news, [Check a story and avoid disinformation](desinformation.md).

## Before

- [ ] **Decide who speaks.** In a group, choose in advance one or two people who answer, with a shared message.
- [ ] **Prepare one sentence**: who we are, what we ask, what people can do. No more.
- [ ] **Decide what you will not say**: your full name, employer, address, what you know about other people.

## Facing the mic

- [ ] **You can refuse, or answer without showing your face**: say so beforehand and ask how the shot will be framed.
- [ ] **Answer the question you decided to address**, not the one asked if it's a trap. "What matters to us is…".
- [ ] **Avoid figures you cannot source**: a wrong toll will be quoted and resurface. See [Check a story](desinformation.md).
- [ ] **Avoid words that expose you**: threats, accusations against named people, support for violence. A single sentence can be reused alone.
- [ ] **Speak as "we" for the group** rather than describing other people's acts.

## After

- [ ] **Note the outlet and the date**, and keep a record of what was said.
- [ ] **If there is an error or your words are distorted**, ask the outlet for a correction. You can also contact the {{ res("cdjm") }} (journalism ethics and mediation council) or, for television and radio, {{ res("arcom") }}.
- [ ] **Don't share a clip that targets you** without thinking about exposure: see [Social media: don't feed hate](reseaux-sociaux.md).

## Sources

This guide gives communication advice, not legal advice. If in doubt about a publication, ask a lawyer.
