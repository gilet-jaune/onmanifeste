# Never stay alone

Isolation is the first risk at a protest: no one to witness an arrest, alert a lawyer or help you if you are hurt.[^legal-team]

## Before you go

- [ ] Come with a **buddy** (or a small group) and agree never to split up.
- [ ] Set **two meeting points**: one near the march, one further away, somewhere quiet.
- [ ] Tell someone **who is not protesting**: your route, when you expect to be back, who to call if they don't hear from you.
- [ ] Write the number of a **legal team** and of a relative on paper (or on your arm with a marker). Your phone can be lost, seized or run out of battery.[^eff-protest][^legal-team]
- [ ] Write down your medication, allergies or chronic conditions on a piece of paper in your pocket.

## During

- **Check in regularly** with your group (every 30 minutes, or whenever the crowd moves).
- Stay in a part of the march where you feel comfortable; spot the **exits** and side streets.
- If your buddy is arrested: **note the time, the place and what you see** (unit, badge number if visible) and call the legal team. Don't get arrested yourself trying to help.[^legal-team]
- If the crowd is dispersed, go to the meeting point and wait for an agreed time before alerting your outside contact.

## After

- Don't go home alone, especially at night: leave **together**, or get walked to public transport.
- Send a "home safe" message to your contact.
- If someone is in custody, organise **someone to meet them** outside the police station: release can happen at any hour.
