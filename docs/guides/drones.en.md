# Drones and aerial surveillance at protests

!!! abstract "Key points"

    - Police and gendarmerie can film a demonstration with a drone **if the prefect authorised it in a written, reasoned decision**, and only if the gathering is "likely to cause serious disorder".[^csi-l242]
    - The authorisation lasts **for the duration of the gathering**. The drone cannot **record sound**, use **facial recognition**, or be automatically linked to other files.[^csi-l242]
    - Footage is in principle **erased after 7 days**, unless used in a judicial, administrative or disciplinary procedure.[^csi-l242]
    - You can ask for **access** or **erasure** of your data; if you get no answer, you can **complain to the CNIL**.[^cnil-videoprotection]
    - Do not touch the drone or try to destroy it: destroying property is an offence ([art. 322-1](risques-penaux.md)).

Drones are used to see a march from above, spot crowd movements and guide forces on the ground. This guide says **what the law allows**, **what it forbids**, and **what you can do** if you think you were filmed. It gives no way to avoid surveillance.

!!! warning "Not legal advice"

    These rules change often: law no. 2026-798 of 18 August 2026 amended article L242-5 again. Check the version in force and, if in doubt, ask a lawyer.

## When a drone may film a demonstration

Police, gendarmerie and deployed military personnel can be **authorised** to capture and record images with an aerial camera. For demonstrations, the purpose is "the security of gatherings of people on the public highway or in places open to the public", to let forces on the ground maintain or restore public order. The gathering must be **likely to cause serious disorder**, and use must remain **proportionate**.[^csi-l242]

| Point | What the law says |
| --- | --- |
| **Who authorises** | The **prefect** (or the Paris police prefect), by a **written, reasoned decision**. In an emergency, by any means, then formalised within the hour.[^csi-l242] |
| **For how long** | For a gathering: **the duration of the gathering**. At most 3 months, renewable, for other purposes; 72 hours in an emergency.[^csi-l242] |
| **Where** | A "strictly necessary" **perimeter**, stated in the request.[^csi-l242] |
| **Forbidden** | Capturing **sound**, **facial recognition**, automatic links to other processing.[^csi-l242] |
| **Public information** | "By any appropriate means", **unless** circumstances forbid it or it would defeat the purpose of the operation.[^csi-l242] |
| **Retention** | **7 days maximum** after the end of deployment, unless a judicial, administrative or disciplinary procedure is under way.[^csi-l242] |
| **Register** | The authority keeps a register of deployments, sent weekly to the prefect.[^csi-l242] |

## What the Constitutional Council required

In January 2022 the Constitutional Council upheld the principle of drones, with **safeguards**.[^cc-2021-834]

- A drone sees "anywhere and without its presence being detected" a very large number of people: particular guarantees are needed.
- The prefect can only authorise if satisfied that **no less intrusive means** exist, except where agents face a serious threat.
- **Renewal** is only possible if the drone remains **the only means** of reaching the aim.
- The ban on facial recognition also covers analyses done **afterwards** with other systems.
- Authorisation **without a prior decision** for 4 hours was **struck down**.

## If you think you were filmed

1. **Note** the place, time and date, and if you can what shows a drone was there. This will support any step.
2. **Ask for access or erasure** of your data from the responsible service (police or gendarmerie).
3. **With no answer**, or a refusal, you can **file a complaint with the CNIL**.[^cnil-videoprotection]
4. To challenge **the prefect's authorisation** itself, there are emergency procedures before the administrative judge: the **référé-liberté** (answer within 48 hours for a serious and manifestly unlawful infringement of a fundamental freedom) or the **référé-suspension**.[^ce-recours] Go through an association or a lawyer: these appeals have strict conditions.
5. If the footage is used against you in proceedings, it can be **kept longer than 7 days**: talk to your lawyer ([Police custody](garde-a-vue.md), [Procedure](suites-judiciaires.md)).

!!! info "What this guide does not cover"

    The value of drone footage as **evidence** in court is not detailed here: I found no clear official source. The 2020 Council of State decisions on police drones are not detailed either.
