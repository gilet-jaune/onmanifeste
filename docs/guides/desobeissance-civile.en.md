# Civil disobedience: risks, preparation, training

!!! abstract "The essentials"

    - **Civil disobedience** is a **deliberate breach of the law**, on a matter of **public interest**, carried out **publicly** and **without violence**: the four cumulative criteria the CNCDH reports from the UN. It is neither a declared protest nor rioting.[^cncdh-espace-civique-2025][^onu-forst-desobeissance-2024]
    - In the texts we read, we found **no offence called "civil disobedience"**: it is the **acts** (blocking a road, damaging property, entering a place) that are prosecuted. This is not a guarantee.
    - **Obstructing traffic**: 2 years and €4,500. **Damaging property**: up to 2 years and €30,000. **Entering or staying in a home** by force or coercion: 3 years and €45,000. These are **maximums**.[^cdr-l412-1][^cp-322-1][^cp-226-4]
    - A **state of necessity** can exempt you, but only in the face of an **actual or imminent danger**, through an act that is **necessary** and **proportionate**. A judge assesses each case.[^cp-122-7]
    - Freedom of expression does not **cancel out** the offence: in 2025 the ECtHR found that light penalties for taking down presidential portraits did not violate it.[^cedh-ludes-2025]
    - **Training** and acting **with a group** that provides legal support reduces nasty surprises.

This guide gives **legal information**, not advice or encouragement. It describes no equipment and no method of action. For the general list of offences at protests: [What the law forbids at a protest](risques-penaux.md).

!!! warning "Not legal advice"

    This information does not replace a lawyer, and this guide has not yet been reviewed by a legal professional. Arrested? See [Police custody](garde-a-vue.md).

## What it is not

- **A declared protest**: it is declared, it is supervised, and taking part is not an offence. See [The right to protest](droit-de-manifester.md).
- **An undeclared or banned protest**: taking part is not an offence for demonstrators if it is merely undeclared; if it is banned, the fine is **€135**. The organisers risk up to **6 months and €7,500**.[^ldh-manif-interdite][^cp-431-9]
- **Rioting**: for its reasons, without justifying it, see [Why does it get broken at protests?](casse.md).

## Landmarks: three questions, not a ladder

This guide does not rank people (protester, activist, disobedient) from "softest" to "hardest". It looks at the **action**, with three questions.

<div class="flow" markdown>

1. **Is there physical force against people, or serious damage to property?** →
    - **Yes**: this is not civil disobedience under the criteria above, which require non-violence. Violence, damage or weapons are prosecuted as such: see [What the law forbids at a protest](risques-penaux.md) and [Why does it get broken at protests?](casse.md).[^cncdh-espace-civique-2025]
    - **No**: next question.
2. **Does the action deliberately break a rule?** (blocking a road, occupying a place, defying a ban…) →
    - **No**: it is exercising a freedom, such as protesting. See [The right to protest](droit-de-manifester.md).[^ldh-manif-interdite]
    - **Yes**: next question.
3. **Is the action public, openly owned, and on a matter of public interest?** →
    - **Yes**: it meets the criteria of civil disobedience cited above. The offence remains punishable: see the table below.[^cncdh-espace-civique-2025]
    - **No**: it is not civil disobedience, but the offence remains punishable as such.

</div>

- **"Activist"** is not a rung of this diagram: in this guide the word means a **long-term commitment**, not a mode of action. In the texts we read, we found no offence of "activism": it is the acts that count.
- **Within a single protest**, isolated acts of disruption or disorder by some participants do not make the whole protest "violent", according to the CNCDH quoting the UN.[^cncdh-espace-civique-2025]
- This diagram describes **categories**, not moral judgments: a judge assesses each situation.

## How organisations describe themselves

There is **no universally recognised definition** of civil disobedience, according to the UN Special Rapporteur.[^onu-forst-desobeissance-2024] Here is how organisations describe their **own** actions. These are self-descriptions: this guide does not assess them and recommends no organisation.

| Organisation | How it describes its actions | Damage to property |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Amnesty International** (manual, 2024) | "Non-violent direct action" is a broad term: petitions, gatherings, protests and also civil disobedience, which it defines as a premeditated breach of a national law.[^amnesty-manuel-desobeissance-2024] | Does not organise acts aimed at intentionally causing it, unless it is moderate (temporary, reversible, easily replaceable), reasonable and justified.[^amnesty-manuel-desobeissance-2024] |
| **Extinction Rebellion France** | A civil disobedience movement and a non-violent network; actions in the open, at the risk of being arrested.[^xr-france-principes] | No significant destruction of property; it does not condemn other movements that use it.[^xr-france-principes] |
| **Greenpeace France** | Non-violence is a struggle technique; its actions "sometimes amount to civil disobedience"; it talks first and acts with faces uncovered.[^greenpeace-fr-non-violence] | Exceptional cases, thought through beforehand, "as light as possible", with no alternative.[^greenpeace-fr-non-violence] |
| **L214** | "Peaceful and caring actions": filmed investigations, complaints, street actions. Its page on methods of action does not mention civil disobedience.[^l214-modes-action] | Not specified on this page. |
| **Sea Shepherd** | "All our missions rely on non-violent direct action"; invokes the UN World Charter for Nature to enforce conservation laws. The English page cites blocking harpoons and cutting illegal nets, with governments and police.[^seashepherd-fr-qui-sommes-nous][^seashepherd-org-approach-2025] | No definition of non-violence on the pages read. We did not check the legal reach of the Charter it invokes. |

- **The line between "non-violence" and property is drawn in different places.** For the UN, violence is physical force against others or serious damage to property.[^onu-forst-desobeissance-2024] Each organisation above sets its own limit.
- **The law adopts none of them.** Damaging another's property remains punishable, even slightly: see the table below.[^cp-322-1]

## What the law can punish

The text does not depend on the cause defended: it is the **act** that is examined. A few examples, with maximum penalties:

| Act | Text | Maximum penalty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Placing an obstacle on a road open to traffic to hinder vehicles | Highway Code, L412-1[^cdr-l412-1] | 2 years and €4,500 |
| Destroying, damaging or defacing another's property; marking without permission | Criminal Code, 322-1[^cp-322-1] | 2 years and €30,000 (minor damage: €3,750 and community service) |
| Entering or staying in another's home by manoeuvres, threats, assault or coercion | Criminal Code, 226-4[^cp-226-4] | 3 years and €45,000 |
| Staying in a gathering after the summonses | Criminal Code, 431-4[^cp-431-4] | 1 year and €15,000 |

This table is a **sample**, not a full list. For detail and aggravating circumstances, see [What the law forbids at a protest](risques-penaux.md). The text on homes concerns a place of residence: we draw nothing from it for other places.

## State of necessity: not a right to break the law

The Criminal Code provides that a person is **not criminally liable** when, **faced with an actual or imminent danger** to themselves, to another or to property, they perform an **act necessary** to safeguard that person or property, **unless** the means used are **disproportionate** to the seriousness of the threat.[^cp-122-7]

- All three conditions matter: an **actual or imminent** danger, a **necessary** act, **proportionate** means.
- It is not a blank cheque for a cause, however just: a judge and their criteria decide, after the fact.

## What judges have decided (two examples)

- **Breaking into a nuclear plant (Cattenom).** Eight Greenpeace France members entered the plant in October 2017 to denounce the protection of its spent-fuel pools. In 2021 the Criminal Chamber dismissed their appeals: a future danger that no current measure could prevent is not an "actual or imminent" danger, and the offence was not in itself such as to remedy it. The association was also found guilty of provoking the intrusion, and an employee guilty of aiding it, notably for a meeting held the day before and described as a "briefing".[^cass-crim-cattenom-2021]
- **Taking down presidential portraits.** Members or supporters of ANV-COP21 took portraits from town halls in 2019. Convicted of theft as a joint enterprise and given **suspended** fines (€200 to €500), they went to the ECtHR. On 3 July 2025 it held, by a majority, that there was **no violation** of freedom of expression: taking the portraits down was enough to convey the message, it was the failure to return them that amounted to theft, and the penalties were among the most lenient available. Home searches, seized phones and DNA samples were part of the proceedings. This is a Chamber judgment, not final at the date of the press release: check what followed.[^cedh-ludes-2025]

These two cases do not say how another judge will rule on your case. See [Phone: locking, passcode and seizure](telephone.md) and [DNA and registration](adn-signalisation.md).

## What international law says

According to the CNCDH, the UN Human Rights Committee recognises that **collective** civil disobedience can be protected by the right of peaceful assembly (article 21 of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights), **provided it stays non-violent**. The Committee rejects the idea that disrupting vehicle or pedestrian traffic is enough to make an action violent.[^cncdh-espace-civique-2025] This international protection does not prevent a conviction before a French court, as shown above.

## Preparing

- [ ] **Get trained**: theory, practice, legal framework, roles. See below.
- [ ] **Act with a group** that has already prepared the action, the roles (see [Roles at a protest](roles.md)) and legal support.
- [ ] **Know what you accept to risk** beforehand, not during: fine, custody, trial, criminal record. See [What the law forbids at a protest](risques-penaux.md).
- [ ] **Have someone who does not take part**, who knows where you are and can alert a lawyer: see [Support from a distance](soutenir-a-distance.md) and [Never stay alone](ne-pas-rester-seul.md).

## Get trained and join

- **Training in non-violent action and civil disobedience**: {{ res("anv-cop21-formations", "ANV-COP21") }} and local {{ res("alternatiba") }} groups run theory and practice training: preparing an action, sharing roles, knowing the legal framework and the risks.[^france3-formation-desobeissance]
- **Join a movement**: {{ res("extinction-rebellion") }} and {{ res("greenpeace-groupes-locaux", "Greenpeace") }} are organised in local groups, the way in to take part and learn with others.[^france3-formation-desobeissance]
- **In a subsidised association**: the CNCDH notes that the pledge not to "undertake or incite" manifestly unlawful action, in the republican engagement contract, has led to subsidy withdrawals for associations that practise or train people in peaceful civil disobedience. Ask your own.[^cncdh-espace-civique-2025]
