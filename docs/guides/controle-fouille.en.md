# ID checks, bag searches and fines

!!! abstract "Key points"

    - You must **prove your identity**, otherwise you can be held for checks for up to **4 hours**.
    - **Never give a false identity.**
    - A **drone** can only film a demonstration with the **prefect's written authorisation** ([see below](#drones-over-a-demonstration)).
    - Near **stations**, **toll booths** and **demonstrations**, checks and searches are possible on the **prosecutor's order** ([see below](#near-stations-toll-booths-and-demonstrations)).
    - A **full bag search** is reserved for a judicial police officer in specific cases; a pat-down is done by someone of the same sex.
    - **€135** fine: **don't pay on the spot** if you want to contest; you have **45 days** after the notice.

!!! warning "Disclaimer"

    General information about French law, not legal advice. If you face prosecution or want to contest a fine, ask a lawyer or a legal team.

## ID check and identity verification

- In France, you are not legally required to carry an ID card (except foreign nationals, who must be able to prove their right to stay), but you must **prove your identity** by any means during a lawful police check.[^sp-controle][^sp-retenue]
- **If you have no ID or refuse to state your identity**: the police can hold you on the spot or at the station for an **identity verification (vérification d'identité)** for up to **4 hours** (8 hours in Mayotte).[^sp-controle]
- During an identity verification (which is **not** police custody):
    - you have the right to have the public prosecutor and any person of your choice notified;[^sp-controle]
    - taking fingerprints or photographs is **prohibited**, unless expressly authorised by the prosecutor if you still refuse to give your identity.[^sp-controle]
- **Never give a false identity**: giving the name of another real person is a serious criminal offence.[^sp-controle]

## Bag search and security pat-down

Not all bag checks have the same legal basis:[^sp-fouille][^sm-guide]

1. **Visual inspection of a bag** (opening the bag to look inside without putting hands in): allowed during a preventive check or to enter a perimeter, with your consent. If you refuse, access can be denied or a senior police officer (OPJ) can be called.[^sp-fouille]
2. **Full bag search** (putting hands inside and examining the contents): treated as a search (perquisition). It can only be carried out by an **OPJ** (or under their authority), in the case of an **offence in progress (flagrant délit)**, a serious threat to public safety, or under a **written order from the public prosecutor (réquisition)**, which is very common on protest days.[^sp-fouille][^sm-guide]
3. **Security pat-down (palpation de sécurité)** (over clothing, to look for dangerous objects): must be performed by an officer of the **same sex** as you.[^sp-fouille]

- Stay calm and do not physically obstruct the search (risk of being charged with **resisting an officer / rébellion**), but if an item is confiscated without you being placed in custody, ask for a receipt or write down the time, place and badge number.[^sm-guide]

## Saline, goggles, mask: can they be confiscated?

No law bans wearing protection as long as it does not hide your face, but seizures of **saline solution**, **Maalox**, **scissors**, **protective goggles**, **masks** and **helmets** are frequently reported during bag searches, and gear found in a bag has been used as evidence to prosecute people for "taking part in a group preparing violence" (article 222-14-2 of the Criminal Code).[^franceinfo-protections][^amnesty-force][^medicaction-gestes][^ldh-fiche-manif-2026]

- **What has been reported.** In 2016 in Lyon, protesters said their saline had been confiscated; the Rhône prefecture denied giving any such instruction and said that only items hiding the face could be taken.[^buzzfeed-serum-lyon] In July 2024, near an action against the mega-reservoirs ("mégabassines"), gendarmes seized goggles, masks, saline solution and even sanitary pads ("which could serve as a mask"), citing "weapons by destination". According to the legal team present, the prefect's orders had not been shown to justify the vehicle searches.[^reporterre-bassines-saisies] Médic'Action warns that police and courts may see protective gear (especially a full-face cartridge mask) as premeditation of illegal acts or an intent to hide one's face, with a risk of seizure or even arrest.[^medicaction-touxtes]
- **What the texts we read say** (a reading to be confirmed by a lawyer):
    - Near a demonstration, police can inspect or search bags **on the written order of the public prosecutor**, to look for the carrying of a weapon.[^cpp-78-2-5] The search takes place **in your presence**, and you can **ask for a report (procès-verbal)**, a copy of which is given to you.[^cpp-78-controles]
    - A **weapon** is an object designed to kill or injure. Any other object is treated as a weapon only if it can be dangerous and is **used, or intended by the person carrying it**, to kill, injure or threaten.[^cp-132-75] In our reading, saline carried to rinse your eyes does not fit that definition. An officer may judge otherwise on the spot: you contest afterwards.
    - These articles **do not expressly provide** for seizing objects or issuing a receipt. Other seizure rules exist, which we have not detailed here.
- **Prefectoral orders and circulars.** A prefectoral order can ban protective gear (helmet, mask…) on the day of a protest: read the one that applies to you. The LDH considers their legality doubtful and reports that an administrative court annulled one in 2024 (Paris administrative court, 29 November 2024); we could not verify that decision.[^ldh-fiche-manif-2026] For its part, the Conseil d'État ruled in 2021 that the Interior Minister has no power to set, by circular, conditions on wearing protective equipment, whether for journalists or for anyone taking part in a protest.[^ce-snmo]
- **What to do on the spot.** Stay calm and do not resist physically. You can ask whether there is a **written order from the prosecutor**, but you cannot always find out on the spot. **Ask for the report** and a list of what is taken. Note the time, the place, the type of officer (alone or with colleagues) and the badge number, and take photos or film if you can: a confiscation can be challenged, but evidence is needed (witnesses, videos).[^ldh-fiche-manif-2026] Afterwards, talk to a legal team or a lawyer ([see "Filing a complaint, voluntary interview and trial"](suites-judiciaires.md)).
- **Practical tips** (not rules of law): prefer **discreet protection under your clothes**, carry **nothing that could pass for a weapon** ([see "Protest kit and outfit"](kit-manif.md)), and carry saline in **single doses** rather than a bottle, kept with your first-aid items in a pouch, to show a medical use.

## Can they confiscate your ID?

The sources we read provide for handing over an identity document only in a few specific cases:

- **During the check**: you can prove your identity **by any means** (ID card, passport, licence, but also a birth certificate, a student card, or the word of someone with you). If you cannot or refuse to, you can be **held** for an identity verification, **4 hours at most**, with the right to have the prosecutor and a person of your choice informed, and a copy of the record is given to you at the end.[^sp-controle][^cpp-78-3]
- **Identity recording** (municipal police, customs, SNCF, RATP…): you are asked to justify your identity so a fine can be recorded. If you refuse, the officer must alert a judicial police officer (OPJ); if none can be reached, the officer **has no right to hold you**.[^sp-controle]
- **Foreign nationals** without a residence document: taken to a police station to check their right to stay.[^sp-controle]
- **Judicial supervision** (ordered by an investigating judge or a judge of liberties and detention, never by an officer on the street): the judge may require you to **hand over to the court registry, police or gendarmerie** all identity documents, notably the passport, **in exchange for a receipt that serves as proof of identity**.[^cpp-138]

We found no text allowing an officer to keep your ID card beyond one of these cases, and no public-service page explaining it. If an officer keeps it: **ask for a receipt or a record** mentioning it, note the time, place and badge number, and ask for it back; if nothing happens, talk to a legal team or a lawyer, and report the facts to the {{ res("defenseur-des-droits") }} or the {{ res("igpn-signalement", "IGPN") }}. For your belongings and papers during police custody, see [Police custody, on release](garde-a-vue.md#on-release).

## On-the-spot fine (€135): do not pay right away

At protests, police sometimes issue fixed penalty fines (in particular **€135** for taking part in a protest banned by prefectural order):[^amnesty-droits][^sp-amende]

- **Never pay on the spot** if you intend to contest the fine: paying counts as admitting the offence and permanently ends any right of appeal.[^sp-amende]
- You do not have to sign the electronic terminal if you dispute the allegation.
- Wait until you receive the **fine notice (avis de contravention)** by post at home: you have **45 days** from the date it was sent to file an **exemption request (requête en exonération)** (by registered letter with acknowledgement of receipt or online via ANTAI) with supporting evidence (for example: no published ban order, no dispersal warnings given, or inability to leave a kettle).[^sp-amende][^ce-snmo]
- **Contest together**: the {{ res("caj-kit-amendes", "Collectif d'Action Judiciaire") }} offers a kit to contest these fines (strategies, collective defence, template letters). On the first page of the kit it lists as possible arguments a lack of legal basis, the suspension or expiry of the ban order and a wrong perimeter, and advises contacting other people fined for the same facts.[^caj-kit-amendes] This is a collective's view, not a statute: ask a lawyer or a legal team.

## Near stations, toll booths and demonstrations

Checks are not the same everywhere.[^cpp-78-controles]

- **Border zones, stations, ports and international airports**: **anyone's** identity can be checked, without any particular reason, within **20 km** of the border and in the publicly accessible areas of stations, ports and airports open to international traffic (designated by order), as well as at the **motorway toll booths** concerned. Limit: **12 consecutive hours** in the same place, and **no systematic checks** on everyone present.
- **Written orders from the prosecutor**: to look for offences they specify (terrorism, weapons, explosives, theft, handling stolen goods, drug trafficking), in places and for a period of **24 hours (renewable)**, police can check anyone's identity, search **vehicles** (with the driver present) and **bags** (with the owner present).[^cpp-78-controles] You get a **written record** if you ask for it.
- **Preventing a serious threat to safety** (article 78-2-4): vehicle or bag search **with your consent** or, failing that, on the prosecutor's instructions; you can be held **30 minutes at most** meanwhile.[^cpp-78-2-4]
- **Around a demonstration** (article 78-2-5): on a **written order from the prosecutor**, a visual inspection and search of bags and vehicle searches can take place at the scene and its immediate surroundings, to look for a **weapon** (taking part in a demonstration while armed, [art. 431-10](risques-penaux.md)). The Constitutional Council specified that these operations can only target **specified places and limited periods**.[^cpp-78-2-5][^cc-2019-780]
- **In all cases**: if you cannot or will not prove your identity, police can hold you **4 hours at most** to check it (art. 78-3, see above).[^cpp-78-3]

    These articles will be repealed and recodified from **2029** (order no. 2025-1091 of 19 November 2025). Check the version in force.

## Drones over a demonstration

Police and gendarmerie can film a demonstration with a drone **if the prefect authorised it in a written, reasoned decision**, only if the gathering is "likely to cause serious disorder", and **for the duration of the gathering**.[^csi-l242]

- The drone cannot **record sound**, use **facial recognition**, or be automatically linked to other files.[^csi-l242]
- The public is informed "by any appropriate means", unless circumstances forbid it.[^csi-l242]
- Footage is in principle **erased after 7 days**, unless a judicial, administrative or disciplinary procedure is under way.[^csi-l242]
- The Constitutional Council required the prefect to check that **no less intrusive means** exist, and renewal only if the drone remains the only means.[^cc-2021-834]
- To ask for **access** or **erasure** of your data: write to the responsible service, then **complain to the CNIL** if there is no answer.[^cnil-videoprotection] To challenge the prefect's authorisation: référé-liberté or référé-suspension before the administrative judge, with an association or a lawyer.[^ce-recours]
- Do not try to destroy a drone: that is damage to property ([art. 322-1](risques-penaux.md)).

This article (L242-5) was amended by the law of 18 August 2026: check the version in force.
