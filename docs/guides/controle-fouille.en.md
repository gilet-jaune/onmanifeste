# ID checks, bag searches and fines

!!! abstract "Key points"

    - You must **prove your identity**, otherwise you can be held for checks for up to **4 hours**.
    - **Never give a false identity.**
    - Near **stations**, **toll booths** and **demonstrations**, checks and searches are possible on the **prosecutor's order** ([see below](#near-stations-toll-booths-and-demonstrations)).
    - A **full bag search** is reserved for a judicial police officer in specific cases; a pat-down is done by someone of the same sex.
    - **€135** fine: **don't pay on the spot** if you want to contest; you have **45 days** after the notice.

!!! warning "Disclaimer"

    General information about French law, not legal advice. If you face prosecution or want to contest a fine, ask a lawyer or a legal team.

## ID check and identity verification

- In France, you are not legally required to carry an ID card (except foreign nationals, who must be able to prove their right to stay), but you must **prove your identity** by any means during a lawful police check.[^sp-controle][^sp-retenue]
- **If you have no ID or refuse to state your identity**: the police can hold you on the spot or at the station for an **identity verification (vérification d'identité)** for up to **4 hours** (8 hours in Mayotte).[^sp-controle]
- During an identity verification (which is **not** police custody):
    - you have the right to have the public prosecutor and any person of your choice notified;[^sp-controle]
    - taking fingerprints or photographs is **prohibited**, unless expressly authorised by the prosecutor if you still refuse to give your identity.[^sp-controle]
- **Never give a false identity**: giving the name of another real person is a serious criminal offence.[^sp-controle]

## Bag search and security pat-down

Not all bag checks have the same legal basis:[^sp-fouille][^sm-guide]

1. **Visual inspection of a bag** (opening the bag to look inside without putting hands in): allowed during a preventive check or to enter a perimeter, with your consent. If you refuse, access can be denied or a senior police officer (OPJ) can be called.[^sp-fouille]
2. **Full bag search** (putting hands inside and examining the contents): treated as a search (perquisition). It can only be carried out by an **OPJ** (or under their authority), in the case of an **offence in progress (flagrant délit)**, a serious threat to public safety, or under a **written order from the public prosecutor (réquisition)**, which is very common on protest days.[^sp-fouille][^sm-guide]
3. **Security pat-down (palpation de sécurité)** (over clothing, to look for dangerous objects): must be performed by an officer of the **same sex** as you.[^sp-fouille]

- Stay calm and do not physically obstruct the search (risk of being charged with **resisting an officer / rébellion**), but if an item is confiscated without you being placed in custody, ask for a receipt or write down the time, place and badge number.[^sm-guide]

## On-the-spot fine (€135): do not pay right away

At protests, police sometimes issue fixed penalty fines (in particular **€135** for taking part in a protest banned by prefectural order):[^amnesty-droits][^sp-amende]

- **Never pay on the spot** if you intend to contest the fine: paying counts as admitting the offence and permanently ends any right of appeal.[^sp-amende]
- You do not have to sign the electronic terminal if you dispute the allegation.
- Wait until you receive the **fine notice (avis de contravention)** by post at home: you have **45 days** from the date it was sent to file an **exemption request (requête en exonération)** (by registered letter with acknowledgement of receipt or online via ANTAI) with supporting evidence (for example: no published ban order, no dispersal warnings given, or inability to leave a kettle).[^sp-amende][^ce-snmo]

## Near stations, toll booths and demonstrations

Checks are not the same everywhere.[^cpp-78-controles]

- **Border zones, stations, ports and international airports**: **anyone's** identity can be checked, without any particular reason, within **20 km** of the border and in the publicly accessible areas of stations, ports and airports open to international traffic (designated by order), as well as at the **motorway toll booths** concerned. Limit: **12 consecutive hours** in the same place, and **no systematic checks** on everyone present.
- **Written orders from the prosecutor**: to look for offences they specify (terrorism, weapons, explosives, theft, handling stolen goods, drug trafficking), in places and for a period of **24 hours (renewable)**, police can check anyone's identity, search **vehicles** (with the driver present) and **bags** (with the owner present).[^cpp-78-controles] You get a **written record** if you ask for it.
- **Preventing a serious threat to safety** (article 78-2-4): vehicle or bag search **with your consent** or, failing that, on the prosecutor's instructions; you can be held **30 minutes at most** meanwhile.[^cpp-78-2-4]
- **Around a demonstration** (article 78-2-5): on a **written order from the prosecutor**, a visual inspection and search of bags and vehicle searches can take place at the scene and its immediate surroundings, to look for a **weapon** (taking part in a demonstration while armed, [art. 431-10](risques-penaux.md)). The Constitutional Council specified that these operations can only target **specified places and limited periods**.[^cpp-78-2-5][^cc-2019-780]
- **In all cases**: if you cannot or will not prove your identity, police can hold you **4 hours at most** to check it (art. 78-3, see above).[^cpp-78-3]
