# Film the police

!!! abstract "Key points"

    - **Filming the police is legal**: do it, it is often the only evidence.
    - Turn on encrypted **automatic backup**, including over mobile data.
    - Film **continuously**, with context, framed on the officers, **not on protesters**.
    - Keep the **original untouched** and get a copy off the phone as soon as possible.
    - Before publishing: **blur** anything that identifies a person and remove metadata.

**Film police interventions, always.** It is legal in France: officers on duty have no special right over their image, and the freedom to inform prevails.[^amnesty-filmer][^ldh-filmer] This footage is often the only evidence of violence or of an inaccurate police report; France's Défenseure des droits calls it "very useful".[^ddd-videos]

The LDH adds that law enforcement officers have no right to forbid you from filming them, to arrest you for that reason, to confiscate or destroy your equipment or to ask you to delete content, on pain of disciplinary and criminal sanctions. The only exception to the absence of image rights: agents of the intervention and counter-terrorism units listed by ministerial order (GIGN, GIPN, BRI…), and cases of defamation or insult because of their duties.[^ldh-fiche-manif-2026]

But footage can also expose protesters. Film the police, protect the people.

## Before: automatic backup

If your phone is seized, broken or stolen, footage that wasn't backed up is lost.

- [ ] Turn on **automatic backup** of photos and videos, **including over mobile data**: by default it often only runs on Wi-Fi.[^icloud-cellular][^google-backup]
- [ ] Choose an **end-to-end encrypted** service (Ente, Proton Drive, or iCloud with "Advanced Data Protection"): otherwise the provider can access your images and may be required to hand them over.[^apple-adp][^icloud-security]
- [ ] Before the protest, check that a test photo shows up in the cloud **over 4G/5G**, with Wi-Fi off.

## During

- [ ] Start **before** the action if you can, and film **continuously**: what comes before and after matters as much as the act itself.[^witness-preuve]
- [ ] Show the **context**: a street sign, a landmark, the time on screen or said out loud.[^witness-preuve]
- [ ] **Frame the officers**: actions, weapons used, unit, ID number (RIO) if visible. Avoid the faces of protesters and of people being arrested.[^witness-police]
- [ ] **Why avoid faces, even if you will blur them afterwards**: blurring only protects the published copy. The original, which must be kept intact, still contains the faces, and it can be seen if the phone is seized, or passed on to the legal team and to a lawyer. Avoiding faces while filming reduces that risk. It is not a ban: filming a public protest is still allowed.[^sp-droit-image]
- [ ] **A face you cannot avoid** (crowd, an injured or arrested person): don't seek it out, keep the original intact and show it only to the legal team or a lawyer; publish only a blurred copy without metadata. A victim's face can be part of the evidence.
- [ ] Hold the phone **horizontally** and steady; keep your distance, don't obstruct the intervention.
- [ ] Don't stop filming if told to without reason: it is your right. Stay calm and polite.[^amnesty-filmer]

## Right after: keep the evidence

- [ ] **Never edit the original** (no cropping, no filter): its metadata (date, place, device) is what makes it evidence.[^witness-metadata]
- [ ] **Get a copy off the phone** as soon as possible: send it to the legal team, a lawyer, or encrypted storage. A phone can be seized or broken.[^witness-archivage]
- [ ] Write down the time, exact place and what happened before the recording started.
- [ ] An app like **ProofMode** adds verification data proving the file has not been altered.[^proofmode]

## Publish without exposing

- [ ] Publish a **copy**, never the original.
- [ ] **Blur everything that makes a person identifiable**, not only faces: tattoos, haircut or hair colour, recognisable clothes, bags or shoes, badges, banners bearing a name, voices (mute the sound if needed). Signal's blur tool helps with faces; the rest is done by hand.[^signal-blur][^witness-police][^eff-protest]
- [ ] **Strip the metadata** from the copy (mat2): it reveals your location and device.[^mat2]
- [ ] **Also blur police officers' faces** before sharing, as a precaution, unless their face is needed as evidence: then keep the original intact and give it to your lawyer or the legal team. Sharing information that identifies or locates a person in order to expose them to a direct risk of harm is an offence; blurring removes any ambiguity.[^doxxing]
- [ ] Don't publish officers' **personal details** (name, address, family): exposing them to a risk is an offence, even though filming their intervention is legal.[^doxxing]
- [ ] **Images of violence**: knowingly recording images of certain violent acts can be treated as complicity, and sharing the recording is punished by 5 years and €75,000, except in the normal exercise of a profession of informing the public or if the recording is made **to serve as evidence in court**.[^cp-222-33-3] The LDH sums it up: you can be declared an accomplice, unless the images are meant for the justice system.[^ldh-fiche-manif-2026]
- [ ] **No file on police officers**: the LDH reminds that creating a file of photos of law enforcement is forbidden; the text it cites (article 226-16-2 of the Criminal Code) more broadly punishes the processing of public officials' personal data because of their status, outside the legal frameworks (5 years and €300,000).[^cp-226-16-2][^ldh-fiche-manif-2026]
- [ ] **Archive the post** (yours, or an official statement) with the Wayback Machine or archive.today: it stays available if deleted.[^wayback] Never upload raw footage showing faces to a public archive.

## Pass it on

- To the **legal team** and the arrested person's lawyer: footage can change the outcome of a trial.
- To the **Défenseur des droits** for misconduct by security forces.[^ddd]
- To a media outlet or an observatory (LDH) if you want to document publicly.
- **Finding footage filmed by a bystander** (an assault, police violence): the collaborative map {{ res("surlefait") }} publishes blurred copies but keeps the original with its date and location, which the publisher passes, after manual review and identity checks, to a lawyer, the person concerned or the judicial authority ("File & access"). Go through your lawyer: admissibility in court is a matter of procedure, not of the site. It is not a tool for identifying an officer. The project is new and its association is "being registered": don't rely on it as the only copy.
- **If you upload your own footage there**: keep your original yourself. The account is pseudonymous but linked to your uploads, the IP address is kept for 1 year (disclosed only on a judicial requisition) and the person filmed can ask for the original to be erased.
