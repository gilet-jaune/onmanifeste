# Signal groups: organising without being infiltrated

!!! abstract "Key points"

    - **Public** announcement group → **airlock group** to meet in person (created by anyone) → vetted **trusted group** → team **sub-groups**.
    - **Graduated trust**: a **vetted secretary**, **one voucher** and a **second check by HR** for the trusted group; **two vouchers** for a sensitive sub-group. Never through a link that gets passed around.
    - **No vetting stops a determined infiltrator**: write nothing you wouldn't want read out in police custody.
    - Before joining: a short **activist name**, also used in person; number hidden from **everyone**; **disappearing** messages, in every group from its creation.
    - Someone gets taken away: **remove them from all their groups straight away**, as a precaution.
    - Compromised group: if you **know where the leak is**, remove it and the group carries on; if not, **move out** and re-invite people **one by one**.

An encrypted messenger like {{ res("signal") }} protects your messages in transit, not against someone inside the group or a seized phone. How you organise your groups, however, can limit what an infiltration or a seizure reveals.

!!! info "Where to start?"

    - **You are joining groups** → [the groups](groupes-signal.md#the-groups), then [part 1: members](groupes-signal.md#part-1-members).
    - **You organise** (secretary, HR, keyholder) → [the groups](groupes-signal.md#the-groups), [the roles](groupes-signal.md#the-roles), then [part 2: organising](groupes-signal.md#part-2-organising).
    - **Someone has just been arrested, or a group is compromised** → [part 3: compromised group](groupes-signal.md#part-3-compromised-group).
    - **You just want the charter** → [copy-paste templates](groupes-signal.md#copy-paste-templates).

!!! warning "Assume someone is reading"

    An in-person meeting keeps out the curious, trolls and the far right, not a well-prepared undercover officer or someone who talks after police custody. Written messages are evidence: knowingly taking part in a group that **prepares** violence or damage is punishable by one year in prison and a €15,000 fine, even if nothing happened.[^cp-222-14-2] In the groups: **logistics, mutual aid, public information**. Everything else is not written down.

!!! tip "This guide is public: adapt it"

    The police can read it too, and that is not a problem: your security does not rest on keeping the method secret, but on what is written nowhere here (who is in which group, your IDs, the register, meeting places, who your HR and keyholders are). **Change the role names, labels and group naming scheme** rather than copying the guide's: on a seized phone, they won't immediately point to your key people.

## The groups

<div class="flow flow--funnel" markdown>

1. **Public group** (one per city) → anyone joins via a shared link; only keyholders post: announcements, date, declared location, links to the guides.
    - **To move on to an airlock group**: set up your settings, make yourself known to a recruiter, ask to join an airlock group (approval), or create one.
2. **Airlock group** (temporary) → created by **any member of the public group**, independently, to organise **one** in-person meeting; only the time and place are written there.
    - **To move on to the trusted group**: an in-person meeting with a secretary (settings, QR code scanned), **one voucher**, then HR's second check, who post the link with approval in the cleaned airlock group.
3. **Trusted group** (vetted, `MVT-CTY-4K9-General`) → logistics, mutual aid, public information; nothing illegal.
    - **To move on to a sub-group**: a real need in the team, a period in the trusted group, and **two vouchers** if the sub-group is sensitive; the sub-group's keyholder adds them.
4. **Sub-groups by topic**, in the same family (same ID) → each only sees what concerns its team:
    - `4K9-Medic`
    - `4K9-Legal`
    - `4K9-Logistics`

</div>

The **trusted group and its sub-groups** are the **vetted groups**, and a **family** sharing the same ID (see [naming groups](groupes-signal.md#naming-groups)). Someone who infiltrates one sub-group does not see the others. Next to the funnel, the [temporary group](groupes-signal.md#the-temporary-group), for an action day or a mobilisation, leads nowhere: it is disposable.

## The roles

Nobody holds every role, and roles **rotate**. And **no group brings together every key person** (see [coordinating](groupes-signal.md#coordinating)).

| Role | What they do | What they don't do | Admin in Signal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Recruiter** | Talks about the movement at protests, on roundabouts, at public meetings; points people to the public group and the airlock groups | Validate; vouch for their own recruits; write anything down about them | No |
| **Secretary** (already-vetted member) | Comes to airlock groups; helps everyone set up Signal; **validates** once the conditions are met; cleans the airlock group and adds HR to it; stays the newcomers' contact | Validate without a voucher; **add anyone to the trusted group** (HR do the adding); keep an attendance list | Of the airlock group they run |
| **HR** ("human resources") | Like at work: do the **second check** on validated people and **add** them to the trusted group; receive doubts; decide on removals in pairs; settle cases when a secretary hesitates | Run airlock groups; decide a removal alone; be keyholders of every group | Yes, of the trusted group |
| **Keyholder** | Holds a group's keys: **watches over its configuration** (message timer, permissions, link), adds and removes; **keeps an eye out** for unusual arrivals and interactions and reports them to HR. HR are keyholders of the trusted group | Add someone who hasn't been validated; go to an action with their keyholder phone | Yes, at least two per group |
| **Moderation** | Reminds people of the charter, deals with issues privately | Accuse anyone in the group | No |
| **"Lost group" lead** | Triggers the [part 3 procedure](groupes-signal.md#part-3-compromised-group), alerts the [local legal team](../annuaire/index.md?tags=antirepression) | Discuss an arrest in writing | Depends on the group |

**Team roles**, as needed: medic, legal (observing, lawyer contacts, following up arrests), build (banners, placards, equipment), logistics (transport, food, storage), comms (visuals, announcements, social media).

**A few more words:**

- **Voucher**: a vetted member who really knows a new person (outside the airlock group: friend, colleague, neighbour, or seen in action at several protests) and vouches for them, privately, to the secretary.
- **Activist name**: the pseudonym used on Signal and in person.
- **Register**: the list, kept by two or three people outside the groups, of active and lost groups and of who is in which group.
- **Fallback point**: a regular place and time, agreed in person, to meet up if the groups go down.
- **Precautionary removal**: removing someone from their groups as a precaution, for example when they are arrested.
- **Leak**: the person or phone through which an outsider gets into a group.

## Part 1: members

### Your path

<div class="flow" markdown>

1. **Get ready** → activist name, hidden number, disappearing messages (see below).
2. **Join the public group** → read the announcements.
3. **Join an airlock group, or create one** with other people from the public group, to meet in person.
4. **Come to meetings and actions** → a vetted secretary helps you set up Signal and scans your account's QR code.
5. **Validation** → once a vetted member who knows you vouches for you to the secretary; otherwise, keep coming to actions: that is where people get to know each other.
6. **Join the trusted group** → an HR member joins you in the airlock group, checks your profile, then posts the trusted group's link there: request to join, they approve. Read the pinned charter.
7. **Take part** → nothing illegal in writing; doubts about someone: HR, privately.
8. **Arrested, lost your phone, group gone** → see below.

</div>

### Your activist name

The same name on Signal **and in person**, at meetings and at protests: you don't shout a real first name in a march, and two different names always end up getting mixed up.

- [ ] **Short and easy to say**: two syllables, a common first name or a simple word ("Lou", "Max", "Linden"). It must be easy to shout, remember and spell.
- [ ] **Unconnected to you**: not your initials, not a nickname your family or colleagues know, not a handle you already use elsewhere (social media, games, forums).
- [ ] **Unique in the group**: check it isn't already taken.
- [ ] If a full name is needed (sign-up, car-share list), an equally simple **fake surname**; never your real name in a group.
- [ ] **Don't ask** for other people's real names, and don't give yours, even in confidence: what you don't know, you can't say in police custody.

### Your settings

Do this **before** joining your first group, airlock group included: once you are in, your old profile has already been seen. A secretary helps you at the meeting; nobody else takes your unlocked phone in hand.

**In Signal**

- [ ] **Profile name**: your activist name; **no photo** of yourself (or a neutral image).[^signal-usernames]
- [ ] **Number hidden from everyone**: Settings › Privacy › Phone number › "Who can see my number": **Nobody** (the default, but your number stays visible to anyone who already has it in their contacts), and "Who can find me by my number": **Nobody** (by default, anyone can check that your number is on Signal).[^signal-confidentialite-numero] Signal itself knows your number; in response to a legal request, it says it can only provide the registration date and the last connection.[^signal-bigbrother]
- [ ] A **username** (it ends with at least two digits) so people can add you without your number.[^signal-usernames]
- [ ] **Disappearing messages by default** for all new chats: Settings › Privacy › Default timer for new chats.[^signal-ephemeres]
- [ ] **Signal PIN and registration lock**: the PIN protects your **account**; with registration lock on, nobody can re-register your number on another phone without it.[^eff-signal]
- [ ] **Signal's lock** (Settings › Privacy › Screen lock): the app locks after a period of inactivity. It has **no code of its own**: it uses the phone's (or fingerprint, face).[^eff-signal]
- [ ] **Hide screen in app switcher**, notifications **without message content**, and **link previews off** ("Generate link previews" setting).[^eff-signal]
- [ ] **No real names in your address book**: if a comrade's number is saved there, Signal shows you the address-book name, not their activist name.[^signal-profils] On a seized phone, that links the two. Connect by **username** rather than number, remove comrades from your contacts, or revoke Signal's access to contacts (phone settings › Signal › Contacts). If you want a reminder, use Signal's **nicknames**, visible only to you,[^signal-confidentialite-numero] with the activist name, never the real one.
- [ ] **No backup of group history** to the cloud or a shared computer; no automatic saving of photos to your gallery.

**On the phone**

- [ ] A **code** of at least 6 digits or alphanumeric: it is what protects your messages, since Signal uses it. **Biometrics off** before an action, quick auto-lock ([phone: lock, passcode and seizure](telephone.md)).

### In the groups

- [ ] **Nothing illegal in writing**: no calls for violence or damage, no threats, not even as a joke (see the box above). Sensitive matters are discussed in person, without phones.
- [ ] **No voice messages**: your voice identifies you, it is biometric data,[^cnil-assistants-vocaux] and a voice note also picks up the sounds around you (place, other voices). It is also played out loud, sometimes near other people. Write instead.
- [ ] **Clean links before sharing them**: a link copied from Instagram, TikTok, YouTube or X often contains a share ID tied to **your account** (`?igsh=`, `?si=`, short `vm.tiktok.com` links…). Public tools can find out who shared it.[^behindit] Delete everything after the `?`; for a short link, open it first, then copy the full address and clean it.
- [ ] **Blur faces** before sending a photo, with Signal's blur tool.[^signal-blur] No real names, addresses or screenshots.
- [ ] **No live location**: a meeting place is given in person, or at the last minute in the airlock group.
- [ ] **Need help? Name someone**: "@Lou, can you handle the banner on Saturday?" rather than "can anyone…?". The bigger a group, the less each person responds, except when you name the person.[^markey-2000][^darley-latane-1968] **Emergency**: call **15** or **112**, not the group.
- [ ] **Label**: Signal lets you show your role next to your name, in each group separately; only set one if the charter says so (see [role labels](groupes-signal.md#role-labels)).
- [ ] **Doubts about someone**: HR, privately. Never accuse anyone in the group: an infiltration rumour destroys a group more surely than an infiltrator.

### If you are arrested or lose your phone

1. **In police custody, talk about no one**: you have the right to remain silent,[^sp-gav] and nothing obliges you to talk about others, your groups or their members ([police custody](garde-a-vue.md)).
2. **Tell HR** as soon as possible, in person or through a comrade: you will be removed from the groups as a precaution, nothing personal.
3. **Recover your account** on another phone with your number and PIN, then remove any unknown **linked devices** ([phone: if your phone is seized or lost](telephone.md#if-your-phone-is-seized-or-lost)).
4. **At the debrief**, say everything: phone taken, unlocked, code given, how long out of your sight. It isn't a failure; it is what protects the others.

### If the group disappears or you are removed

- [ ] **Plan ahead**: remember, without writing it down, the activist name of your secretary or of an HR member, and a **fallback point** agreed in person (a regular place and time: market, assembly, roundabout).
- [ ] **Don't panic**: a removal is often a precaution (you were near an arrest, a phone was lost).
- [ ] **Don't leave a group on your own** because it seems compromised: wait for keyholders or HR to tell you. As long as you are in it, you are on the list of people to re-invite.
- [ ] **Don't create a replacement group** and don't talk about it on another network; go to the fallback point or contact HR.
- [ ] **Only accept an invitation from a keyholder or secretary you know**, to a group that follows the naming scheme; if in doubt, check in person. An invitation from a stranger, or one posted in the old group, may be a trap.

### Leaving properly

1. **Leave the group**: group settings › Leave group.
2. **Delete the chat**: deleting the chat does not leave the group, and leaving does not delete the history.[^signal-supprimer]
3. **Delete what remains elsewhere**: saved photos and files, screenshots, and the chat on your linked devices (Signal Desktop).
4. **Tell HR** so the register can be updated.

## Part 2: organising

### Recruitment end to end, role by role

| Step | Newcomer | Secretary | HR | Keyholder |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Before** | Joins the public group; sets up their settings (activist name, hidden number) | — | — | Sets up **empty** groups: message timer, permissions, link with approval |
| **2. Airlock group** | Joins an airlock group, or creates one with others (timer set before any invitation, link with approval)[^signal-lien-groupe] | Is added as admin; posts the time and place the day before, then again on the day | — | — |
| **3. Meeting** | Comes, no phone on the table; sets up their settings **with** the secretary | Hosts; helps with settings; **scans each account's safety-number QR code**, which is then marked "verified" on their phone[^eff-signal] | — | — |
| **4. Voucher** | A vetted member who knows them vouches for them, privately | **Validates** if all the conditions are met (see [validating](groupes-signal.md#validating-graduated-trust)) | Settle it if in doubt | — |
| **5. Cleaning** | — | Removes unvalidated people, turns off the airlock group's link, **adds an HR member to it**, who is thus only exposed to validated people | — | — |
| **6. Second check** | Answers any questions | — | Check profiles: name, no photo, hidden number, no duplicate name | — |
| **7. Adding** | Requests to join through the link posted in the airlock group | — | Post the trusted group's link, **approve** only the airlock group's members, reset the link, leave the airlock group, record the names in the register[^signal-lien-groupe] | Checks that the arrivals match: no strangers, no duplicate names |
| **8. Afterwards** | Reads the charter; trial period; sensitive sub-groups later | Stays their contact | Receive doubts, decide on removals; an arrest: [precautionary removal](groupes-signal.md#precautionary-removal) | **Watches**: unexpected arrivals, name changes, odd behaviour; reports to HR |

### The airlock group

- [ ] **No airlock group gives access to the vetted groups on its own.** Anyone can create one, including someone with bad intentions: it is an open meeting, nothing more. Say so in the public group.
- [ ] **What someone sees there**: the time and place, the others' activist names, and, at the meeting, the secretary's face. So nothing else is written there, and secretaries **rotate**.
- [ ] **It is not a waiting room**: it is for **one** meeting. People not yet validated stay in the public group.
- [ ] **At the meeting, identity is not checked.** No ID, no real name, no job, no neighbourhood: these questions create identifying information, and an infiltrator has a ready-made story. Small talk puts people at ease, with no personal questions and nothing written down; no round of jobs or neighbourhoods, which also protects newcomers from each other. Check instead:
    1. **the account and settings**: QR code scanned, activist name, hidden number;
    2. **who knows them**: "how did you hear about the group? does anyone here or in the movement know you?", to spot a possible voucher;
    3. **what they want to do**: which actions, which team;
    4. **that they understood the charter**, by having them put it in their own words;
    5. **warning signs**, without accusing: pushing for illegal or violent action, wanting to know who is who and which groups exist, asking for names or addresses, taking photos, wanting to get into everything straight away.
- [ ] **After the meeting**, three essential steps: remove unvalidated people (they see nothing further), turn off the link, rename it with a new ID. What they have already seen is not erased: closing the airlock group afterwards is a matter of hygiene (fewer groups), not security.
- [ ] **Then, either**: close it, or keep it as a **backup group** for that meeting's small core, to stay in touch if the main group is lost (closed by mistake, keyholders arrested). Nothing is organised there and nobody is added; two admins from the core, preferably **not** the trusted group's keyholders; link off; disappearing messages at 1 week.[^signal-groupes][^signal-gerer-groupe] It doesn't protect against an unknown leak (case B).
- [ ] **Without a backup airlock group**, people find each other through a neutral message from keyholders in the public group ("HR will get back to you") and the fallback point in person.
- [ ] **In the public group, the member list is visible to everyone**, and Signal can't hide it: someone can pick contacts from it for an airlock group, or to harass. Hence the activist name and hidden number, from the public group onwards.

### Recruiters and secretaries

- [ ] **Several of each**, so you depend on no one, and they rotate.
- [ ] **Recruiters don't validate** and cannot vouch for their own recruits: otherwise a single infiltrator brings in their whole circle. They are not keyholders of vetted groups (the role is exposed), and they send the [settings](groupes-signal.md#your-settings) list before a meeting.
- [ ] **Secretaries are already-vetted members**, never someone from the airlock group picked on the spot. Newcomers can, however, take small tasks with no rights in Signal (timekeeping, handing out the floor, welcoming at the door): it gets them involved and known.
- [ ] **During the meeting**, each person handles their own phone and shows their screens; nobody takes an unlocked phone in hand.
- [ ] **No attendance list**, no notes with names.

### Validating: graduated trust

The meeting is a **first contact**. Validation then depends on the group's level:

**What does "vetted" mean?** Three checks, each answering a different question:

| What is checked | Question | Who, how | Time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The account** | Is this Signal account really that of the person in front of me? | The secretary scans the **safety-number QR code**: the account is marked "verified", and nobody can pose as them with another account[^eff-signal] | 10 seconds |
| **The person** | Does someone reliable really know them? | A **voucher** who knows them outside the movement, or has seen them act at several actions, confirms it privately to the secretary | Immediate if someone knows them; a few weeks otherwise |
| **The settings** | Might they expose the group through carelessness? | The secretary, then HR: activist name, no photo, hidden number, no duplicate name | 5 minutes |

- **Fast path**, when someone already knows them: meeting in the airlock group (15 minutes of settings and QR scan), the voucher's confirmation, cleaning the airlock group, HR's second check, the trusted group's link. **The same day.**
- **Slow path**, when nobody knows them: they come to actions for a few weeks. This time can't be shortened: it is the only way to get to know someone.
- **What vetting doesn't guarantee**: intentions. An infiltrator with a genuine voucher will get through; hence "nothing illegal in writing" and compartmentalising into sub-groups.

| To join | You need |
| --- | --- |
| **The trusted group** (nothing sensitive is written there) | A **vetted secretary** who has met the person, set up their settings with them and scanned their account's QR code, **one voucher**, then the **second check by HR**, who add them |
| **A sensitive sub-group** (medics, legal, logistics…) | **Two vouchers** who really know the person, after a period in the trusted group; the sub-group's keyholder adds them |

- [ ] **Vouchers don't need to be in the airlock group**: they are vetted members who know the person **elsewhere** (friend, colleague, neighbour, union comrade). They confirm it **privately** to the secretary.
- [ ] **Nobody knows them?** They come to public actions for a few weeks; vetted members who have seen them in action and talked with them can then vouch for them.
- [ ] **Met at a protest, even as a medic?** That is not yet knowing them: anyone can wear a medic armband or a legal observer vest, and these teams' sub-groups are sensitive. No direct adding to a trusted group. However, having seen them act at **several** actions and talked with them means you can vouch for them; and if they come from a known collective (street medics, legal team), a vetted member who knows that collective can be their voucher.
- [ ] **A very active person?** A vetted member who has acted alongside them at two or three actions **proposes** them to a secretary and becomes their voucher. The secretary meets them, on the spot during an action if needed (scanning a QR code takes 10 seconds), then HR add them: the same day or the next. Unions, associations and collectives help too: a vetted member who knows them through their union or collective can be their voucher straight away.
- [ ] **Newcomers never vouch for each other**: several infiltrators who arrived together would validate each other. No votes either, nor any threshold of "yes" votes.
- [ ] **A single serious doubt is enough to wait**: the secretary raises it with HR, who settle it.
- [ ] **Signal warns when two members of a group have the same name**: check it, it may be impersonation.[^signal-confidentialite-numero]
- [ ] **Never ask** for ID, photos, address or employer: it creates a dangerous file if seized, and proves nothing.
- [ ] **Warning signs**: someone who pushes for illegal or violent action, asks for names, addresses or the list of groups, or wants to get into everything straight away; a secretary validating many people at once; a voucher who keeps coming up.
- [ ] **No trust list.** A list of "who has met whom", on paper, in a Signal group or in an online voting tool, **draws the map of your network**: one seized phone or the tool's server is enough to hand it over in full. Nothing is passed on in writing; the register never records who vouched for whom.
- [ ] **At the very start**, in a city with no vetted members yet, the first core is a group of people who already know each other in person: they are the first vetted members, secretaries and vouchers.

### Naming groups

A short, coded name helps you find your way and **replace** groups without confusion. Fictional example: `MVT-CTY-4K9-Medic`. The trusted group and its sub-groups form a **family**, which shares the same ID: `MVT-CTY-4K9-General`, `MVT-CTY-4K9-Medic`, `MVT-CTY-4K9-Legal`. In the name, the trusted group is simply called `General`: a neutral word that doesn't reveal a level.

| Part | Meaning | Advice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `MVT` | The movement or campaign | Optional |
| `CTY` | The city or area (three letters) | Readable: the police already know where protests happen |
| `4K9` | ID of the **family** (the trusted group and its sub-groups) | **Randomly drawn**, never sequential (1, 2, 3 gives away how many families have existed or been lost). Family lost: all its groups are replaced under a new ID. A single sub-group lost: it is recreated in the same family, with a number (`MVT-CTY-4K9-Medic2`) |
| `Medic` | The topic, role or team | Stay vague for sensitive roles ("Logistics" rather than the details of the equipment) |

**Discreet variant**, if you also want to hide the movement, the city and the topics: random IDs only, for example `4K9-T2`.

- `4K9` replaces `MVT-CTY-4K9`: the family ID, on its own.
- `T2` replaces `Medic`: a second ID for the topic, also drawn at random, and the same in every family (`T2` always means the medics, for example).
- The mapping (which ID for which city, which topic) is memorised or kept in the register, never in the groups. Each member knows what their own groups are for, and that is enough.
- The cost: it is harder to find your way. Keep a clear name unless you really need discretion.

- [ ] **Avoid words that reveal a level** ("vetted", "core", "orga"): they flag the important groups on a screen or a screenshot.
- [ ] **On a seized phone, the shared ID shows which groups belong together**: their shared members would show it anyway, and the family is replaced in one go.
- [ ] **Pick a name that won't need changing**: every change of name or description shows up as an alert in the chat.[^signal-gerer-groupe]
- [ ] **Keep a register** of active and lost families and groups, and of who is in which group (by activist name), held by two or three people, outside the groups: it is what lets you remove someone everywhere within minutes.

### Setting up the groups

| Group | Size | Disappearing messages | Keyholders | Group link | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Public group** | Up to 1,000 (Signal's maximum)[^signal-groupes] | 4 weeks (the maximum)[^signal-ephemeres] | 2 or 3; **only keyholders post** | On, no approval | Short charter and a link to this site |
| **Airlock group** | 5 to 15 people | 1 week: its lifetime; it holds nothing sensitive | The person who created it and the secretary (to clean it and add HR) | On, **approve new members**, reset for each meeting[^signal-lien-groupe] | Empty |
| **Trusted group** | About a hundred at most | 1 week | 2 or 3, not the same everywhere | **Off**, except while adding people: on with approval, reset right after | Charter only |
| **Sub-group** | 5 to 15; under 10 if sensitive | 1 week; 1 day if sensitive, a few hours on action days | 2 | **Off** | Charter only |
| **Temporary group** | The people present | A few hours for a day; the length of the mobilisation otherwise | The person who created it and one other | QR code shown in person, reset once everyone has joined | Empty |

A group is set up **empty**, before the first invitation: the message timer only covers **new** messages.[^signal-ephemeres]

<div class="flow" markdown>

1. **Create the group alone or with your co-keyholder** → name following the naming scheme, no photo.
2. **Disappearing messages** → the timer from the table, from creation.[^signal-ephemeres]
3. **Permissions** → only keyholders edit the group info and the timer, add members, and, in the public group and the airlock group, set a label; in the public group, only keyholders send messages.[^signal-groupes][^signal-gerer-groupe]
4. **Group link** → with **new-member approval**; for a vetted group, off outside additions and reset after each one.[^signal-lien-groupe]
5. **Description** → the charter, nothing else; also pin it as the first message.
6. **Second keyholder** → add them and make them admin in Signal **before** anyone else.
7. **Register** → note the group ID and its keyholders.
8. **Only then** add the members.

</div>

**A group that outgrows its size is split, it doesn't keep growing**: the bigger a group, the more likely it contains a leak, and the more people a leak exposes. These sizes are common-sense benchmarks: everyone in the trusted group should at least recognise each other by sight, and really know each other in a sub-group. Airlock group too full: two meetings.

| Create | When |
| --- | --- |
| **A new family** (new ID, with its trusted group and sub-groups) | The trusted group nears a hundred and members no longer recognise each other by sight (split by neighbourhood or area); another city; a separate campaign; high-school students (with high-school keyholders); an unidentified leak (case B) |
| **A sub-group** | At least 3 to 5 people share a role and need to coordinate (medics, legal…) |
| **A second sub-group for the same role**, in the same family (`MVT-CTY-4K9-Medic2`) | The first exceeds 15 people, or two teams work on different ground |
| **Nothing new** | The need is one-off: name someone in the trusted group, or create a temporary group |

- [ ] **Match the timer to the announced meeting**: it must cover the time left until the meeting, plus a margin (meeting in 6 days: 1 week; tomorrow: 1 day). Set it **before** posting the message: a new timer only applies to later messages,[^signal-ephemeres] and every change shows up in the chat.
- [ ] **A received message only starts disappearing once it has been read.**[^signal-ephemeres] On a seized phone, **unread** messages remain: short timers on action days.
- [ ] **Never in the description**: names, addresses, meeting places, links to other groups, the list of sub-groups. No recognisable group photo.
- [ ] **Keyholders don't go to an action with their keyholder phone** when an arrest is possible: unlocked, it lets someone add members, read and change the group. Give the role to people who stay in the background, or remove it temporarily.
- [ ] **Between keyholders, verify your safety numbers** in person (QR code).[^eff-signal]

### Closing a group

Signal cannot delete a group for everyone: **closing** it means removing members one by one, then leaving it, after comparing its list with the new group's if there is one (see [case B](groupes-signal.md#case-b-you-dont-know-move-out)). Then record the ID as closed in the register.

| Close it | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **The airlock group, after its meeting** (unless kept as a backup) | It was used once; its link and list are known to unvalidated people. They only saw the time, the place and activist names in it: removing them is enough going forward, but what is already on their phone stays there |
| **Unidentified leak** (case B) | The leak may still be inside: move out, then close the old one |
| **The temporary group, at the end of its day or mobilisation** | It is no longer needed |
| **The group is no longer used** (campaign over, team dissolved, merged) | A dormant group stays on every phone, with its history and members: exposed if a phone is seized, for nothing |
| **The group is too big** | Split it into new groups, then close the old one (or keep it as one of them) |

**Don't close it** for a known leak that has been removed (case A), a member arrested (precautionary removal), a name that has got around (renaming is enough) or a conflict (that is moderation's job).

### Role labels

Signal lets each member show a **label** next to their name. Each person can only set one **for themselves**, and it only applies **in the group where it was created**: it has to be set again in each group. Labels are end-to-end encrypted, and keyholders can restrict them to keyholders (Group settings › Permissions).[^signal-libelles]

Keep a **shared list** in the charter, so everyone uses the same words, for example:

| Label       | Role                                                 |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| `Secretary` | Supports and validates newcomers                     |
| `HR`        | Receives doubts, decides on removals                 |
| `Keyholder` | Holds the group's keys (admin in Signal)             |
| `Recruiter` | Points people to the public group and airlock groups |
| `Medic`     | First aid                                            |
| `Legal`     | Observing, lawyer contacts, following up arrests     |
| `Build`     | Banners, placards, equipment                         |
| `Logistics` | Transport, food, storage                             |
| `Comms`     | Visuals, announcements, social media                 |

- [ ] **Anyone can show "HR" or "Keyholder"** if everyone is allowed to set a label. In the public group and the airlock group, restrict labels to keyholders; elsewhere, check: a real keyholder also carries Signal's "Admin" tag, and HR and secretaries are people you have met in person.
- [ ] **A label is visible on a seized phone**: it points to key people. In large groups, only roles that are useful to others (medic, legal, secretary), and no label that reveals a level ("orga", "core").

**Without labels**, roles can be known in other ways: the "Admin" tag already marks the keyholders; being in the `Medic` sub-group means being a medic; the secretary introduces the roles at the meeting, and a quick round at the start of an action ("who's medic, who's legal today?") is enough. Avoid the profile "About" field: it shows in all your conversations.

### Coordinating

- [ ] **Ask by naming someone**: the more people there are, the less each person responds, because everyone assumes someone else will. This is the **bystander effect**,[^darley-latane-1968] and it also happens in online chats, except when you name the person you are asking.[^markey-2000]
- [ ] **On an action day**, the people coordinating meet in person, or in a [temporary group](groupes-signal.md#the-temporary-group).
- [ ] **Nobody is in every sub-group** to link them: one person present everywhere would expose the whole family through a single seized phone. Exchanges between teams go through the trusted group.
- [ ] **No "orga" group bringing together all HR, keyholders and secretaries**, and no meeting where all of them are present at once: a single seized phone, or a single raid, would hand them all over. On 21 June 1943, in Caluire, the arrest of Jean Moulin and the Resistance leaders he had gathered showed this risk.[^ordre-liberation-jean-moulin] Key roles coordinate in person, or in small groups of 2 or 3 people, by topic.

### The temporary group

To organise quickly, for an action day or for the length of a mobilisation, with people you have just met: a **disposable** group, used on the assumption that the police may be in it. It sits next to the funnel, not inside it: it doesn't lead to the trusted group.

- [ ] **For a day**: created on the spot by anyone; people join through the **group's QR code shown in person**, and the link is reset once everyone has joined.[^signal-lien-groupe] Disappearing messages of a few hours.
- [ ] **For a mobilisation** (a few days to two weeks): people join after meeting a secretary (account QR code scanned, settings done), **without a voucher**; disappearing messages of one day.
- [ ] **In both cases**: a neutral name, no movement or city (`J-7Q`, for example); the timer **set before inviting anyone**.[^signal-ephemeres]
- [ ] **Practical matters only**: "medic needed outside the station", "falling back to X street", "need water". No names, no photos of faces, nothing illegal.
- [ ] **No link with the trusted groups**: nobody is added from them, they are not mentioned.
- [ ] **Closed at the end** of the day or mobilisation: the admin removes everyone and leaves; everyone deletes the chat.[^signal-supprimer] The people seen in action there then have their vouchers for the trusted group.
- [ ] **No network**: {{ res("briar") }} or {{ res("meshtastic") }} ([communicating at a protest](communiquer.md#comparison)), or no phone at all: pairs and a meeting point agreed beforehand.

### Minors in the groups

High-school students and adults can organise together, with extra rules:

- **Never push a minor into illegal action** (school blockade, damage): directly inciting a minor to commit an offence is punishable by 5 years in prison, and 7 years near a school or for a minor under 15.[^cp-227-21]
- **Separate groups** for high-school students where possible, with high-school keyholders.
- **No private adult–minor conversations** unless necessary; everything goes through the group.
- **No photos** of minors.
- In police custody, minors are often pressed to give names: the less they know, the better protected they are ([high-school students guide](lyceens.md)).

## Part 3: compromised group

### What to do?

<div class="flow" markdown>

1. **What is happening?**
    - **Someone is being taken away, or has lost their phone** → [precautionary removal](groupes-signal.md#precautionary-removal), straight away.
    - **A stranger in the group, a leak, a serious doubt** → step 2.
2. **Is the group compromised?** → see the list below.
3. **Do you know where the leak is?**
    - **Yes, and it's removed** → [case A](groupes-signal.md#case-a-you-know-where-the-leak-is): the group carries on.
    - **No** → [case B](groupes-signal.md#case-b-you-dont-know-move-out): move out.
4. **Sub-groups** → the same question for each (see below).

</div>

### When is a group compromised?

A group is compromised when someone who shouldn't can **read its messages** (past or future) or **know its members**. Signal keeps no record of who is in which group,[^signal-groupes] but **an unlocked phone shows everything**: members, messages not yet deleted, the group's name, description and history of alerts.

**Compromised, act now:**

- a member is **arrested or held in police custody with their phone**;
- a phone is **lost or stolen**;
- an **unknown** member appears, or someone was added without being validated by a secretary;
- Signal shows that a member's **safety number** has changed while they are in custody or have lost their phone: their account may have been reinstalled elsewhere;[^eff-signal]
- an infiltrator is **identified**.

**Assess, without panicking:**

- a **leak**: information that was only in the group turns up with the police, in the press or with the far right;
- a member arrested **without** their phone: nothing can be read, but they may talk under pressure; stay alert, no automatic move;
- a member who leaves angry or is excluded.

**The key question: do you know where the leak is?** The "leak" is the person or the phone through which an outsider gets into the group.

- **You know, and you have removed it** (a member arrested with their phone, a lost phone, an infiltrator unmasked): that phone or person has seen the **past** (history, members, name), but nothing of the **future**. The group can carry on.
- **You don't know** (information got out and nobody knows how, a doubt with no identified culprit): the leak may still be inside the group; the past **and** the future are exposed. You need to **move out**.

### Precautionary removal

Removing someone from a group costs nothing and is fixed in a minute. Waiting to find out whether they are in custody gives time to unlock their phone.

<div class="flow" markdown>

1. **You see someone being taken away** → alert HR or the "lost group" lead **by private message**, not in the group: activist name, time, place, whether they had their phone.
2. **The lead checks the register** → list of the person's groups.
3. **Keyholders remove them from all their groups, straight away.** If they are a keyholder, remove that role first.[^signal-gerer-groupe]
4. **Sensitive messages from the last 24 hours** → a keyholder can delete them for everyone;[^signal-gerer-groupe] the deletion only reaches the seized phone if it reconnects: don't rely on it.
5. **In the group, one neutral sentence** ("X has been removed as a precaution") and nothing about the arrest; follow-up goes through the [legal team](../annuaire/index.md?tags=antirepression) ([police custody](garde-a-vue.md)).
6. **When they are released, debrief in person with HR**: phone seized? unlocked? code given? returned? how long out of their sight?
    - **Stayed with them and locked, or not taken along** → bring them back.
    - **Seized, unlocked or out of their sight** → their groups are compromised: case A. Treat the phone as compromised too: factory reset or a new phone, then verify safety numbers in person before bringing them back.[^eff-signal]

</div>

Removal protects the **future**, not the past: what was already on the phone stays there, including unread messages, whose timer has not started.[^signal-ephemeres]

### Case A: you know where the leak is

1. **Check the person has been removed** from every group they were in (register).
2. **List what is exposed**: messages still within the timer and unread ones, the member list, name, description, alert history (additions, removals, name changes). Treat all of it as known.
3. **Warn exposed members individually**, especially those whose number was visible.
4. **Clean up for the future**: description reduced to the charter, shorter timer, recent sensitive messages deleted for everyone.[^signal-gerer-groupe]
5. **Renaming only helps going forward**: the seized phone keeps the old name. Change it only if it says too much, and record the new ID in the register.
6. **No need to dissolve the group** if nobody else is in doubt.

### Case B: you don't know, move out

1. **Announce nothing in the group**: whoever is leaking is reading it. Decide in person, between keyholders and HR, and quietly look for where the leak comes from: what information got out, who had it (which group, which sub-group, on what date). This often narrows the circle down to a few people; a doubt proves nothing, accuse no one. If exchanges must stop at once, a clear, neutral sentence is enough: "**Pause: nobody posts here until further notice.**" Without saying why, or where to meet: the leak knows the fallback point like the other members. Then re-invite people one by one.
2. **Create a new family**: a new trusted group with a new ID, **without a link**.
3. **Re-invite people one by one, in proportion**: first those known in person for a long time, by several members, after a short in-person check and a fresh QR-code verification; people who joined recently, or who had the information that leaked, go through a **new validation**: a secretary in person, one voucher (or even two), then HR's second check. If the leak continues in the new group, it is among its members: the circle narrows.
4. **Close the old group only once everyone has moved**: during the move, its member list is your list of people to re-invite, and Signal keeps no record of who was in a group.[^signal-groupes] **Before closing, compare the two lists**: people not yet re-invited (absent, unreachable) are recorded in the register by activist name, to be found at the fallback point. Signal cannot delete a group for everyone either: post a neutral message ("group closed"), remove members one by one, then leave it.
5. **Everyone leaves the old group once they are in the new one**, not before, then deletes the chat: deleting the chat does not leave the group.[^signal-supprimer]
6. **Mark the ID as lost** in the register.

**Has the old group already been deleted?**

- **If only the chat was deleted**, the group still exists: type its name into Signal's search to find it again, with its members.[^signal-supprimer]
- **If it was really closed**: ask the other keyholders and HR, who may still have it; the **register** gives the activist names to find; each secretary can have the accounts they verified by QR code re-invited; keyholders of sub-groups that are still active re-invite their members; for everyone else, the **fallback point** in person.

### What about sub-groups?

- **The person or phone behind the leak was in the sub-group** → same procedure for it.
- **Neither was** → the sub-group is not exposed by this leak. If its name or ID appeared in the parent group's messages or description, rename it with the family's new ID; no need to dissolve it.
- **Unidentified leak in the parent group** → review the sub-group members who came from the parent; dissolve only if there is doubt about one of them.

## Copy-paste templates

Replace what is in square brackets. Keep the description short (Signal limits its length); the details go in the charter, pinned as the first message.

### Description of a trusted group

```text
[MVT]-[CTY]-[ID]-General
Group names: MVT-CITY-ID-TOPIC. The ID is the family's (the trusted group and its sub-groups); it changes if the family is replaced.
Here: practical info, mutual aid, logistics. Nothing illegal: no calls for violence or damage, no threats, not even as a joke.
No real names, addresses, photos of faces or screenshots.
Disappearing messages: 1 week.
Full charter: pinned message.
```

### Description of a sub-group

```text
[MVT]-[CTY]-[ID]-[TOPIC]
[TOPIC] sub-group: [one sentence on its role, e.g. "coordinating street medics"].
Same rules as the trusted group: nothing illegal, no real names, no screenshots.
This group is not mentioned elsewhere, neither its name nor its members.
Disappearing messages: [1 day].
Full charter: pinned message.
```

### Charter to pin

```text
CHARTER — read before posting

1. GROUP NAMES
MVT-CITY-ID-TOPIC, e.g. MVT-CTY-4K9-Medic. The ID, drawn at random, is shared by the trusted group and its sub-groups. When the family is replaced, the ID changes. Only join a vetted group through the link an HR member posts in your airlock group, or on a direct invitation from a keyholder you know; never through a link going around elsewhere. No airlock group gives access to a vetted group on its own.

2. WHAT WE POST
Practical info, mutual aid, logistics, public information.
What we never post: calls for violence or damage, threats (even as a joke), plans for illegal action. These messages are evidence: written threats are punishable, and so is taking part in a group that prepares violence or damage, even if nothing happens.
Sensitive matters are discussed in person, without phones.
No voice notes. Clean links (nothing after the "?"). Blurred faces.
Need help: name someone (@name). Emergency: 15 or 112.

3. ANONYMITY AND ROLES
Your activist name, here and in person. No real names, no addresses, no photos of faces, no screenshots. Hidden number, disappearing messages on.
Label: your role in this group (Medic, Legal, Build, Logistics, Comms…), to set again in each group. "Keyholder" and "HR": only if that really is your role.

4. IF YOU ARE ARRESTED OR LOSE YOUR PHONE
In police custody, you have the right to remain silent: talk about no one. You will be removed from all groups as a precaution: nothing personal. Don't discuss the arrest here. Tell your secretary or HR as soon as possible, in person or through a comrade, to come back.

5. IF THE GROUP DISAPPEARS OR YOU ARE REMOVED
Don't panic, don't create a new group on your own. Don't leave a group on your own: wait to be told, otherwise you can't be re-invited. Go to the fallback meeting point agreed in person, or contact HR. Decline any invitation from someone you don't know.

6. TO LEAVE
Leave the group, then delete the chat, saved files and screenshots. Tell HR.

Doubts about someone: talk privately to HR, who decide on removals. Never accuse anyone in the group.
```

The legal framework behind the charter: a written threat is punishable by 6 months in prison and a €7,500 fine, 3 years and €45,000 for a death threat;[^cp-222-17] knowingly taking part in a group that prepares violence or damage, by one year and €15,000.[^cp-222-14-2] With minors in the group, see [minors in the groups](groupes-signal.md#minors-in-the-groups).

## When Signal is not enough

- **No network**: {{ res("briar") }} works over Bluetooth or local wifi, without a phone number ([communicating at a protest](communiquer.md#comparison)).[^briar-how]
- **No phone number**: {{ res("element") }} (Matrix) works with a simple account ID and suits large spaces organised into rooms.
- **Shared documents**: {{ res("cryptpad") }}, encrypted, no account required.
- **The most sensitive matters**: in person, without phones (left in another room, or switched off and kept away).
