# First aid

!!! abstract "Key points"

    - **Protect, alert, help**; life-threatening emergency: **112** or **15**, with a precise location.
    - Gas: get out of the cloud **facing the wind**, without running, don't rub, **rinse** with saline solution.
    - Injured person: when in doubt, **15**; pressure point if bleeding heavily; no medical procedures (stitching, removing fragments).
    - Eye injury: **don't rinse**, cover without pressing, call 112.
    - Unconscious and breathing: **recovery position (PLS)**; not breathing: **chest compressions without stopping**.
    - Violent blow to the head or neck: **do not move them**, head in line, helmet on.
    - Head injury: don't leave the person alone; vomiting or confusion, call 112.
    - Afterwards: get any injury **recorded** (certificate with ITT) and keep track of symptoms.

    - **Someone is injured or gassed, right now** → [the flowchart](premiers-secours.md#flowchart-someone-is-injured-or-gassed).

Three reflexes, in order: **protect** (get yourself and the person to safety), **alert**, **help**. Protect yourself too: the aim is not to end up with two victims. Look for the march's **street medics**: they are trained and equipped.[^cr-etapes]

!!! warning "Life-threatening emergency"

    Unconscious person, breathing badly, heavy bleeding, head or eye injury: call **112** (or **15**, SAMU). Give a precise location (street, number, landmark).

## Flowchart: someone is injured or gassed

1. **Life-threatening?** Unconscious, struggling to breathe, bleeding heavily, head or eye injury.
    - **Yes** → call **112** (or **15**) with a precise location, stay with the person.[^cr-etapes]
    - **No** → next step.
2. **Tear gas?**
    - **Yes** → get out of the cloud **facing the wind**, don't rub, **rinse** with saline solution, remove contact lenses.[^medic-gestes]
    - **No** → next step.
3. **What injury?**
    - **Bleeding** → press hard with a clean cloth, without letting go.[^cr-hemorragie]

    - **Violent blow to the head, neck or back** → **don't move them**, head in line, **112**.
    - **Head injury** → don't leave the person alone; vomiting, confusion or loss of consciousness: **112**.
    - **Burn** → lukewarm water for 10 to 20 minutes.[^cr-brulure]
4. **Then** → street medics or a doctor; get the injuries **recorded** (certificate with ITT) and photograph them.[^ldh-violences]

## Tear gas

1. **Don't panic**: the situation is **temporary**, and running will only make you swallow more gas. If you have protection, protect **your eyes first**, so you can find your bearings.[^medicaction-gestes]
2. **Get out of the cloud**: check the **wind direction** and where the police are, move away (often ten metres or so to the side is enough), stand **facing the wind**, on higher ground if possible. Don't run into the crowd: leave **at a calm jog** to avoid crowd surges.[^medic-gestes][^medicaction-gestes]
3. **Without a mask**: make an **air pocket** inside your jacket or jumper and take **small breaths every ten seconds** (without holding your breath, so as not to panic). **Don't bend down**: gas sinks towards the ground (take care with children).[^medicaction-gestes]
4. **Don't rub** your eyes or face.
5. **Rinse the eyes** generously with saline or clean water, head tilted to the side, from the inner to the outer corner of the eye.[^medic-gestes]
6. Remove contact lenses if you wear them (clean hands).[^allodocteurs-proteger]
7. Spit, blow your nose, don't swallow.
8. After the protest, once the residue is removed: **cold** shower first (hot water opens the pores), wash clothes separately ([the full procedure](venir-repartir.md#back-home-decontamination-and-care)).[^medic-soins]

If you were hit by a **pepper gel spray** (much stronger), remove the gel gently with **wipes** (baby-wipe type), rubbing as little as possible and using each part of the wipe only once. Médic'Action advises against running water on the skin at that point: it would dissolve the CS crystals and activate the product even more.[^medicaction-touxtes]

If you are struggling or disoriented, **wave an arm** to signal it. If you are doing better, **help** others: tap the person's shoulder to show you are there to help (and not a cop), invite them not to run and to leave calmly.[^medicaction-gestes]

See a doctor if breathing difficulty, eye pain or vomiting lasts several hours, and in case of asthma, pregnancy or heart conditions.

## Helping an injured person

!!! info "When in doubt, call 15"

    Better to call for nothing than too late. You can give **first aid** (chest compressions, recovery position, quick disinfection, pressure point): these are first-aider skills. **Medical procedures** (stitching, blood sampling, tracheotomy, removing fragments) are on the other hand **strongly discouraged**, and **do not give medication**. One exception: an asthmatic person in respiratory distress who asks for their inhaler. They know their treatment, **help them take it**, do not refuse it.[^medicaction-gestes][^pse-recueil]

    According to Médic'Action, first aid given does not fall, in practice, under the offence of practising medicine illegally, and it knows of no prosecution for it.[^medicaction-touxtes] **Helping a person in danger**, when you can do so without risk to yourself or others, is not optional: deliberately failing to do so is an offence (5 years and €75,000).[^cp-223-6] Médic'Action adds that if you hold a first-aid diploma (PSE1/PSE2) or are a health professional, you are all the more bound to assist an injured person.[^medicaction-touxtes]

**First contact**: channel the crowd by speaking clearly and **assigning tasks to specific people** ("you in the red T-shirt, call 15"), to avoid the bystander effect. Priorities depend on the situation: form a **safety perimeter** if the area is dangerous, watch the surroundings, call the emergency services.[^medicaction-gestes]

- Ask people **not to photograph the victim** ("respect the victim's privacy, their safety comes first, thank you").
- Someone able to give care takes charge of it; another takes care of the **psychological side**: introduce yourself calmly, try to find one of their acquaintances.
- Ask the calmest bystanders to explain what happened, to assess the causes and consequences of the injury.

**Consent and introduction**: if the person is conscious, ask for their agreement, say who you are and what you are about to do ("I'm a street medic, do you want me to put saline in your eyes?"), including for a possible transfer to hospital. If dialogue is impossible (shock, panic, deafness, foreign language), watch their behaviour: struggling, crying or freezing may express discomfort, whose cause is not necessarily an injury. If they do not want to be touched or treated, point them to what suits them best: leaving the protest, seeing a doctor or A&E, staying with friends or family.[^medicaction-touxtes]

**If medics are already there**: offer your help without insisting, help set up a **protective perimeter**, protect the privacy of the victim and of the medics from photographers and, if the medics suggest it and the victim agrees, take care of the **call to the emergency services** (address, approximate age, name, medical condition). Avoid crowding around the victim.[^medicaction-touxtes]

**Assessment, in order (the ABC)**:[^medicaction-gestes]

1. **L: large wound.** Very serious wound (large vein or artery): apply a **pressure point** at once (press hard where it bleeds heavily); a tourniquet **only as a last resort**.
2. **Address** the person: talk to them, ask them to squeeze your hands, see if they react to stimuli.
3. **A: airway.** Check they are breathing: listen close to their mouth (or watch for mist on a smartphone held in front of it) and see whether their chest rises.
4. **B: bleeding.** Check they have no wounds that keep bleeding.
5. **C: circulation.** Take their pulse: middle and index finger (**never the thumb**) on the inside of the wrist with light pressure, or in the hollow of the neck under the jaw. A person who is feeling faint has a very weak pulse.

**Unconscious** (does not respond): check their breathing for 10 seconds. They **are breathing**: put them in the **recovery position (PLS)**, call 15 and keep watching their breathing, unless there was a violent blow to the head, neck or back (see below).[^cr-pls] They **are not breathing normally** or have no pulse: call 15, start **chest compressions straight away** (centre of the chest, hard and fast), **without stopping** until help arrives; if there are several of you, **swap every 2 minutes** with as little interruption as possible, and use a defibrillator if there is one.[^cr-arret-cardiaque][^pse-recueil]

**Calling the emergency services (112, or 114 if you are deaf or hard of hearing)**: say that you are a volunteer first aider at a protest and that you are with a person who has… (a diploma reassures, but the call takes very little knowledge); give the **exact address and how to get there**, so that the ambulance leaves while you finish the assessment; describe the symptoms, consciousness, breathing, pulse and the actions already taken; with several victims, group the calls; **never hang up first** and follow the instructions.[^medicaction-touxtes]

**Give nothing to eat or drink** to a seriously injured or half-conscious person: risk of inhalation, and they must stay fasting if they need surgery.

## First-aid kit

Don't perform a procedure you don't know: get trained with {{ res("medicaction-guides") }}.[^medicaction-gestes]

- **Basics**: single-dose **saline solution**, sterile or non-woven absorbent compresses, bandages, tubular net bandage, medical tape and adhesive plasters, colourless **chlorhexidine**-type disinfectant (_Biseptine_-type spray after cleaning with saline or soap and water, never mixing two different antiseptics; Médic'Action advises against iodine solutions on open wounds, though some collectives collect foaming _Betadine Scrub_), hand sanitiser, **100% water fragrance-free baby wipes** (for tear-gas gel), **FFP2 or FFP3** masks, single-use gloves, ideally **latex-free** (stored in pairs in film canisters or Kinder egg capsules), water bottles and something to "re-sugar" a faint person (drinkable fruit compote, cereal or energy bars).[^medicaction-gestes][^medicaction-appel-matos][^streetmedic-lyon-collecte][^ameli-soigner-plaie][^ameli-trousse-secours][^vidal-biseptine][^vidal-betadine-scrub]
- **Depending on your training and collective**: survival blanket (gold side out, for shock), cold pack and cold spray, absorbent and **haemostatic** dressings, support bandages, triangular bandages, post-op dressing sets and sterile drapes, burn hydrogel (_Burnshield_ or _Burncare_) and **tulle gras** dressing, topical creams or gels for bruises or burns (_Hemoclar_, _Flector_, _Ialuset_, always checking consent, contraindications and allergies first), tweezers, safety pin, pen (to note the time a tourniquet was applied, for example), **scissors**, as well as assessment gear (penlight or headlamp and spare batteries, blood-pressure monitor, thermometer, blood-glucose meter, pulse oximeter).[^medicaction-gestes][^medicaction-appel-matos][^streetmedic-lyon-collecte][^vidal-flector]
- **Skin, mouth, nose**: Médic'Action and Street Medic Lyon mention **Maalox** (in bottles or sticks, **never on the eyes**, for which only saline solution is used) or a CS-gas decontaminant. Médic'Action dilutes Maalox: about one single dose per litre of water, beyond that there is a risk of a burning sensation.[^medicaction-touxtes][^streetmedic-lyon-collecte]
- **Beware of confiscation**: saline, Maalox, masks, sealed goggles and scissors are **often seized** during searches.[^medicaction-gestes] For the applicable texts, prefectoral orders and what to do on the spot, see [Saline, goggles, mask: can they be confiscated?](controle-fouille.md#saline-goggles-mask-can-they-be-confiscated).

## Injuries

- **Bleeding**: press hard with a compress or clean cloth, without letting go, until help arrives.[^cr-hemorragie] If the bleeding stops, make a pressure dressing (new compress and bandage); if it continues or is too heavy, call 112.[^medicaction-touxtes]
- **Grenade shrapnel peppering (GM2L, GL-304, stun munition)**: tell emergency staff that red/orange plastic or black rubber fragments are **invisible on standard X-rays** (ask for an ultrasound or CT scan) and watch for shortness of breath or dizziness from the **blast effect** ([see "Police weapons: effects, injuries and debris"](armes-police.md)).[^desarmons-grenades-2024]
- **Violent blow to the head, neck or back** (hard hit, serious fall): **do not move them**, ask them not to move, hold their head with both hands **in line with neck and trunk**, call 112. Do not remove their helmet unless you are trained: a trained team removes it with two people, or alone if the person is unconscious, to check breathing.[^pse-recueil]
- **Head blow** (baton, projectile, minor fall): have the person sit or lie down, don't leave them alone, call 112 if they vomit, are confused, drowsy or lost consciousness, even briefly.[^medic-gestes]
- **Eye injury**: don't rinse or touch an eye hurt by a projectile; cover it without pressing and call 112.
- **Burn** (grenade, tear-gas puck): cool with lukewarm water for 10 to 20 minutes; don't burst blisters.[^cr-brulure] Do not remove clothing that sticks. Peeling skin (2nd degree): cool, greasy gauze and dressing, see a doctor. White, leathery, hard skin or a painless burn (3rd degree): cool and call the emergency services without delay.[^medicaction-touxtes]
- **Bruise** (contusion, bump): apply cold (pack or spray) **through a compress**, never directly; avoid putting weight on the limb and rest it. See a doctor if the skull or lungs are hit, if the area becomes red, hot and painful, if movement becomes difficult or if it has not improved after 48 to 72 hours; go to A&E if there is a loss of feeling or mobility.[^medicaction-touxtes]
- **Explosion noise** and ringing ears: see someone quickly, hearing damage is better treated early.

## Fainting, heat, panic attack

- Move the person **into the shade, somewhere calm**, seated; loosen clothing; give small sips of water if conscious and with no other injury.
- **Unconscious after fainting**: check how long and in what conditions they collapsed, put them in the recovery position (PLS); if the loss of consciousness lasts, call the emergency services. **Heatstroke**: repeated loss of consciousness; rehydrate and cool down, protect from heat or rain (survival blanket, umbrella) and have them evacuated if it lasts.[^medicaction-touxtes]
- Panic attack: speak calmly, offer to breathe slowly together, stay with them. Touch can make a crisis worse: check their agreement first; there is no universal method, and being there often is enough.[^medicaction-touxtes]

## Afterwards: long-term follow-up

Effects don't always stop the same evening. Keep a record and see a doctor if needed.

- **Note** each exposure: date, place, duration, intensity (gas in an enclosed space, repeated firing), symptoms. This log helps the doctor and may be useful later.[^lacrymo-long]
- **Tear gas**: cough, breathing difficulty, skin or eye irritation, headaches, digestive or **menstrual cycle** disorders lasting more than a few days → see a doctor and mention the exposure. Take particular care with asthma, pregnancy or chronic illness. The effects of repeated exposure are still poorly understood, but a 2023 study links several days of exposure to more cycle disorders; the participatory survey {{ res("lacrymos-de-sang") }} collects testimonies.[^lacrymo-long][^lacrymo-femmes][^etude-hassan-2023][^lacrymos-de-sang]
- **Ears**: ringing (tinnitus), a "blocked" ear or hearing loss after an explosion → **ENT specialist or A&E within 24 hours**: early treatment improves the chances of recovery.[^acouphenes]
- **Psychological**: nightmares, startling, avoiding places or crowds, sleep or concentration problems **more than a month** after the events → talk to a doctor. The {{ res("cn2r", "regional psychological trauma centres (Cn2r)") }} see adults and minors.[^cn2r]

## Afterwards: get it documented

- Have any injury recorded by a **doctor** (descriptive certificate with ITT, days of incapacity), photograph injuries and keep clothes and munition debris: every step is detailed in [After violence: document injuries and file a complaint](suites-judiciaires.md#after-violence-document-injuries-and-file-a-complaint).[^ldh-violences]
- Psychological after-effects are common, sometimes days later: {{ res("france-victimes", "France Victimes") }} listens for free on **116 006** ([emotional shock and mental health](sante-mentale.md)).[^france-victimes]
