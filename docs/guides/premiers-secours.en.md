# First aid

Three reflexes, in order: **protect** (get yourself and the person to safety), **alert**, **help**. Look for the march's **street medics**: they are trained and equipped.[^cr-etapes]

!!! warning "Life-threatening emergency"

    Unconscious person, breathing badly, heavy bleeding, head or eye injury: call **112** (or **15**, SAMU). Give a precise location (street, number, landmark).

## Tear gas

1. **Get out of the cloud**: move away, stand **facing the wind**, on higher ground if possible. Don't run into the crowd.[^medic-gestes]
2. **Don't rub** your eyes or face.
3. **Rinse the eyes** generously with saline or clean water, head tilted to the side, from the inner to the outer corner of the eye.[^medic-gestes]
4. Remove contact lenses if you wear them (clean hands).[^allodocteurs-proteger]
5. Spit, blow your nose, don't swallow.
6. After the protest: **cold** shower first (hot water opens the pores), wash clothes separately.[^medic-soins]

See a doctor if breathing difficulty, eye pain or vomiting lasts several hours, and in case of asthma, pregnancy or heart conditions.

## Injuries

- **Bleeding**: press hard with a compress or clean cloth, without letting go, until help arrives.[^cr-hemorragie]
- **Head blow** (baton, projectile, fall): have the person sit or lie down, don't leave them alone, call 112 if they vomit, are confused, drowsy or lost consciousness, even briefly.[^medic-gestes]
- **Eye injury**: don't rinse or touch an eye hurt by a projectile; cover it without pressing and call 112.
- **Burn** (grenade): cool with lukewarm water for 10 to 20 minutes; don't burst blisters.[^cr-brulure]
- **Explosion noise** and ringing ears: see someone quickly, hearing damage is better treated early.

## Fainting, heat, panic attack

- Move the person **into the shade, somewhere calm**, seated; loosen clothing; give small sips of water if conscious.
- Panic attack: speak calmly, offer to breathe slowly together, stay with them.

## Afterwards: long-term follow-up

Effects don't always stop the same evening. Keep a record and see a doctor if needed.

- **Note** each exposure: date, place, duration, intensity (gas in an enclosed space, repeated firing), symptoms. This log helps the doctor and may be useful later.[^lacrymo-long]
- **Tear gas**: cough, breathing difficulty, skin or eye irritation, headaches, digestive or **menstrual cycle** disorders lasting more than a few days → see a doctor and mention the exposure. Take particular care with asthma, pregnancy or chronic illness. The effects of repeated exposure are still poorly understood.[^lacrymo-long][^lacrymo-femmes]
- **Ears**: ringing (tinnitus), a "blocked" ear or hearing loss after an explosion → **ENT specialist or A&E within 24 hours**: early treatment improves the chances of recovery.[^acouphenes]
- **Psychological**: nightmares, startling, avoiding places or crowds, sleep or concentration problems **more than a month** after the events → talk to a doctor. The {{ res("cn2r", "regional psychological trauma centres (Cn2r)") }} see adults and minors.[^cn2r]

## Afterwards: get it documented

- Have any injury recorded by a **doctor** (certificate with ITT, days of incapacity), photograph injuries with a ruler or object for scale, keep clothes and projectiles.[^ldh-violences]
- Psychological after-effects are common, sometimes days later: {{ res("france-victimes", "France Victimes") }} listens for free on **116 006**.[^france-victimes]
