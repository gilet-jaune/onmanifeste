# Emotional shock, anxiety and mental health

!!! abstract "Key points"

    - Immediate distress: **3114** or France Victimes on **116 006**.
    - Anxiety attack: get to shelter, **4-2-6 breathing**, the **5-4-3-2-1** method.
    - An **emotional aftershock** within 24 to 72 hours is normal: don't stay alone.
    - Symptoms lasting **more than a month**: see a professional.
    - Stop watching violent videos, take breaks, act **collectively**.

!!! warning "In case of immediate distress"

    If you are having dark thoughts or need to talk right now: call **3114** (free, confidential, 24/7 in France) or **France Victimes** on **116 006**.[^numero-3114][^france-victimes]

## In the moment: calming an anxiety or panic attack

After a police charge, grenade blasts, kettling or in a custody cell, your body can trigger an intense alarm response (racing heart, shortness of breath, shaking, feeling detached from reality):[^medic-gestes]

1. **Get to a calm spot**: move away from noise and crowds, sit down with your back against a wall.[^medic-gestes]
2. **Slow your breathing**: inhale through your nose for **4 seconds**, hold for **2 seconds**, exhale slowly through your mouth for **6 seconds** (a long exhale slows the heart rate).
3. **Ground yourself in the present (5-4-3-2-1 method)**: name around you (out loud or in your head):
    - **5 things** you can see;
    - **4 things** you can touch (the ground under your feet, the fabric of your jacket);
    - **3 sounds** you can hear;
    - **2 smells**;
    - **1 deep breath**.
4. **If you are helping someone in shock**: introduce yourself calmly, ask permission before touching them, speak in a steady voice and offer to breathe together at the same pace.[^medic-gestes]

## The following days: the aftermath

It is completely normal to experience an **emotional backlash** 24 to 72 hours after violence, an arrest or police custody (sudden exhaustion, crying, irritability, insomnia, images replaying on a loop):[^cn2r][^france-victimes]

- **Do not stay alone**: talk about what you went through with your buddy, loved ones or a support group, without downplaying how you feel.[^cn2r]
- **Release the pressure to "compartmentalise"**: a protest can be a gruelling time and, if poorly handled, leave long-term trauma. Take time to unwind, then talk it over with others if needed (_defusing_, then _debriefing_).[^medicaction-gestes] Médic'Action suggests doing the _defusing_ within **24 hours** of the action (sit down with friends, eat, laugh) and the _debriefing_, ideally collective, **2 to 10 days** after.[^medicaction-touxtes]
- **Take care of your body**: sleep, warm meals, limit alcohol and stimulants, and step away from videos of violence for a few days.
- **When to see a professional**: if nightmares, startling at the slightest noise, flashbacks, anxiety or avoiding public places last **more than a month** after the events, it may be post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), which responds very well to care. Talk to a doctor or a regional trauma centre ({{ res("cn2r") }}).[^cn2r]

## Discouragement and activist burnout

Faced with violent news or repression, sadness, anger or a feeling of powerlessness are common:

- **Turn off continuous news feeds**: checking reliable sources once or twice a day is enough; watching violent footage on repeat exhausts you without helping anyone.
- **Pass the baton without guilt**: movements last over time because each person can step back and rest when overwhelmed.
- **Act collectively**: collective action (solidarity funds, community kitchens, poster pasting, local groups) breaks isolation and restores agency.
