# Photographing and filming for the press

!!! abstract "The essentials"

    - **Journalist, freelancer or citizen**: statuses differ, the freedom to inform is the same.
    - The Labour Code defines a **professional journalist** by a main, regular, paid activity that provides most of their income. A volunteer does not meet it.[^ct-l7111-3]
    - In 2021 the Council of State **struck down the obligation to move away** imposed on journalists when a dispersal order is given.[^ce-snmo-actualite]
    - Photographing a public demonstration **does not require the consent** of people who do not appear there in isolation.[^sp-droit-image]
    - **Remove metadata** before publishing.[^rsf-photographers-checklist]

This guide complements [Filming law enforcement](filmer.md), which covers backup, blurring and passing on footage. To talk to a journalist as a protester: [Talking to the press at a protest](parler-a-la-presse.md).

!!! tip "Where to start?"

    - You cover a protest for an outlet: [your status](#your-status), then [dispersal](#during-a-dispersal).
    - You publish your photos: [photographing people](#photographing-people), then [protecting your images](#protecting-your-images-and-sources).

## Your status

- [ ] **Professional journalist**: a professional journalist is anyone whose activity in a press company, publication or news agency is main, regular and paid, and who earns most of their income from it. The press card rests on that definition.[^ct-l7111-3]
- [ ] **Freelancer or independent photographer**: the same definition applies: very occasional or unpaid activity does not meet it.[^ct-l7111-3]
- [ ] **Citizen photographing or filming**: you have no card, but the right to information applies to your images, within the limits below.[^sp-droit-image]
- [ ] **Keep your documents on you** (press card if you have one, an outlet's commission or contract): they help you get recognised.

## During a dispersal

- [ ] **The Council of State struck down the obligation to move away** imposed on journalists when a dispersal order is given, the accreditation requirement for real-time information, and some conditions on wearing protective gear.[^ce-snmo-actualite]
- [ ] **That is not a guarantee on the ground.** Stay identifiable as press, do not hinder law enforcement, keep your card and papers, and film continuously.
- [ ] **If you are arrested**, ask to contact a lawyer and an outlet or journalists' union: see [Custody](garde-a-vue.md).

## Photographing people

- [ ] **A public demonstration**: your consent is not required to publish a photo if the person does not appear there in isolation.[^sp-droit-image]
- [ ] **A person isolated and recognisable**: ask for their agreement before publishing, or blur.[^sp-droit-image]
- [ ] **Avoid minors' faces** and people in distress: this is an ethical choice, not a legal obligation this guide can state.
- [ ] **Favour wide shots** and situations over individuals: a crowd can be shown without exposing anyone.
- [ ] **If a person asks for their image to be removed**, remove it: Service-Public says the person should first ask the author to take it down.[^sp-droit-image]

## Protecting your images and sources

- [ ] **Metadata** in a photo or video (date, place, device) can reveal sensitive information. If it is not needed, remove it before publishing or sending.[^rsf-photographers-checklist]
- [ ] **Encrypt your devices** and turn on encrypted backup: see [Filming law enforcement](filmer.md#before-automatic-backup).[^rsf-photographers-checklist]
- [ ] **Blur, archive, pass on**: see [Publish without exposing](filmer.md#publish-without-exposing) and [Passing on](filmer.md#pass-it-on).
- [ ] **Do not hand raw images to people you do not know**: an urgent request for videos can come from anyone.
