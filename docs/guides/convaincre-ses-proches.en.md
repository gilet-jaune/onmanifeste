# Talk to your loved ones

!!! abstract "Key points"

    - **Listen first**, rephrase, look for **shared values**.
    - A **lived story** convinces more than a flood of figures.
    - No humiliation, no "fascist" labels: the person shuts down.
    - Aim for **one small step** and a conversation that **keeps going**.

You rarely change someone's mind in one conversation, and almost never by being "right" louder. Conversations that move people rely on listening, lived stories and shared values.[^deep-canvassing]

## Before

- **Pick the moment**: not in the heat of a shocking image, not in front of everyone at the family dinner.
- Set a **realistic** goal: being heard, sowing a doubt, getting them to come and see once, not a complete turnaround.

## During

1. **Listen first.** Ask what the person thinks and why: "What bothers you about these protests?"
2. **Reflect back** what you understood. Someone who feels understood becomes more open.
3. **Look for shared values**: safety, justice, the children's future, work done well. Don't guess the person's side: ask what matters to them, rather than talking about "right" or "left".
4. **Tell a lived story** (yours or someone close) rather than a flood of figures.[^deep-canvassing]
5. **Ask questions** rather than lecture: "What if it were your son in custody?"
6. Correct false information with the **sandwich**: the true fact, the rumour (in one sentence), the true fact again with its source.[^truth-sandwich]

## Avoid

- Humiliating, calling them ignorant or "fascist": they dig in.
- Sticking on a label ("you're right-wing, so…"): they feel treated as a camp, not as a person.
- Tackling everything at once: one idea per conversation.
- Trying to win the debate: the goal is for the conversation to **continue**.

## After

- Suggest **a small step**: read an article, watch a video, come to a calm rally, sign a petition.
- Come back to it later, without pressure. Changing one's mind takes time.

## Stepping out of the media trap over "rioters"

- **Don't get trapped in a "for or against property damage" debate** or in pitting working-class neighbourhoods against small towns and rural areas: in October 2026, France's broadcast regulator Arcom called on TV and radio channels to exercise "the utmost vigilance" and a "rigorous and balanced" treatment, especially when information may provoke fear, and opened proceedings on several broadcast sequences.[^arcom-communique-2026-10-22] Viewers and journalists' societies judged the airtime devoted to violence disproportionate ([why property gets smashed at protests](casse.md)).[^wiki-17-octobre-2026] On the evening news of 28 September to 8 October 2026, the Fondation Jean-Jaurès found sharp differences: M6 and France 2 ran an item on the students' demands on 1 and 2 October, TF1 its first on 7 October.[^jean-jaures-tf1-jt-2026] The media-criticism association Acrimed argues that most major outlets relayed without distance the account that LFI was responsible for the 17 October disorder, while, according to Mediapart, an intelligence commissioner says they were instructed to target that party; the Interior Minister and the Prime Minister deny it. This is an unnamed testimony and a denial, not an established fact.[^acrimed-lfi-17-octobre-2026][^mediapart-bouc-emissaire-2026][^lcp-lfi-renseignement-dementi-2026]
- **Start from shared material interests**: fuel and grocery prices, wages, pensions, the state of schools and hospitals. Ask what concretely weighs on their daily life.
- **Show what the security crackdown changes for everyone**: curfews, blanket protest bans, plans to shut down social media or algorithmic video surveillance end up applying to every cause.[^amnesty-surenchere-2026][^amnesty-7-signes-2026][^blast-coupure-reseaux-2026]
- **Offer calm, accessible local ways to act**: if someone is afraid of tense city-centre marches, invite them to stop by a friendly roundabout or local market for a chat, put a yellow vest on their dashboard, or contribute to a strike fund ([organising locally](organiser.md#organising-locally-beyond-city-centres)).

## Facing someone who believes in voting

Don't say "voting is pointless": the person would hear contempt for their convictions and shut down. Start from what they already defend, democracy.

- **Don't set it against voting**: "Keep voting, and let's add something else between elections." Protesting is a right guaranteed by the Constitution, not an alternative to the ballot.
- **Ask what should happen when a vote isn't respected** rather than hammering a conclusion.
- **Rely on dated, sourced facts**, not on "we're ignored": the timeline [Votes and voices ignored](../confiance/index.md) and the profile [someone who believes in voting](../rallier/index.md#pourquoi-electeur).
- **If you bring up a new Republic**, present it as an open debate, not a dogma: the proposals (constituent assembly, proportional representation, citizens' initiative referendum) are discussed and criticised, including by specialists (sourced answer in the [profile](../rallier/index.md#pourquoi-electeur)).
- **Offer a small step that respects their convictions**: sign a petition, back a shared-initiative referendum, come to a calm rally.

## "The day we need the police…"

The typical objection after an attack: "Criticise them all you like, the day there's an attack you'll be glad they're there." Don't dispute it: **it is fair**. Show that criticising crowd policing does not contradict it.

- **Start by agreeing**: facing an attack, we want trained, equipped and protected police and gendarmes. Criticising a flash-ball launcher (LBD) or kettling does not mean wanting them not to intervene.
- **These are not the same jobs.** Facing a mass shooting, the response relies on specialised units (BRI, RAID, GIGN, coordinated by the FIPN), backed by the first officers on the scene. Policing a demonstration is a different skill. Saying you challenge a **practice**, not a **person**, is fairer and more convincing than "the cops".
- **Don't say "those who maim would never be sent".** That is false: on 13 November 2015, the first officers into the Bataclan were night-shift BAC (anti-crime brigade) officers, arriving 11 minutes after the attack began, before the elite units. Everyday police units like these also work at demonstrations, and there is no clean line between "those who injure" and "those who protect".[^an-rapport-attentats-2016]
- **Turn the argument toward resources.** The parliamentary inquiry found that these first officers lacked the equipment to face heavily armed terrorists, and that BAC training and weaponry were strengthened afterwards.[^an-rapport-attentats-2016] Ask: "If we want them ready on the day, what is better to fund: training hours and protective gear, or crowd-control grenades?"
- **Talk about exhaustion, not blame.** Officers worn out by months of demonstrations are less available and less clear-headed in a crisis: defending their working conditions and de-escalation also protects them (sourced figures: [police officer profile](../rallier/index.md#pourquoi-policier)).
- **Acknowledge what deserves it.** The medical responders heard by the inquiry praised "extraordinary" officers who shielded them.[^an-rapport-attentats-2016] Saying "thank you to those who ran toward danger" takes nothing away from the demand that weapons and demonstration tactics be regulated ([police weapons](armes-police.md)).
- **Ask rather than answer**: "You want them to be the ones who protect us? Then we want the same things: that they be trained, respected and accountable for their actions."

To talk to a police officer or gendarme: [Why join us?](../rallier/index.md).

## "Young people don't want to work any more" (and "you had it all")

A conversation that keeps coming back: "in my day, we made sacrifices", answered by "you had it easier". Don't try to rank the generations: **each side says part of the truth**, and official figures help show it without arguing.

- **Start by acknowledging the effort.** Many people worked hard all their lives, and that is not what is being challenged. You can respect that work and refuse to let it become an obligation for others.
- **"They don't want to work": the figures say otherwise.** In 2025, 34.5% of 15-24 year-olds are in employment (ILO definition: at least one hour worked), 4.6 points more than in 2019, and 30.5% of those who work are in work-study or internships. But only 39.8% are on permanent contracts (76.2% among those aged 25 and over) and 19.8% of the active 15-24 year-olds are unemployed.[^insee-premiere-2096-emploi-2025] Young people work, often in less stable jobs.
- **"With the minimum wage, life was better": partly true.** Since March 1990, the gross hourly minimum wage has been multiplied by 2.6 and prices by 1.8, about 40% more gross purchasing power (calculated from the published indices).[^insee-smic-2026] But from 2001 to 2020, existing-home prices were multiplied by 2.3 in mainland France, against 1.3 to 1.4 for consumer prices, rents and household disposable income.[^insee-logement-revenus-2021] The minimum wage buys more everyday goods, but housing rose much more than incomes (series ending in the second quarter of 2020).
- **"Retirees had it all": a shortcut.** According to the COR (February 2026, working paper), retirees' standard of living in 2023 is comparable to that of the whole population: 6.5% higher once imputed rents are counted, because they are more often homeowners. Their poverty rate is 10.5%, against 15.4% for the whole population and 21.9% for under-18s. Their relative standard of living has been stable since 1996 after a sharp rise since 1970, and is expected to fall in the long run.[^cor-niveau-vie-retraites-2026]
- **These are averages.** They hide large gaps (region, qualifications, wealth, age among retirees): say "on average", give the year and the source.
- **Bring the discussion back to common causes.** Wages, housing and public services affect several generations at once; that is what the [profiles](../rallier/index.md) show, depending on each person's situation.
- **Ask the question rather than give the answer**: "What seems fair to you, between what we owe to our work and what we owe to our life?"

## Where to find verified orders of magnitude

When a conversation turns to "who owns the media", "where public money goes" or "benefit fraud", rely on independent observatories and public reports (click any name to open its **contact card**):

- **Income, employment, housing, pensions**: {{ res("insee", "INSEE") }} publishes the official statistics (always check the year and the definition), and the {{ res("cor-retraites", "COR") }} those on pensions and retirees' standard of living.
- **Prices and inflation**: {{ res("insee", "INSEE") }} publishes the consumer price index every month. According to its provisional estimate of 30 October 2026, prices rose 3.4% year on year in October (energy: +24.1%, food: +1.2%); these provisional figures are to be confirmed by the final results of 13 November.[^insee-ipc-oct-2026-provisoire] To compare with other European countries, use the harmonised index (+3.8% over the same period).
- **Ties between billionaires and media outlets**: the chart {{ res("medias-qui-possede-quoi", "“French media: who owns what?”") }} (_Le Monde diplomatique_ & {{ res("acrimed", "Acrimed") }}) maps which industrial conglomerates (luxury goods, construction, defence, telecoms, shipping) own major newspapers, TV channels and radio stations.
- **Public budget and tax breakdown**: {{ res("fipeco", "FIPECO") }} (former Court of Audit magistrates and INSEE statisticians) explains in plain-language sheets where public revenue comes from (the weight of VAT compared with income and corporate tax) and where spending goes.
- **Unconditional corporate subsidies (e.g. CICE, R&D tax credit, social-contribution cuts)**: the reference academic study by {{ res("ires-capitalisme-perfusion", "IRES (“Capitalism on life support”)") }} catalogues all public subsidies to businesses (~€157bn in 2019, the largest budget item) and the lack of social or environmental conditions[^ires-capitalisme-perfusion], while the {{ res("observatoire-multinationales", "Observatoire des multinationales") }} tracks dividends and layoffs at major recipient corporations.
- **Tax fraud, tax havens and comparison with benefit fraud**: the {{ res("eu-tax-observatory", "EU Tax Observatory") }} and the {{ res("stop-paradis-fiscaux", "Plateforme Paradis Fiscaux et Judiciaires") }} document tax evasion by multinationals and ultra-high-net-worth individuals. On official orders of magnitude, _Vie publique_'s overview notes that tax fraud (and undeclared work by employers) far exceeds social-benefit fraud, which itself is smaller than **non-take-up** of benefits (eligible people not claiming RSA or activity bonuses).[^vie-publique-fraudes] On wealth distribution: {{ res("observatoire-inegalites", "Observatoire des inégalités") }} and {{ res("wid-world", "WID.world") }}.
