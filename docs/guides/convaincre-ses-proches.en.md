# Talk to your loved ones

!!! abstract "Key points"

    - **Listen first**, rephrase, look for **shared values**.
    - A **lived story** convinces more than a flood of figures.
    - No humiliation, no "fascist" labels: the person shuts down.
    - Aim for **one small step** and a conversation that **keeps going**.

You rarely change someone's mind in one conversation, and almost never by being "right" louder. Conversations that move people rely on listening, lived stories and shared values.[^deep-canvassing]

Sourced arguments depending on the person (woman, retiree, teacher, small business owner, police officer…): [Why join us?](../rallier/index.md).

## Before

- **Pick the moment**: not in the heat of a shocking image, not in front of everyone at the family dinner.
- Set a **realistic** goal: being heard, sowing a doubt, getting them to come and see once, not a complete turnaround.

## During

1. **Listen first.** Ask what the person thinks and why: "What bothers you about these protests?"
2. **Reflect back** what you understood. Someone who feels understood becomes more open.
3. **Look for shared values**: safety, justice, the children's future, work done well. Don't guess the person's side: ask what matters to them, rather than talking about "right" or "left".
4. **Tell a lived story** (yours or someone close) rather than a flood of figures.[^deep-canvassing]
5. **Ask questions** rather than lecture: "What if it were your son in custody?"
6. Correct false information with the **sandwich**: the true fact, the rumour (in one sentence), the true fact again with its source.[^truth-sandwich]

## Avoid

- Humiliating, calling them ignorant or "fascist": they dig in.
- Sticking on a label ("you're right-wing, so…"): they feel treated as a camp, not as a person.
- Tackling everything at once: one idea per conversation.
- Trying to win the debate: the goal is for the conversation to **continue**.

## After

- Suggest **a small step**: read an article, watch a video, come to a calm rally, sign a petition.
- Come back to it later, without pressure. Changing one's mind takes time.

## Stepping out of the media trap over "rioters"

- **Don't get trapped in a "for or against property damage" debate** or in pitting working-class neighbourhoods against small towns and rural areas: in October 2026, France's broadcast regulator Arcom called on TV and radio channels to exercise "the utmost vigilance", as their airtime devoted to violence was seen as disproportionate ([why property gets smashed at protests](casse.md)).[^wiki-17-octobre-2026]
- **Start from shared material interests**: fuel and grocery prices, wages, pensions, the state of schools and hospitals. Ask what concretely weighs on their daily life.
- **Show what the security crackdown changes for everyone**: curfews, blanket protest bans, plans to shut down social media or algorithmic video surveillance end up applying to every cause.[^amnesty-surenchere-2026][^amnesty-7-signes-2026][^blast-coupure-reseaux-2026]
- **Offer calm, accessible local ways to act**: if someone is afraid of tense city-centre marches, invite them to stop by a friendly roundabout or local market for a chat, put a yellow vest on their dashboard, or contribute to a strike fund ([organising locally](organiser.md#organising-locally-beyond-city-centres)).

## Where to find verified orders of magnitude

When a conversation turns to "who owns the media", "where public money goes" or "benefit fraud", rely on independent observatories and public reports (click any name to open its **contact card**):

- **Ties between billionaires and media outlets**: the chart {{ res("medias-qui-possede-quoi", "“French media: who owns what?”") }} (_Le Monde diplomatique_ & {{ res("acrimed", "Acrimed") }}) maps which industrial conglomerates (luxury goods, construction, defence, telecoms, shipping) own major newspapers, TV channels and radio stations.
- **Public budget and tax breakdown**: {{ res("fipeco", "FIPECO") }} (former Court of Audit magistrates and INSEE statisticians) explains in plain-language sheets where public revenue comes from (the weight of VAT compared with income and corporate tax) and where spending goes.
- **Unconditional corporate subsidies (e.g. CICE, R&D tax credit, social-contribution cuts)**: the reference academic study by {{ res("ires-capitalisme-perfusion", "IRES (“Capitalism on life support”)") }} catalogues all public subsidies to businesses (~€157bn in 2019, the largest budget item) and the lack of social or environmental conditions[^ires-capitalisme-perfusion], while the {{ res("observatoire-multinationales", "Observatoire des multinationales") }} tracks dividends and layoffs at major recipient corporations.
- **Tax fraud, tax havens and comparison with benefit fraud**: the {{ res("eu-tax-observatory", "EU Tax Observatory") }} and the {{ res("stop-paradis-fiscaux", "Plateforme Paradis Fiscaux et Judiciaires") }} document tax evasion by multinationals and ultra-high-net-worth individuals. On official orders of magnitude, _Vie publique_'s overview notes that tax fraud (and undeclared work by employers) far exceeds social-benefit fraud, which itself is smaller than **non-take-up** of benefits (eligible people not claiming RSA or activity bonuses).[^vie-publique-fraudes] On wealth distribution: {{ res("observatoire-inegalites", "Observatoire des inégalités") }} and {{ res("wid-world", "WID.world") }}.
