# Protest kit and outfit

!!! abstract "Key points"

    - **Covering** clothes, closed shoes, **no contact lenses**.
    - **Ski goggles** for the eyes, an **A2P3** or **ABEK1P3** half-mask against gas, earplugs.
    - Keep your face **uncovered** until you are exposed to gas: covering it without a legitimate reason is an offence.
    - In the bag: ID, water, saline solution, power bank, numbers on paper.
    - **Leave at home**: anything that could pass for a **weapon**, and lists of comrades' **names**.

Simple, covering clothes with no unnecessary distinctive signs, and a small light bag. The goal: protect yourself, be able to leave quickly, and carry nothing that could be held against you.

!!! tip "Where to start?"

    - What to wear: [clothes](#clothes).

## Clothes

- [ ] **Covering** clothes (long sleeves, trousers) in cotton or sturdy synthetic fabric: gas settles on the skin.[^medic-gestes]
- [ ] Comfortable **closed shoes** for walking and running.
- [ ] No **contact lenses**: gas gets trapped underneath. Wear your glasses, under ski goggles.[^allodocteurs-proteger]
- [ ] Long hair tied back, no dangling jewellery.
- [ ] Avoid **oily** creams, make-up and sunscreen: they hold irritants on the skin.[^medic-gestes]
- [ ] A spare layer in a sealed plastic bag, to change after gas exposure.

## Protection

| Equipment | What it is for | Good to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ski goggles** (preferably) or sealed safety goggles | Protect the eyes from gas, shards and projectiles | Wide, fit over prescription glasses. Foam vents let gas through: choose a model without vents (safety goggles **EN 166 marked "5"**, gas and fine dust) or tape over the vents. Impact-certified protection also carries an impact marking (F, B or A). [^en166][^allodocteurs-proteger] |
| Suction swim goggles | **Avoid if you have better** | They block gas, but they are not protective equipment: no impact certification, small cups pressed on the eye socket, fog up quickly, narrow field of view. [^en166] |
| Sunglasses or glasses alone | Do not protect | Gas gets in around the lenses. [^allodocteurs-proteger] |
| Half mask with **A2P3** or **ABEK1P3** cartridges | Filter particles and vapours of tear gas (CS) | **P3** filters more than 99.95 % of fine particles; the **A/ABEK** part captures vapours. A full-face mask also protects the eyes and leaks less, but it leads more easily to a seizure or to custody (Médic'Action). [^abek][^medicaction-touxtes] |
| **FFP2/FFP3** mask (filtering site mask) | Particles and dust | Does **not** filter vapours: useful, but not enough against dense gas. **Surgical masks are useless** against gas. According to Médic'Action, FFP2 protect rather little and FFP3 rather well. [^abek][^medicaction-gestes][^medicaction-touxtes] |
| Earplugs | Protect hearing from explosive grenades | Foam ones, always in a pocket. |
| Body protection: knee pads, elbow pads, wrist guards (roller type), shin guards | Cushion falls, crushes, blows and projectiles on the limbs | Wear them **under your clothes**, discreet and flexible; street medics use them too. [^se-proteger] |
| Helmet (bike, ski) | Protect the head from falls and projectiles | The most visible item: often debated and may be confiscated at checks. Médic'Action recommends a **light** helmet (ski type), adjustable and comfortable, without an aggressive look: it protects against limited impacts (batons, LBD, falls), not firearms. [^outside][^medicaction-touxtes] |
| Scarf | Last resort against gas | Barely effective; no substitute for a mask. |

!!! warning "Protective gear: not banned, but sometimes confiscated"

    No law bans wearing protection as long as it does not hide your face, but goggles, masks, helmets and saline are frequently seized during searches (or targeted by a prefectoral order on the day). Prefer **discreet protection under your clothes**, and carry **nothing that could pass for a weapon**: see [what the texts say and what to do if gear is confiscated](controle-fouille.md#saline-goggles-mask-can-they-be-confiscated).

!!! warning "Keep your face uncovered until you are exposed"

    Covering your face at a protest **without a legitimate reason** is an offence in France (article 431-9-1 of the Criminal Code: up to 1 year in prison and a €15,000 fine). Courts have accepted protection against tear gas as a legitimate reason, but the police assess it first. **Keep mask and goggles in your hand or around your neck, and put them on only when gas is used.**[^cp-431-9-1][^visage-motif]

## In the bag

- [ ] **ID** (a check without papers can lead to an identity verification of up to 4 hours), some cash, transport ticket.[^sp-controle]
- [ ] **Water**: one bottle to drink, one to rinse.
- [ ] **Saline** single doses (eye rinse), plasters, compresses ([detailed first-aid kit](premiers-secours.md#first-aid-kit)).
- [ ] Sugary snack, personal medication with **a copy of the prescription** (useful if you are arrested).[^medicaction-gestes]
- [ ] If you menstruate: **sanitary pads** rather than tampons, which are considered too risky when worn for a long time.[^medicaction-gestes]
- [ ] **Phone** (charged, power bank, lock code, automatic photo backup to an encrypted service): see the [pre-protest checklist](avant-de-partir.md#phone).[^eff-protest]
- [ ] Paper with useful numbers ([see "Never stay alone"](ne-pas-rester-seul.md)).

## Low budget and mutual aid

Full kit is expensive, and many young people and people who have periods arrive with little protection. The useful minimum and ways to ask for help:

- **Disposable work-site masks are cheap, but protect unevenly.** Médic'Action's orders of magnitude (undated page, indicative prices): FFP1 about €18 for 10 (very limited protection), FFP2 about €22 for 10, FFP3 €10 to €15 for three. They are disposable, rarely last more than one protest and lose effectiveness when wet.[^medicaction-protections] In Strasbourg, street medics quote FFP3 masks at €7 each in 2026.[^ici-strasbourg-street-medics-2026]
- **Swimming goggles: only as a last resort.** Médic'Action considers them unsafe against a hard impact (risk of eye loss, notably from an LBD) and keeps them for the edge of a protest when you have nothing else.[^medicaction-protections]
- **Ask the street medics.** In Reims on 17 October 2026, one said he could supply masks and saline to demonstrators.[^ici-reims-street-medics-2026] In Strasbourg, a group buys FFP3 masks to hand out, funded by a kitty its members fill (over €100 spent since 17 October).[^ici-strasbourg-street-medics-2026] Whether it is free depends on the group and its means: ask politely, and give if you can (see {{ res("medicaction-guides") }} and their [call for supply donations](https://medicaction.org/2026/10/12/appel-a-don-matos-medic-2026/)).
- **Also ask around you in the march** (practical advice, not a rule): saline or a mask is something you can ask for, and many demonstrators carry some. Accept only **new, sealed supplies** (closed single-dose vial, mask in its packaging), never share a mask that has been worn or an opened vial, and bring one or two extra vials to give away.

## Leave at home

- Anything that could be considered a **weapon**: knife, tool, blunt object, firecrackers, even "for self-defence".
- Notebooks, lists or documents with comrades' **names or contacts**.
- A phone holding your whole history: if possible, a secondary phone or a messenger with disappearing messages ([Signal](../annuaire/index.md#signal)).
- Alcohol and drugs: they slow your reflexes and can make an arrest worse.
