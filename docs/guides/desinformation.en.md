# Avoid disinformation

During a mobilization, false information spreads fast: old photos recycled, invented tolls, rumours of bans or dissolutions. It comes from every side, institutions included. Sharing false information weakens the movement and can put people in danger.

## The 4-step method (SIFT)

A method proposed by researcher Mike Caulfield.[^sift]

1. **Stop**: do you feel anger or urgency? That is the moment to slow down before sharing.
2. **Investigate the source**: who is publishing? An identified outlet, an account created last week, a screenshot with no link?
3. **Find better coverage**: is the information reported by reliable outlets or fact-checking services?
4. **Trace the origin**: go back to the original post, with its date and place.

## Check an image or a video

- **Reverse image search**: drop the image into an image search engine (TinEye, Google Lens…) to see whether it has circulated before, when and where.[^afp-factuel]
- Look for **clues about place and date**: shop signs, number plates, weather, language on signs, season.
- Be wary of **cut** videos: look for a longer version or another angle.
- A "too perfect" image may be AI-generated: inconsistent hands, text, reflections.

## Before sharing

- No **tolls** (injured, dead, arrests) without an identified source.
- No protesters' **names or faces**: you could expose them to prosecution or harassment.[^eff-protest]
- If you got it wrong: **correct publicly** and delete.
- Share the link to a **fact-check** rather than the rumour, even to debunk it.
