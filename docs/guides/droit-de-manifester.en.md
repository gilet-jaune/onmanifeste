# The right to protest

!!! abstract "Key points"

    - Protesting is a **freedom**: you **declare** a demonstration, you don't ask for permission.
    - Taking part in an **undeclared** demonstration is not an offence; the risks fall on the organisers.
    - A ban must be **justified case by case** and can be challenged in an emergency hearing (référé-liberté).
    - **Banned** demonstration: **€135** fine, no police custody for that fine alone.

In France, demonstrating is a **freedom**, not a permission you ask for. The State can regulate it and, in specific cases, ban it, but under a judge's review.

## What the law says

- The Constitution does not use the word "demonstration", but the Constitutional Council protects the **"right to the collective expression of ideas and opinions"**, linked to the freedom of expression in the 1789 Declaration of the Rights of Man.[^cc-94-352]
- The European Convention on Human Rights protects **freedom of peaceful assembly** (Article 11).[^cedh-convention]
- This right can be limited to protect public order, but restrictions must be **adapted, necessary and proportionate**. In 2019, the Constitutional Council struck down the law that let the prefect ban an individual from demonstrating, finding that power too broad.[^cc-2019-780]

## Declaring is not asking permission

- **Organisers** declare the demonstration to the town hall or the prefecture **3 to 15 full days** beforehand: purpose, date, time, route.[^sp-declarer-manifestation]
- Taking part in an **undeclared** demonstration is not an offence for demonstrators.[^ldh-manif-interdite] It is the organisers who risk up to 6 months in prison and a €7,500 fine for an undeclared or banned demonstration.[^sp-declarer-manifestation]

## When a ban is lawful

- The mayor or the prefect can ban a demonstration by **order** if they consider it likely to disturb public order.[^sp-declarer-manifestation]
- They must justify it **case by case**, according to the real risk: they cannot ban it solely because of the cause it defends, nor merely on the minister's instruction.[^ce-palestine-2023]
- In October 2026, after the 17 October mobilisation, the President of the Republic announced plans to ban any gathering not organised by a union or called by people "without legitimacy", while curfews and blanket bans were issued in many cities. The Human Rights League (LDH) recalled that reserving the right to protest to unions alone is unlawful, and Amnesty International denounced a "security escalation".[^franceinfo-ldh-syndicats-2026][^ldh-interdictions-24-octobre-2026][^amnesty-surenchere-2026]
- [ ] Before you go, check whether there is a ban order: the prefecture's website and social media, the official bulletin of administrative acts.[^ldh-manif-interdite]

## If the demonstration is banned

- **Taking part**: a petty offence, fixed fine of **€135** (up to €750 before a court). No arrest or police custody for this offence alone.[^ldh-manif-interdite]
- **Contesting the fine**: possible, by arguing the ban order was unlawful.[^ldh-manif-interdite]
- **Contesting the ban itself**: organisers, associations or unions can go to the administrative court with an emergency **"référé-liberté"**. The judge rules within **48 hours** if it is urgent and there is a serious and manifestly unlawful breach of a fundamental freedom.[^justice-refere-liberte]
