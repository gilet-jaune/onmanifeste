# Signal groups: vetting

[:octicons-arrow-left-24: Signal groups: overview](groupes-signal.md)

Terms used here: [the groups](groupes-signal.md#the-groups) (public, airlock, trusted), [the roles](groupes-signal.md#the-roles) (HR, keyholder…) and the [vocabulary](groupes-signal.md#key-vocabulary-and-concepts).

## Validating: graduated trust

The meeting is a **first contact**. Validation then depends on the group's level:

**What does "vetted" mean?** Three checks, each answering a different question:

| What is checked | Question | Who, how | Time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The account** | Is this Signal account really that of the person in front of me? | The secretary scans the **safety-number QR code**: the account is marked "verified", and nobody can pose as them with another account[^eff-signal] | 10 seconds |
| **The person** | Does someone reliable really know them? | A **voucher** who knows them outside the movement, or has seen them act at several actions, confirms it privately to the secretary | Immediate if someone knows them; a few weeks otherwise |
| **The settings** | Might they expose the group through carelessness? | The secretary, then HR: activist name, no photo, hidden number, no duplicate name | 5 minutes |

- **Fast path**, when someone already knows them: meeting in the airlock group (15 minutes of settings and QR scan), the voucher's confirmation, cleaning the airlock group, HR's second check, the trusted group's link. **The same day.**
- **Slow path**, when nobody knows them: they come to actions for a few weeks. This time can't be shortened: it is the only way to get to know someone.
- **What vetting doesn't guarantee**: intentions. A malicious person with a genuine voucher will get through; hence "nothing illegal in writing" and compartmentalising into sub-groups.

| To join | You need |
| --- | --- |
| **The trusted group** (nothing sensitive is written there) | A **vetted secretary** who has met the person, set up their settings with them and scanned their account's QR code, **one voucher**, then the **second check by HR**, who add them |
| **A sensitive sub-group** (medics, legal, logistics…) | **Two vouchers** who really know the person, after a period in the trusted group; the sub-group's keyholder adds them |

- [ ] **Vouchers don't need to be in the airlock group**: they are vetted members who know the person **elsewhere** (friend, colleague, neighbour, union comrade). They confirm it **privately** to the secretary.
- [ ] **Nobody knows them?** They come to public actions for a few weeks; vetted members who have seen them in action and talked with them can then vouch for them.
- [ ] **Met at a protest, even as a medic?** That is not yet knowing them: anyone can wear a medic armband or a legal observer vest, and these teams' sub-groups are sensitive. No direct adding to a trusted group. However, having seen them act at **several** actions and talked with them means you can vouch for them; and if they come from a known collective (street medics, legal team), a vetted member who knows that collective can be their voucher.
- [ ] **A very active person?** A vetted member who has acted alongside them at two or three actions **proposes** them to a secretary and becomes their voucher. The secretary meets them, on the spot during an action if needed (scanning a QR code takes 10 seconds), then HR add them: the same day or the next. Unions, associations and collectives help too: a vetted member who knows them through their union or collective can be their voucher straight away.
- [ ] **Newcomers never vouch for each other**: several malicious people who arrived together would validate each other. No votes either, nor any threshold of "yes" votes.
- [ ] **A single serious doubt is enough to wait**: the secretary raises it with HR, who settle it.
- [ ] **Signal warns when two members of a group have the same name**: check it, it may be impersonation.[^signal-confidentialite-numero]
- [ ] **Never ask** for ID, photos, address or employer: it creates a dangerous file if seized, and proves nothing.
- [ ] **Warning signs**: someone who pushes for illegal or violent action, asks for names, addresses or the list of groups, or wants to get into everything straight away; a secretary validating many people at once; a voucher who keeps coming up.
- [ ] **No trust list.** A list of "who has met whom", on paper, in a Signal group or in an online voting tool, **draws the map of your network**: one seized phone or the tool's server is enough to hand it over in full. Nothing is passed on in writing; the register never records who vouched for whom.
- [ ] **At the very start**, in a city with no vetted members yet, the first core is a group of people who already know each other in person: they are the first vetted members, secretaries and vouchers.
