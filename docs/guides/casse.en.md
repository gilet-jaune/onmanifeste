# Why property gets smashed at protests

!!! abstract "Key points"

    - Property damage comes mostly from a **minority** targeting **symbols** (banks, fast-food chains).
    - "A riot is the **language of the unheard**" (Martin Luther King), who also called riots self-defeating.
    - **Indiscriminate** policing unites and radicalises the crowd.
    - Studies describe a **dilemma**: more visibility, but less support.
    - Damage: up to **2 years and €30,000**, **5 years and €75,000** in a group or with a hidden face.

Broken shop windows, bus shelters, burnt cars: at every major mobilisation, property damage makes the headlines and is used to discredit a whole movement. This guide does not call for smashing anything. Using research, it explains **who does it, why, what it changes for a cause** and **what you risk**.

## A minority, chosen targets

- In marches, property damage comes mostly from an **organised minority**, often called the "black bloc": activists dressed in black and hooded, at the front of the march. Born in Germany in the 1980s, the practice reached France in the late 1990s.[^publicsenat-black-blocs-2018]
- Targets are not random: **banks, fast-food chains, car dealerships**, symbols of capitalism. Historian Sylvain Boulouque calls it "purely symbolic" violence.[^publicsenat-black-blocs-2018]
- According to him, this milieu numbers around **10,000 people** in France, a marginal share of society.[^publicsenat-black-blocs-2018]

## "The language of the unheard"

- In 1966, Martin Luther King put it this way: "a riot is the language of the unheard". In the same interview, he hoped riots could be avoided because they are "self-defeating and socially destructive".[^cbs-king-1966] He was not justifying them: he was asking what society had refused to hear.
- Many yellow vests and high-school students share this feeling of not being listened to: a shared-initiative referendum that cannot be triggered, grievance books left in a drawer, repeated use of Article 49.3. See [Democratic channels flouted](../confiance/index.md).

## When repression raises the tension

- Crowd research shows that policing **perceived as illegitimate and indiscriminate** (violent dispersal, kettling everyone) **unites the crowd and radicalises it**: people who came peacefully become willing to resist the police.[^sussex-esim]
- In the United Kingdom, this work led the police to favour dialogue and to **differentiate** between people instead of treating the crowd as one block.[^sussex-esim]
- See [Documented repression and disinformation](../repressions/index.md).

## Does it work? What the studies say

There is no simple answer: studies describe a **dilemma**.

- **More visibility, less support.** In a series of studies, actions seen as extreme drew more attention to a cause but lowered public support, including among sympathisers, because they were perceived as immoral.[^feinberg-2020-dilemme]
- **In the United States in the 1960s**, nonviolent protests, especially when repressed, moved opinion and votes towards civil rights. Violence started by protesters instead turned the media and politicians towards "law and order", and probably tipped the 1968 presidential election.[^wasow-2020]
- **The "radical flank" effect.** A group using radical tactics can make moderate groups in the same movement more acceptable, by contrast. The effect was measured experimentally, and it comes mostly from tactics, not demands.[^simpson-2022-flanc-radical]
- **Over the long run**, from 1900 to 2006, major nonviolent resistance campaigns succeeded more than **twice as often** as violent ones. These were mostly campaigns to overthrow a regime or win independence.[^chenoweth-wcrw]

### And the yellow vests?

- On 1 December 2018, the Arc de Triomphe was ransacked: a model smashed to pieces, a bust of Napoleon beheaded, hundreds of thousands of euros of damage.[^tendanceouest-arc-triomphe-2018]
- On 10 December, Emmanuel Macron announced €100 more per month at minimum-wage level, overtime free of tax and contributions, and the cancellation of the CSG increase for pensioners under €2,000 a month, while condemning "inadmissible" violence.[^publicsenat-macron-10-decembre-2018]
- Many saw this as proof that "only smashing pays". It cannot be proven: property damage was only one part of a much broader movement (roundabouts, blockades, demonstrations every Saturday).
- The backlash: in 2019, the so-called "anti-rioters" law tried to let the prefect ban an individual from demonstrating. The Constitutional Council struck that measure down.[^cc-2019-780]
- In October 2026, the same pattern repeated after the 17 October mobilisation (550,000 to 800,000 people): rolling news channels devoted a disproportionate share of airtime to property damage and looting—so much so that the media regulator Arcom wrote to them calling for "the utmost vigilance"—and the executive used the unrest to impose curfews, ban gatherings not called by a union, deploy Sentinelle soldiers and prepare with telecom operators to block TikTok, Instagram, Snapchat and X.[^wiki-17-octobre-2026][^wiki-24-octobre-2026][^amnesty-surenchere-2026][^blast-coupure-reseaux-2026] That is also why many groups seek to **move beyond city-centre stand-offs** by multiplying local meeting points (roundabouts, markets, assemblies) and strike support ([organising locally](organiser.md#organising-locally-beyond-city-centres)).

## What you risk

- Destroying or damaging someone else's property: up to **2 years in prison and a €30,000 fine**.[^sp-vandalisme]
- **5 years and €75,000** if committed by several people, or by someone deliberately hiding their face.[^sp-vandalisme]
- The victim can also claim compensation for the damage.[^sp-vandalisme]
- **Knowingly** joining a group, even a temporary one, that prepares violence or damage: **1 year and €15,000**, even without breaking anything. The prosecution must prove intent and material acts of preparation.[^senat-222-14-2]
- Covering your face without a legitimate reason at a demonstration: up to **1 year and €15,000**.[^cp-431-9-1]
