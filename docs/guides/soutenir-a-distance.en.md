# Supporting without going to protests

!!! abstract "Key points"

    - Many people cannot protest (work, health, children, papers, fear): **helping from afar really counts**.
    - **Money**: prefer a **solidarity fund** to a personal transfer.
    - **Supplies**: ask street medics **what they need** first.
    - **Presence**: be the person who picks up, alerts the legal team or meets someone leaving custody.
    - **Voice**: relay online, vote, sign petitions.

## Give to a solidarity fund

A solidarity or anti-repression fund helps people being prosecuted (lawyers' fees, fines, travel) and workers on strike.

- Find the fund **for your city or movement**: {{ res("caisse-soutien-paris-idf", "Paris–Île-de-France") }}, {{ res("caisse-solidarite-lyon", "Lyon") }}, or the {{ res("caisse-solidarite", "national solidarity fund") }} for strikers. The list of {{ res("rajcol", "local collectives") }} helps find the others.
- Prefer **a fund to a transfer to a person**: gifts of money between individuals are subject to gift duties and the recipient must declare them.[^sp-don-argent]
- **Check the origin**: go through the fund's official site, not a link received in a message or an anonymous crowdfunding page.
- A card payment or transfer leaves a trace in your name: look at the payment methods offered before giving.
- Even small, a **regular** gift lets the fund commit to lawyers' fees.

## Equip street medics

Street-medic collectives run on donated supplies. Médic'Action says it can receive **consumable first-aid supplies** (compresses, gloves…) and invites people to contact it for that.[^medicaction-a-propos] Its 2026 appeal lists, by price range, **FFP2 or FFP3** masks, survival blankets, compresses, **saline solution**, dressings, cold packs and hand sanitiser.[^medicaction-appel-matos]

- **Ask before you buy**: contact the street-medic collective in your city (or {{ res("medicaction-guides", "Médic'Action") }}, whose "About" page gives a contact) and ask what is missing. An appeal may be over, and stock quickly becomes redundant.
- **New, sealed and in date**: saline in single doses, sterile compresses, masks in their packaging. Check expiry dates.
- For masks, see the [protest kit](kit-manif.md): surgical masks are useless against gas.[^abek]
- Do not hand supplies to someone you do not know: go through the collective.
- Nothing to donate? **Relay** the appeal to your circle, or offer a room, a lift, or storage.

## Relay online

Sharing, commenting, saving and liking the content you want to see circulate (independent media, collectives, testimonies) is a real lever, within reach of anyone who cannot go out. Relaying a **notified** protest (date, place, organisers) carries no risk. The gestures that count, and those to avoid (do not "share" far-right content, do not publish names or faces) are in [Social media: understanding the algorithm](reseaux-sociaux.md#push-up-what-matters).

## Vote

Voting remains a tool, even though it does not replace the street.

- **Register on the electoral rolls**: possible all year round, online with proof of identity and proof of address under 3 months old, or at the town hall.[^sp-inscription-electorale] For an election, the general rule is to register no later than the **6th Friday before the first round**; the deadline is the 10th day before the first round in some situations (young person turning 18, recent move, recently acquired French nationality…).[^sp-inscription-delais]
- **Vote by proxy** if you cannot travel that day: anyone who is absent on voting day may give a proxy, with no proof of absence. The request is made online, then in person at a police station or gendarmerie. **If a disability or serious illness prevents you from travelling**, you can ask in writing, with a sworn statement, for an officer to come to your home (or hospital, care home…) to check your identity. At most one proxy made in France can be entrusted to the same person on voting day.[^sp-procuration]
- **If you do not have French nationality**: a citizen of another EU country who lives in France, is 18 and enjoys civil and political rights may vote, but **only in municipal and European elections**, through a supplementary electoral list.[^sp-vote-citoyen-europeen] For other foreign nationals, we found no voting right in the pages we read: ask your town hall before doing anything.

## Sign petitions

Petitions are a discreet way to weigh in without leaving home. They rarely have a direct effect: at the National Assembly, beyond 100,000 signatures they are put online and the committee decides to dismiss or examine them; a public debate is possible beyond 500,000 signatories in at least 30 départements or overseas territories.[^petitions-assemblee] At the Senate, a petition that reaches 100,000 signatures within 6 months is passed to the Conference of Presidents, which may decide to act on it.[^petitions-senat]

- **On the official National Assembly and Senate sites**, you must identify yourself with FranceConnect, but signatories **remain anonymous**: only the authors' names are published.[^petitions-assemblee-cgu][^petitions-senat-cgu]
- **Who may sign at the National Assembly**: adults who are French nationals **or lawfully resident in France**.[^petitions-assemblee-cgu] The conditions we read do not mention the electoral rolls. At the Senate, no nationality or residence condition is stated: you must be on the national register of individuals and have a FranceConnect account.[^petitions-senat-cgu]
- **FranceConnect** requires being listed in that register (which implies a **definitive** social security number), being over 15, and holding an account in your own name with a partner (impots.gouv.fr, ameli…). The pages we read say nothing about people without a residence permit.[^franceconnect-conditions] The petition site then receives your surname, first names, sex, date, place and country of birth, and your email address.[^franceconnect-donnees]
- If you are a foreign national without a residence permit, **check before signing**: signing means identifying yourself through FranceConnect.
- **Private platforms work differently.** Read the privacy policy before signing:
    - **Change.org**: by default, your name and location may be shown publicly with the petition. To avoid appearing, untick "Show my name on the petition"; a signature already made must be withdrawn and redone. Even in anonymous mode, the petition starter receives your name, city and/or postal code and the date you signed. Your signature is kept until you ask for it to be deleted. The platform may also work with advertising partners.[^change-confidentialite][^change-signature-anonyme]
    - **Avaaz**: signatories' name, city, country and comment are passed to the campaign's target or to the author of a citizen petition; the page offers no anonymity option (its only answer: do not sign). Avaaz says it neither sells nor rents your data, and generally keeps it for 7 years.[^avaaz-confidentialite]
    - **MesOpinions** (the site redirects to MyPetition): the petition author receives, depending on the case, the date and time of signing, your surname, first name, postal code, city and country, but not your email. Data may go to relevant associations or NGOs if you expressly consented. The policy does not say whether your name is shown publicly. Hosted in Paris (AWS).[^mypetition-confidentialite]
    - **Other platforms**: we have not read their policy. Assume a name and an email address are collected.
    - **In every case**, a private petition has no official standing: it applies pressure, it does not refer a matter to an assembly. Avoid them if your administrative situation is fragile.

## Be the contact person outside

The guides advise alerting **someone who is not at the protest**: that can be you, if you offer.[^legal-team]

- **Answer withheld or unknown numbers**: the police station often calls that way.
- Keep the person's **full name, date and place of birth**: the legal team needs them to locate the person ([Never stay alone](ne-pas-rester-seul.md)).
- If they do not check in on time: call the **legal team**, not the police.
- Plan a **welcome at the exit of custody**: water, food, a charged phone, a place to sleep (see [Custody](garde-a-vue.md)). Release can happen at any hour.

## Help in other ways

- **Relay** calls from legal teams and funds, without sharing names or photos of people ([film and publish](filmer.md)).
- **Look after** children, a pet, a home for someone who protests or leaves custody.
- **Persuade** your circle, calmly ([convince your relatives](convaincre-ses-proches.md)).
- **Learn** first-aid basics, to use in daily life as in a march ([first aid](premiers-secours.md)).
