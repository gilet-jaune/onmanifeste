# What the law forbids at a protest, and what you risk

!!! abstract "The essentials"

    - It is almost always the **use** or the **intended purpose** of an object that is punished, not the object itself: carrying a weapon, throwing, aiming, hiding your face, staying after the summonses.
    - **Staying in a gathering after the summonses**: 1 year and €15,000; **3 years and €45,000** if you hide your face so as not to be identified.[^cp-431-4]
    - **Taking part in a protest while carrying a weapon**: 3 years and €45,000. An object is a weapon only if it is used or intended to kill, wound or threaten.[^cp-431-10][^cp-132-75]
    - **Violence against a police officer or gendarme**, even without any incapacity for work: 3 years and €45,000.[^cp-222-13]
    - This guide gives **legal information**, not advice or encouragement. The penalties are **maximums**. It describes no equipment and no method.

Knowing what is punished avoids finding yourself in custody without knowing it. This guide does not say what to do at a protest (see [The right to protest](droit-de-manifester.md) and [Dispersal, warnings and kettling](dispersion.md)): it summarises what the Criminal Code provides. A judge assesses each situation: this table is not a prediction.

!!! warning "Not legal advice"

    This information does not replace a lawyer. Arrested? See [Police custody (garde à vue)](garde-a-vue.md).

## The most common offences

| What is punished | What the text says | Maximum penalty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Staying in a gathering after the summonses** | A gathering is any assembly of people on the public road or in a public place likely to disturb public order; it can be dispersed after two summonses that had no effect.[^cp-431-3] Voluntarily continuing to take part after the summonses, without carrying a weapon, is an offence.[^cp-431-4] | 1 year and €15,000; 3 years and €45,000 if the face is hidden so as not to be identified |
| **Taking part in a gathering while carrying a weapon** | Same basis, with a weapon.[^cp-431-5] | 3 years and €45,000; 5 years and €75,000 if you continue after the summonses; more still if the face is hidden |
| **Taking part in a protest or public meeting while carrying a weapon** | No summonses are needed.[^cp-431-10] | 3 years and €45,000 |
| **Hiding your face** | Voluntarily hiding all or part of your face, without a legitimate reason, within or right next to a protest on the public road during or after which public-order disturbances are committed or likely to be.[^cp-431-9-1] | 1 year and €15,000 |
| **Taking part in a group preparing violence or damage** | Knowingly taking part in a group, even a temporary one, formed to prepare, through one or more material acts, deliberate violence against people or destruction or damage of property.[^cp-222-14-2] | 1 year and €15,000 |
| **Violence against a person holding public authority** | Violence causing no more than eight days' incapacity for work, or none, against a person holding public authority (police officer, gendarme).[^cp-222-13] | 3 years and €45,000 |
| **Contempt (outrage)** | Words, gestures or threats, writings or images not made public, or sending objects addressed to a person holding public authority in the course of or because of their duties.[^cp-433-5] | 1 year and €15,000 |
| **Resisting officials (rébellion)** | Violent resistance to a person holding public authority acting to enforce the law or an order of the authorities.[^cp-433-6] | 2 years and €30,000; 3 years and €45,000 if committed by a group[^cp-433-7] |
| **Damage** | Destroying, defacing or damaging another person's property; marking inscriptions, signs or drawings without permission on facades, vehicles, public roads or street furniture.[^cp-322-1] | 2 years and €30,000, unless the damage is slight; €3,750 and community service for inscriptions when the damage is slight |
| **Organising an undeclared or banned protest** | Organising a protest on the public road without prior declaration, or despite a ban, or having filed an incomplete declaration.[^cp-431-9] | 6 months and €7,500 |

## The principle: use and intended purpose

A weapon is any object designed to kill or wound. Any other object that may present a danger to people is **treated as** a weapon when it is **used** to kill, wound or threaten, or when its carrier **intends** it to kill, wound or threaten.[^cp-132-75]

An ordinary object in a bag does not become a weapon by itself: its use and the purpose its carrier gives it are what count, and judges assess case by case. The text lists no allowed or forbidden objects.

## A few cases people talk about

The Criminal Code names few of these objects (only explosive or incendiary substances are covered); the classification depends on what is done with them. These are illustrations, not certain classifications.

- **A laser aimed at people**, in particular at the eyes or at officers: the object may be seen as used to wound or threaten, therefore treated as a weapon, and the act may be prosecuted as deliberate violence.[^cp-132-75][^cp-222-13]
- **Dirty matter or other substances thrown at people** (what is called a "cacatov", or balloons filled with paint or another liquid): throwing at people may be prosecuted as violence, and sending objects addressed to an officer falls under contempt.[^cp-222-13][^cp-433-5] If it is property that is targeted (a facade, a vehicle), it is damage.[^cp-322-1]
- **So-called "reinforced" equipment**: helmets, goggles and masks are ordinary protection (see [Protest kit and outfit](kit-manif.md)). Rigid or reinforced equipment that you intend to hit, wound or threaten with, or use that way, may be treated as a weapon,[^cp-132-75] which changes the penalty for carrying a weapon at a protest.[^cp-431-10]
- **An incendiary device** (what is called a "Molotov cocktail"): this is one of the heaviest cases. Destroying or damaging someone's property by an explosive substance, fire or any means likely to endanger people is punished by **10 years and €150,000**, more if people are injured.[^cp-322-6] Merely **possessing or transporting** incendiary or explosive products in order to prepare such acts or attacks on people is punished by **7 years and €100,000**.[^cp-322-11-1]
- **Throwing a tear-gas grenade back** at law enforcement: no article targets this act. It is analysed according to what it causes and the object: throwing an object at officers can be prosecuted as violence against a person holding public authority.[^cp-222-13] An object used to wound or threaten can be treated as a weapon (art. 132-75),[^cp-132-75] which links to carrying a weapon at a demonstration.[^cp-431-10] Grenades are also classed among "war material" (category A2) in the Internal Security Code: this administrative classification alone does not fix the criminal classification.[^csi-r311-2] The judge assesses each situation.
