# Getting there and back

!!! abstract "Key points"

    - Plan the way there **and two routes home**, with an offline map.
    - Get off **one or two stops before** the start of the march, park far from the route.
    - Leave **before the end** if tension rises, and **as soon as warnings are given**.
    - Don't go home **alone**, and send a "home safe" message.

The journey matters as much as the march: plan the way there **and back**, with a plan B if transport closes.

## Before you go

- [ ] Prepare **two routes home**, on foot to stations away from the route.
- [ ] Download an **offline map** ([Organic Maps](../annuaire/index.md#organic-maps)): the mobile network often saturates on site. Tutorials for each app in the [pre-protest checklist](avant-de-partir.md#offline-map).
- [ ] Check **traffic information** the same morning: stations can be closed at the prefecture's request, and the list changes until the last minute.[^ratp][^fermeture] Traffic info and bike sharing in big cities (TCL, Vélo'v, RTM, Tisséo, TBM…): [directory "traffic info"](../annuaire/index.md?tags=info-trafic) · [bikes](../annuaire/index.md?tags=velo).
- [ ] Carry a **ticket** and some cash; the phone is covered in the [checklist](avant-de-partir.md#phone).
- [ ] Set a **meeting point** with your group, away from the route ([never stay alone](ne-pas-rester-seul.md)).

## Getting there

### Public transport

- Get off **one or two stations before** the start of the march: the nearest are often closed or packed.[^ratp]

### Car-sharing

- Organise it with **trusted** people (collective, union, friends), and share the journey and expected return time with a contact who is not protesting.
- Park **far from the route**, in a street you can reach on foot even if the area is cordoned off.

### Bike and bike-share

- Lock your bike **away from the route**, with a good lock.
- With a bike-share (Vélo'v, Vélib'…), **return it before** joining the march; check in the app that a dock is free near your starting point.
- Avoid pushing a bike through a dense crowd: it gets in the way of a quick evacuation.

### On foot

- Comfortable closed shoes, water, and a clear idea of the exit streets ([kit and outfit](kit-manif.md)).

## Getting back

- **Leave before the end** if tension rises: dispersals often happen at the tail of the march.
- At the **warnings**, leave immediately by a clear street ([dispersal and warnings](dispersion.md)).
- If your station is closed, **walk** to the next one rather than waiting there.[^fermeture]
- Don't go home **alone**, especially at night; send a "home safe" message to your contact.
- If you were tear-gassed, change your outer layer before taking public transport ([first aid](premiers-secours.md)).

### Back home: decontamination and care

If you were exposed to tear gas:[^medic-soins]

- [ ] **Take off clothes from the bottom down**: unbutton or pull down; avoid pulling jumpers or T-shirts over your head to keep from rubbing gas crystals across your eyes or face.
- [ ] **Cold shower first**: warm or hot water dilates skin pores and reactivates the CS gas burning sensation. Rinse thoroughly with cool water for several minutes without scrubbing before using soap.
- [ ] **Wash clothes separately**: machine-wash them on their own, away from the rest of your laundry, to avoid contaminating other clothes.
