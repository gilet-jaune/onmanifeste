## Who owns the media and how to check information

- **Media ownership and ties with billionaires**: the chart {{ res("medias-qui-possede-quoi", "“French media: who owns what?”") }} (_Le Monde diplomatique_ & {{ res("acrimed", "Acrimed") }}) maps which industrial conglomerates (luxury goods, construction, defence, telecoms, shipping) own major newspapers, TV channels and radio stations.
- **Media criticism and counter-investigations**: {{ res("acrimed", "Acrimed") }} and {{ res("arret-sur-images") }} analyze media coverage of strikes and protests; {{ res("index-investigation", "INDEX") }} (3D reconstructions and video forensics), {{ res("mediapart") }}, {{ res("disclose") }}, {{ res("streetpress") }} and {{ res("basta") }} conduct independent investigations.
- **Fact-checking desks**: {{ res("afp-factuel") }}, {{ res("checknews") }}, {{ res("les-decodeurs") }} and {{ res("vrai-ou-fake") }}.
- **Journalism ethics and regulatory complaints**: anyone can file a free complaint with the {{ res("cdjm", "CDJM") }} (print, online and broadcast journalism ethics) or {{ res("arcom", "Arcom") }} (TV and radio regulator) over breaches of accuracy or fairness.

## Public budget, debt, corporate subsidies and tax fraud

- **Public budget and tax breakdown**: {{ res("fipeco", "FIPECO") }} (former Court of Audit magistrates and INSEE statisticians) explains in plain-language sheets where public revenue comes from (the weight of VAT compared with income and corporate tax) and where spending goes.
- **Unconditional corporate subsidies (CICE, R&D tax credit, social-contribution cuts)**: the reference academic study by {{ res("ires-capitalisme-perfusion", "IRES (“Capitalism on life support”)") }} catalogues all public subsidies to businesses (~€157bn in 2019, the largest budget item) and the lack of social or environmental conditions[^ires-capitalisme-perfusion], while the {{ res("observatoire-multinationales", "Observatoire des multinationales") }} tracks dividends and layoffs at major recipient corporations.
- **Tax fraud, tax havens vs benefit fraud and non-take-up**: the {{ res("eu-tax-observatory", "EU Tax Observatory") }} and the {{ res("stop-paradis-fiscaux", "Plateforme Paradis Fiscaux et Judiciaires") }} quantify tax evasion by multinationals and ultra-high-net-worth individuals. On official orders of magnitude, _Vie publique_'s overview notes that tax fraud (and undeclared work by employers) far exceeds social-benefit fraud, which itself is smaller than **non-take-up** of social rights (eligible people not claiming RSA or activity bonuses).[^vie-publique-fraudes]
- **Inequality and wealth distribution**: {{ res("observatoire-inegalites", "Observatoire des inégalités") }} and {{ res("wid-world", "WID.world") }}.

## Monitoring elected officials: votes and court convictions

- **Votes and parliamentary participation**: {{ res("datan") }} and {{ res("poligraph") }} (French Parliament) as well as {{ res("howtheyvote") }} (European Parliament) publish detailed voting records and roll-call participation rates (which only measure plenary roll-call votes, not committee or constituency work).
- **Court convictions and public integrity** (corruption, embezzlement of public funds, fictitious jobs, sexual violence…): tracked by {{ res("anticor") }}, {{ res("mediapart") }}, {{ res("poligraph") }} and {{ res("casier-politique") }} (the last two rely on AI extraction from Wikipedia, Wikidata or the press: check each record against its source). The only official statistics, without names, come from the French Anti-Corruption Agency.[^afa-probite-stats]
