# Benefits and resources of elected officials: what the texts say

"They are paid a lot, housed, driven around…": part of the distrust towards elected officials comes from what people believe they know about their benefits. This page gathers what **official documents** say, with their dates, and shows where information is not public.

!!! info "What this page covers"

    Deputies (official National Assembly sheet, January 2026), senators (official Senate brochure, October 2026), former Prime Ministers, former Presidents of the Republic, and the general framework for ministers and local elected officials. It does not give the amounts for ministers or MEPs: we have not read an accessible official source for them. A mistake, something missing? [Report it]({{ repositories.docs.url }}/issues/new/choose).

## A deputy: allowance and resources (January 2026)

| Item | Amount or rule |
| --- | --- |
| Parliamentary allowance | €7,637.39 gross per month, or €5,953.34 net, taxed as a salary[^an-fiche-7-situation-materielle-depute-2026] |
| Parliamentary operating grant (DFP) | €7,238.04 per month for a deputy elected in mainland France, since 1 January 2026: it merges the mandate expense advance and the equipment grant and covers mandate-related expenses that the Assembly does not pay directly[^an-fiche-7-situation-materielle-depute-2026] |
| Staff credit | €11,463 per month to hire up to five staff; the deputy is the employer, and the Assembly pays salaries and charges directly[^an-fiche-7-situation-materielle-depute-2026] |
| Transport | Train travel covered; annual credit of flights (for example 80 trips between Paris and the constituency where it is served by a regular air route, for mainland deputies)[^an-fiche-7-situation-materielle-depute-2026] |
| Housing in Paris | A room at the Assembly's residence, or hotel costs refunded up to €200 a night, or €1,200 a month for a rented pied-à-terre (housing grant)[^an-fiche-7-situation-materielle-depute-2026] |
| Childcare | €323.76 a month for children under three[^an-fiche-7-situation-materielle-depute-2026] |
| After the mandate | Return-to-work allowance equal to 57% of the allowance (€4,353.31 gross a month), for 18 months in principle (up to 27 months depending on age), funded by a 1% contribution from serving deputies[^an-fiche-7-situation-materielle-depute-2026] |
| Pension | Pension from age 62 and 3 months (September 2023), with the age gradually raised to 64; contribution rate of 10.85%; scheme reformed in 2018[^an-fiche-7-situation-materielle-depute-2026] |

!!! warning "Don't add everything up"

    The DFP and the staff credit are not income: they are used to cover mandate expenses and to pay staff. Adding up every line to announce "what a deputy earns" is misleading. The allowance, by contrast, is taxable income.

## A senator (October 2026)

| Item | Amount or rule |
| --- | --- |
| Parliamentary allowance | €5,931.95 gross per month, plus a residence allowance of €177.96 and a function allowance of €1,527.48, or €7,637.39 gross: the same total as for a deputy; the base and residence allowances are taxable[^senat-brochure-le-senat-aujourdhui-2026] |
| Staff credit | €8,827.40 per month for the gross base pay of the team, up to five employees[^senat-brochure-le-senat-aujourdhui-2026] |
| Mandate expenses | Since 1 January 2018, a dual system: direct coverage (transport, postage, Paris taxis, telephony) and advances paid to senators (€6,600 a month for the general advance); all expenses must be justified and reconciled with the advances, the balance being returned to the Senate at year end[^senat-brochure-le-senat-aujourdhui-2026] |
| Transport | Public-transport travel between Paris and the département covered[^senat-brochure-le-senat-aujourdhui-2026] |
| Holding other mandates | Allowances from other elected mandates can be added to the parliamentary allowance only up to one and a half times its basic gross amount[^senat-brochure-le-senat-aujourdhui-2026] |

### Oversight and transparency of expenses

According to the association Transparence Citoyenne, since the abolition of the old flat-rate allowance in 2018, parliamentarians have had to justify their expenses to their assembly's ethics officer, but those supporting documents remain inaccessible to the public. It says it asked the presidents of both assemblies for them on 20 May 2026 and runs a petition.[^transparence-citoyenne-petition-frais-mandat] These are its claims: we have not read the replies of the presidents of the two assemblies and attribute no statement to them. The Senate, for its part, states in its brochure that its senators must justify their mandate expenses under the Act of 15 September 2017.[^senat-brochure-le-senat-aujourdhui-2026]

## Ministers: the framework, without the amounts

The pay of members of the Government is set by decree no. 2012-983 of 23 August 2012. A written question by a deputy, published on 3 November 2020, asked for all the components of ministers' pay (salary, residence allowance, function allowance, family supplement, special-duty allowances, contributions); it was closed on 21 June 2022, at the end of the parliamentary term, with no reply published on its page.[^an-question-33559-remuneration-ministres] We therefore give no amount: the figures in circulation come from third-party sites that we could not reconcile with the official text.

## Local elected officials

According to the Directorate-General for Local Authorities, holding a local mandate is in principle unpaid. The function allowance is not a salary: it is compensation provided for by law, conditional on effective exercise of duties, which cannot exceed a ceiling set by category of mandate according to the population of the authority, expressed as a percentage of the civil-service terminal gross index.[^dgcl-indemnite-de-fonction-elus-locaux] The table of ceilings in euros is published on the Directorate's page; we do not reproduce it so as not to freeze amounts that are revised.

## Former Prime Ministers: the end of "lifetime" benefits

The decree of 16 September 2025 limits to ten years, from the end of their duties, the resources paid by the State for former Prime Ministers, and ends them by 1 January 2026 at the latest for those whose duties ended more than ten years ago.[^legifrance-decret-2025-965-anciens-pm] According to franceinfo, 17 former Prime Ministers, as well as former interior ministers who left office more than ten years ago, lose the chauffeured car and security officers on that date, with only cases requiring close protection able to be maintained.[^franceinfo-fin-avantages-a-vie-pm-2026]

## Former Presidents of the Republic

Decree no. 2016-1302 of 4 October 2016 governs their material and human support. According to the Government's reply published in the Official Journal of 10 October 2023, it provides a cabinet of seven members and two service staff for five years after leaving office, then three members and one staff member, furnished and equipped premises whose rent and charges are covered, reception and travel costs, and a vehicle with drivers provided by the Interior Ministry as part of protection.[^an-question-9580-anciens-presidents-2023]

The same reply puts figures on 2022 spending for the two former presidents concerned: €196,981 for Nicolas Sarkozy and €221,074 for François Hollande (rent and taxes, costs linked to their former duties), excluding staff pay. Security spending, paid by the Interior Ministry, came to €1,303,050 and €1,210,286 the same year. The average of the two highest and two lowest salaries in their cabinets is €6,392 and €5,448 net.[^an-question-9580-anciens-presidents-2023]

A written question published on 21 July 2026 asks for this detail to be updated for 2023 to 2026 and whether a final conviction could reduce these resources.[^an-question-17117-anciens-presidents] At the verification date (11 November 2026), the question's page shows no reply: **the latest detailed figures read relate to 2022**.
