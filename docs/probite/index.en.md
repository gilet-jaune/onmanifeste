# Integrity and justice for elected officials: what the decisions say

Part of the distrust of institutions comes from the idea that politicians are judged differently from everyone else. This page does not settle that: it gathers **court decisions actually handed down** concerning elected officials, with their date, sentence and status (final or not), and explains why some sentences end earlier than planned.

!!! warning "Presumption of innocence"

    Only court decisions (judgments, rulings) appear here. An investigation, a formal charge or a prosecutor's requests are not convictions. A person remains presumed innocent until a conviction is final, that is, as long as an appeal is still possible.

!!! info "What this page is not"

    It is not a representative sample: we chose national figures whose decisions are documented by sources we read. It says nothing about how often elected officials are convicted, or who is convicted more or less. A mistake, something missing? [Report it]({{ repositories.docs.url }}/issues/new/choose).

!!! tip "For complete, up-to-date tracking"

    This page is not a substitute for dedicated tracking. For all elected officials and cases, with updated statuses, see {{ res("poligraph", "Poligraph") }}, {{ res("anticor", "Anticor") }} and {{ res("casier-politique", "Casier politique") }}. ## Sentences cut short: which mechanisms, which examples

A sentence can end before its term without being a favour or a pardon. These are the mechanisms these cases illustrate, as described by the sources.

- **Release before a final ruling.** Sentenced at first instance on 25 September 2025 with provisional enforcement, Nicolas Sarkozy is jailed, then released under judicial supervision on 10 November 2025 by the court of appeal, after twenty days in detention. The Club des Juristes recalls that, under Article 144 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, detention before a final judgment can be maintained only if it is the sole means of preventing evidence from disappearing, pressure, flight or reoffending.[^clubjuristes-sarkozy-liberte-2025]
- **Conditional release.** It is granted by the sentence enforcement court, with obligations. Patrick Balkany obtained it in 2022 (confirmed by the Paris Court of Appeal, without an electronic tag, after the prosecution appealed) then in January 2026, for the eighteen months remaining, in view of his age, health, low risk of reoffending and financial effort.[^lalibre-balkany-liberation-conditionnelle-2022][^clubjuristes-balkany-liberation-conditionnelle-2026]
- **Electronic tag.** The firm part of a sentence can be adjusted: one firm year under a tag for Nicolas Sarkozy in the wiretapping case,[^publicsenat-sarkozy-ecoutes-definitif-2024] two firm years adjusted under a tag handed down against Marine Le Pen in 2025.[^lcp-le-pen-premiere-instance-2025]
- **Sentence reduced on appeal.** François Fillon, sentenced to four years including one firm in 2022, receives four years suspended in 2025 after a third trial.[^france24-fillon-cassation-2024][^franceinfo-fillon-desistement-2026] Marine Le Pen goes from four years including two firm in 2025 to three years including one firm in 2026.[^lcp-le-pen-premiere-instance-2025][^lcp-le-pen-appel-2026]

!!! note "What we do not say"

    Nothing here allows us to say that these people were treated more or less favourably than other defendants: that would require comparable statistics on releases and adjustments, which we have not read.
